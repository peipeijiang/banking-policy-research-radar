<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

本文扩展了Goodwin（1967）的捕食者-猎物增长周期模型，将阿片类药物使用障碍（Opioid Use Disorder, OUD）对劳动生产率的负面影响纳入其中。利用1998—2019年美国州级数据，我们首先记录了劳动份额的恶化之后伴随药物诱导死亡率的上升。随后，我们通过一种新颖的离散选择机制将这一联系纳入模型，其中OUD的概率随工资份额的下降而上升，因此当实际工资未能跟上生产率增长时，阿片类药物使用增加。这一行为健康渠道通过生产率损害函数反馈到经济中。应用Andronov-Hopf分岔定理的存在性部分，我们证明了由此产生的三维非线性系统存在极限环，数值模拟也证实了这一点。沿着该周期，OUD的上升 paradoxically 提高了就业率，因为生产率下降需要每单位产出投入更多劳动；然而在不同稳态之间，较高的OUD流行率与较低的就业率相关联，这与阿片类药物使用障碍降低劳动力参与率的证据一致。此外，我们的数值实验表明，OUD对收入的敏感性会扩大波动，而生产率对OUD的敏感性则通过一种自我校正的反馈——加速工资份额的恢复——反直觉地压缩了波动。削弱收入与OUD之间联系的政策，例如更强的社会保障网，能够稳定由此产生的动态。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

This paper extends Goodwin's (1967) predator-prey growth-cycle model to incorporate the negative impact of Opioid Use Disorder (OUD) on labor productivity. Using U.S. state-level data for 1998--2019, we first document that deteriorations in the labor share are followed by rising drug-induced mortality. We then build this link into the model through a novel discrete-choice mechanism in which the probability of OUD decreases with the wage share, so when real wages fail to keep pace with productivity growth, opioid use rises. This behavioral health channel feeds back into the economy through a productivity damage function. Applying the existence part of the Andronov-Hopf bifurcation theorem, we show that the resulting three-dimensional nonlinear system admits a limit cycle, as confirmed by our numerical simulations. Along the cycle, rising OUD paradoxically raises the employment rate, as falling productivity requires more labor per unit of output; across steady states, however, higher OUD prevalence is associated with a lower employment rate, consistent with evidence that opioid use disorder reduces labor force participation. Moreover, our numerical experiments show that OUD sensitivity to income widens fluctuations, while productivity sensitivity to OUD counterintuitively compresses them through a self-correcting feedback that speeds the recovery of the wage share. Policies that weaken the link between income and OUD, such as stronger social safety nets, stabilize the resulting dynamics.

</details>
