# Delayed Retirement, Intergenerational Transfers, and Fertility

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

全球范围内人口老龄化与生育率下降同时发生，给劳动力供给和养老金体系带来压力。中国于2025年启动渐进式延迟退休（gradual delayed-retirement, GDR）改革，针对男性、女干部和女工人按不同时间表、分出生队列逐步提高法定退休年龄。为评估该改革对老年人个体及家庭内部的影响，我们构建了一个包含老年人退休决策、代际转移和成年子女生育决策的模型，并利用2025年前法定退休年龄处的模糊回归断点方法估计模型参数。反事实模拟表明，对于成熟改革队列，GDR改革增加了老年人就业及对成年子女的货币转移，但减少了祖辈育儿时间。由于育儿减少的效应超过货币转移增加的效应，生育率和老年人福利均下降，其中低收入女性群体的损失尤为显著。完全弹性退休、强制部分退休和强制全职工作在不产生类似的生育率和福利下降的情况下，均无法提高就业。育儿补贴可以抵消生育率和福利的下降，而将补贴资格限定于女性或低收入女性需要更高的补贴率，但可减少平均财政支出。这些发现表明，退休政策评估不仅应考虑其对老年人劳动供给的直接影响，还应考虑其通过代际转移产生的生育效应；育儿支持有助于缓解延迟退休的这些负面代际影响。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Population aging and fertility decline are occurring simultaneously worldwide, straining labor supply and pension systems. China began a gradual delayed-retirement (GDR) reform in 2025, raising statutory retirement ages on different schedules for men, female cadres, and female workers across birth cohorts. To evaluate its effects on older individuals and within the family, we develop a model of older adults' retirement, intergenerational transfers, and adult children's fertility decisions, with parameters estimated via fuzzy regression discontinuity at the pre-2025 statutory retirement ages. Counterfactual simulations show that, for mature reform cohorts, the GDR reform increases older employment and monetary transfers to adult children but reduces grandparental childcare. Because the reduction in childcare outweighs the increase in monetary transfers, fertility and older adults' welfare decline, with particularly large losses among lower-income women. Fully flexible retirement, mandatory partial retirement, and mandatory full-time work do not raise employment without similarly reducing fertility and welfare. Childcare subsidies can offset the declines in fertility and welfare, while restricting eligibility to women or lower-income women requires higher subsidy rates but reduces average fiscal expenditure. These findings suggest that evaluations of retirement policy should consider not only its direct effects on older adults' labor supply, but also its fertility effects through intergenerational transfers; childcare support can help mitigate these negative intergenerational effects of delayed retirement.
