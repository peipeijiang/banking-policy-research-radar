<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

研究活动会产生高度本地化的正向溢出效应，然而在美国，随着人口从主要研究机构所在的历史城市迁出，研究活动与人口及经济活动的空间错配日益加剧。将研究人员重新配置到人口中心地区，可能扩大当地获取知识的渠道，但如果历史机构或大型研究集群能够提升研究者的产出，这种重新配置也可能降低知识生产总量。因此，研究人员的空间再配置需要在知识获取的收益与知识生产的损失之间进行权衡。为量化重新配置所导致的知识生产损失，我们利用文献计量数据将个体效应与区位效应分离开来，并表明区位效应能够解释不同地区研究产出的显著差异。我们的工具变量估计提供了关于本机构集聚效应的稳健证据，但对外部集群效应的证据较弱。随后，我们将这些估计结果纳入反事实再配置分析，并表明将研究人员边际性地重新配置到研究活动相对较少的大型都市区，有可能提高总体产出，因为由此带来的知识生产损失较小，且可以被较小的知识获取收益所抵消。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Research activity generates highly localized positive spillovers, yet in the U.S. it has become increasingly spatially misaligned with population and economic activity as people moved away from legacy cities where major research institutions remain anchored. Reallocating researchers toward population centers could broaden local access to knowledge but may reduce knowledge production if legacy institutions or large research clusters raise researcher output. The spatial reallocation of researchers therefore entails a trade-off between gains in knowledge access and losses in knowledge production. To quantify the knowledge production loss from reallocation, we use bibliographic data to separate individual effects from location effects and show that location effects account for substantial differences in research output across locations. Our instrumental-variables estimates provide robust evidence of own-institution agglomeration effects but weaker evidence of external-cluster effects. We then incorporate these estimates into counterfactual reallocations and show that marginally reallocating researchers toward large metropolitan areas with relatively little research activity can plausibly raise aggregate output because the associated knowledge production losses are modest and can be offset by small access gains.
