<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

在近两个世纪的时间里，美国发明已变得越来越不相似：不仅发明者之间的正面交锋减少，相邻发明之间的距离也在不断扩大。我们利用经过验证的神经语言模型，对超过1100万件美国专利（1836–2023年）的权利要求全文进行分析，记录了这一相似性的长期下降趋势，并通过专利抵触率（一种衡量独立同时发明的指标）下降98%加以佐证。要正确衡量这一现象需要进行验证，因为同一专利文本的不同表示形式可能得出关于发明是趋于收敛还是不断分散的相反结论。我们的验证框架是针对专利文本的首次系统性比较，在思想空间中筛选出这些位置。该模型解释了发明的分散化，并将其与若干独立记录在案的模式联系起来——每位发明者研发投入的上升、专利价值的增加、知识溢出的减弱以及研究生产率的下降。其机制是空间性的；随着发明者分散开来以开拓新领域，发明变得更有价值，但他人吸收这些发明的成本也更高。通过这种方式，该模型将溢出强度、创新步长和研究生产率从固定的原始要素转变为发明者定位的结果。一项校准分解表明，美国研究生产率的长期下降中约有40%可归因于这些空间力量，此外还有“捕捞枯竭”和知识负担等传统解释。在思想空间中，发明者之间的相对位置对于增长的重要性，不亚于发明者数量的多少。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Over nearly two centuries, U.S. inventions have become increasingly dissimilar: not just fewer head-to-head collisions between inventors, but growing distance between neighboring inventions. We document this secular decline in similarity using validated neural language models applied to the full text of claims in over 11 million U.S. patents (1836–2023), corroborated by a 98 percent decline in patent interference rates, a measure of independent simultaneous invention. Measuring this correctly requires validation, since different representations of the same patent text can yield opposite conclusions about whether inventions are converging or spreading out. Our validation framework, the first systematic comparison for patent text, selects among these locations in idea space. The model explains spreading out and connects it to several independently documented patterns — rising R&D investment per inventor, increasing patent values, weakening knowledge spillovers, and declining research productivity. The mechanism is spatial; as inventors spread out to capture new territory, inventions become more valuable but also more costly for others to absorb. In doing so, the model turns spillover intensity, innovation step size, and research productivity from fixed primitives into outcomes of inventor positioning. A calibrated decomposition attributes roughly 40 percent of the long-run decline in U.S. research productivity to these spatial forces, alongside traditional explanations such as fishing out and the burden of knowledge. Where inventors stand relative to each other in idea space matters as much for growth as how many of them there are.

</details>
