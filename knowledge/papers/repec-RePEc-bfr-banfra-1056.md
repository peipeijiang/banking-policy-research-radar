<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

近期将制裁研究与威权主义研究相结合的进展，将制裁的有效性与政权类型联系起来。这些研究普遍得出结论，经济制裁往往对民主国家比对专制国家更有效，同时，对制度薄弱的专制国家比对其他威权类型更有效。这一处于国际关系与比较政治学交叉领域的文献，侧重于民主-专制二分法或威权类型的分类，而忽视了对“混合”政权类型的探讨。同样重要的是，它忽略了考察作为制裁子类别的援助暂停的影响。我们通过研究以下问题来弥补这些不足：（i）目标国的威权类型或民主开放程度是否能更好地预测制裁的有效性；（ii）混合政权在制裁下表现得更像封闭专制国家还是民主国家；（iii）援助暂停在有效性方面是否与其他制裁表现相似。这些假设在一个包含丰富援助制裁数据的原始数据集上进行了检验。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Recent advances combining sanctions scholarship and the study of authoritarianism relate sanctions effectiveness to regime type. These studies generally conclude that economic sanctions tend to be more effective on democracies than on autocracies, but also on weakly institutionalised autocracies rather than on other authoritarian types. This literature at the intersection between international relations and comparative politics focuses on the democracy-autocracy dichotomy, or the categorization of autocratic types, to the detriment of the exploration of ‘hybrid’ regime types. Equally importantly, it has neglected examining the impact of aid suspensions as a subcategory of sanctions. We address these deficits by investigating whether (i) the autocratic type or the level of democratic openness of the target better predicts sanctions effectiveness, (ii) whether hybrid regimes behave like closed autocracies or like democracies under sanctions, and (iii) whether aid suspensions perform similarly than other sanctions in terms of effectiveness. The hypotheses are tested on an original dataset rich in aid sanctions.
