# Informative Local Average Treatment Effects Using Propensity Scores

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25253v1)

## 一句话结论

> 论文提出信息性条件局部平均处理效应（ICLATE），通过固定二元工具变量两取值下的处理倾向得分来控制驱动依从性的不可观测因素，建立非参数识别、与边际处理效应的关系及可检验含义，并用交叉拟合机器学习与双稳健局部线性回归估计，以Job Corps项目评估培训收益效应随年龄的变化。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

在工具变量（instrumental variable, IV）模型中，跨协变量比较条件局部平均处理效应时，观察到的协变量上的效应异质性可能与遵从者总体（即那些处理状态会响应工具变量的个体）的变化相混淆。不同协变量取值下的遵从者可能在未被观察到的特征上存在差异，而这些特征也可能驱动效应异质性。我们提出了信息性条件局部平均处理效应（informative conditional local average treatment effects, ICLATEs），该效应以感兴趣的一部分协变量为条件，并以二元工具变量两种取值下的处理倾向得分为条件。固定这些得分可以使驱动处理选择和遵从性的不可观测因素的范围保持不变，而未被作为条件的协变量的构成则可能发生变化。在条件IV假设和适当的支撑条件下，我们建立了非参数识别，并刻画了ICLATE与边际处理效应之间的关系。我们区分了驱动处理选择的不可观测因素中的效应同质性与被排除协变量上的效应同质性。我们阐明了它们在将遵从者效应外推到总体效应中的作用，并表明二者联合起来即使仅有一个二元工具变量也能产生可检验的推论。在估计方面，我们将 nuisance 函数的交叉拟合机器学习与双重稳健得分的合并局部线性回归相结合。在欠平滑和正则条件下，我们建立了逐点渐近正态性，模拟研究对此提供了支持。作为实证示例，我们将该方法应用于Job Corps项目，以评估培训参与的 earnings 效应如何随年龄变化。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

In instrumental variable (IV) models, comparisons of conditional local average treatment effects across covariates can mix effect heterogeneity in observed covariates with changes in the complier population, that is, those whose treatment responds to the instrument. Compliers at different covariate values may differ in unobserved characteristics that may also drive effect heterogeneity. We propose informative conditional local average treatment effects (ICLATEs), which condition on a subset of covariates of interest and on the treatment propensity scores under both values of a binary instrument. Fixing these scores holds constant the range of unobservables that drive treatment selection and thus compliance, while the composition of the covariates not conditioned on may change. Under conditional IV assumptions and suitable support conditions, we establish nonparametric identification and characterize the relationship of the ICLATEs to marginal treatment effects. We distinguish effect homogeneity in the unobservables driving treatment selection from effect homogeneity across excluded covariates. We clarify their roles in extrapolating complier effects to population effects and show that, jointly, they yield a testable implication even with a single binary instrument. For estimation, we combine cross-fitted machine learning of the nuisance functions with pooled local linear regression of doubly robust scores. Under undersmoothing and regularity conditions, we establish pointwise asymptotic normality, which a simulation study supports. As an empirical illustration, we apply the method to the Job Corps program to assess how the earnings effects of training participation vary with age.
