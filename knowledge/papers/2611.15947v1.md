# Introducing and Repealing an EV Subsidy: Evidence from New Zealand's Clean Car Discount

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

新西兰的清洁汽车折扣（Clean Car Discount, CCD）是一项奖惩结合（feebate）计划，自2021年7月起对低排放车辆提供补贴，自2022年4月起对高排放车辆加收费用，并于2023年12月底被废除。此后不久，电动汽车（EV）被纳入道路使用费征收范围。我们利用2014年至2024年的月度车辆注册数据，研究电动汽车采用率如何响应该计划的引入及突然废除。基于Bai和Perron的结构断点分析，结合时间断点回归，显示纯电动、插电式混合动力和传统车辆的注册量断点与政策实施日期相吻合。为估计因果效应，我们采用双重差分方法，以澳大利亚昆士兰州为对照地区，并使用Newey-West标准误。由于新西兰市场规模约为昆士兰州的四倍，分析采用对数转换后的注册量，以使平行趋势指代相等的增长率。结果呈现不对称性。补贴引入后，新西兰的电动汽车注册量大幅上升，但其上升比例与昆士兰州相近，相对于昆士兰州的平均效应在统计上不显著。允许斜率变化的设定表明电动汽车采用率存在逐步加速。政策废除后，电动汽车注册量相对于昆士兰州下降了约72%，且该下降趋势在2025年持续存在。两个政策日期之前均出现了抢购潮。新注册车辆的平均CO2排放量在计划实施期间显著下降，并在计划结束后反弹。结果表明，突然终止电动汽车购买激励可能导致电动汽车采用率大幅且持久地下降，而逐步、预先公布的退出安排可能更为可取。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

New Zealand's Clean Car Discount (CCD) was a feebate scheme that offered rebates for low-emission vehicles from July 2021, added fees for high-emission vehicles from April 2022, and was abolished at the end of December 2023. Shortly afterwards, road user charges were introduced for electric vehicles (EVs). We study how EV adoption responded to the introduction and the abrupt repeal of the scheme, using monthly vehicle registration data from 2014 to 2024. A structural break analysis following Bai and Perron, combined with a regression discontinuity in time, shows that breaks in the registrations of battery electric, plug-in hybrid and conventional vehicles coincide with the policy dates. To estimate causal effects, we apply a difference-in-differences approach with Queensland, Australia, as a comparison region and Newey-West standard errors. Because the New Zealand market was about four times larger, the analysis uses log-transformed registrations, so that parallel trends refer to equal growth rates. The results are asymmetric. After the introduction of the rebates, EV registrations in New Zealand rose strongly, but by a similar proportion as in Queensland, and the average effect relative to Queensland is not statistically significant. A specification allowing for a change in slope suggests a gradual acceleration of EV uptake. After the repeal, EV registrations fell by about 72% relative to Queensland, and the decline persisted in 2025. Both policy dates were preceded by a rush of purchases. The average CO2 emissions of newly registered vehicles fell markedly during the scheme and rebounded after its end. The results suggest that an abrupt end of EV purchase incentives can cause a large and lasting drop in EV uptake, and that a gradual, pre-announced phase-out may be preferable.
