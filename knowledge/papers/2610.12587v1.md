<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

心理账户通常用自我控制来解释，两阶段预算则用可分离偏好来解释。我们提出基于卖方市场势力的第三种解释。承诺在某一类别中花费固定金额的消费者具有单位弹性需求，因此该类别中的卖方通过限制数量得不到任何好处。相反，以固定实物数量为目标的消费者具有完全无弹性需求，这是垄断者可以利用的情况。我们刻画了消除卖方加价激励的承诺，并表明固定金额预算是最不严格的。接着我们表明，重要的是预算刚性而非偏好的可分离性。价格变化时重新优化其类别预算的消费者，只有在各类别互为替代品时才能免受市场势力的影响；而刚性预算在任何情况下都能使其免受影响。因此，心理账户可以被解读为一种对抗加价的承诺装置，这一理据与现有文献中的自我控制动机是分开的。

</details>

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Mental accounting is usually explained by self-control, and two-stage budgeting by separable preferences. We propose a third explanation based on sellers' market power. A consumer who commits to spend a fixed number of dollars in a category has unit-elastic demand, so a seller in that category gains nothing by restricting quantity. A consumer who instead targets a fixed physical quantity has perfectly inelastic demand, a case that a monopolist can exploit. We characterize the commitments that remove a seller's incentive to mark up prices, and show that the fixed dollar budget is the least restrictive. We then show that what matters is budget rigidity rather than separability of preferences. A consumer who re-optimizes her category budget when prices change is insulated from market power only if categories are substitutes; a rigid budget insulates her in every case. Mental accounting can therefore be read as a commitment device against markups, a rationale that is separate from the self-control motive in the existing literature.

</details>
