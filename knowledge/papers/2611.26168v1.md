# Globally promoting legumes by production or use subsidies

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26168v1)

## 一句话结论

> 论文用开源CGE模型（CGEBox）模拟全球豆类生产补贴与消费补贴的效果，发现消费补贴更能降低豆类价格并提升家庭营养摄入，而生产补贴更能增加国内供给但会推高耕地占用并挤占牧场与森林，并以加纳和德国为重点案例。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

尽管人们普遍认同扩大豆类生产和消费对经济、环境和健康具有积极影响，但用于豆类种植的耕地比例仍然相对较小，与其他蛋白质来源（如肉类）相比，豆类在人类消费中的份额微乎其微。豆类生产和消费所涉及的外部性为政策干预提供了空间。在此背景下，我们使用CGEBox——一个开源且开放获取的可计算一般均衡（Computable General Equilibrium）建模平台——估算了全球性补贴对豆类生产和消费的影响，该平台整合了土地利用、土地覆盖和能源核算，并考虑了农业和非农业家庭。该分析提供了高度细化的农业食品数据，并特别关注加纳和德国这两个具有不同农业生态和社会经济条件、面临多重粮食系统问题的国家。与生产补贴相比，我们发现消费补贴在降低豆类消费者价格和刺激家庭宏量营养素摄入方面更为有效，且不会挤占其他食品的消费。相反，生产补贴在促进国内供给方面更为有效，但也会刺激豆类的饲料用途。此外，补贴推动了耕地扩张，损害了牧场和林地。这些补贴的影响还揭示了深刻的区域差异，其受到土地利用以及生产和消费模式的影响。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Despite consensus on the positive economic, environmental, and health effects of expanding legume production and consumption, the share of cropland devoted to them remains relatively small, and their share of human consumption is negligible compared with other protein sources such as meat. The presence of externalities associated with legume production and consumption gives room for policy interventions. In this context, we estimate the effects of global subsidies on legume production and consumption using CGEBox, an open-source and open-access Computable General Equilibrium modelling platform, with integrated accounting for land use, land cover, and energy, and considering agricultural and non-agricultural households. The analysis offers high agri-food detail and a special focus on Ghana and Germany, two countries with different agroecological and socioeconomic conditions facing multiple food system issues. Compared to production subsidies, we find that use subsidies are more effective at reducing consumer prices of legumes and stimulating household intake of macronutrients, without displacing consumption of other foods. In contrast, production subsidies are more effective at boosting domestic supply, but also stimulate their feed use. Furthermore, subsidies drive up crop land to the detriment of pasture land and forests. The impact of these subsidies also reveals profound regional disparities, influenced by patterns of land use and of production and consumption.

</details>
