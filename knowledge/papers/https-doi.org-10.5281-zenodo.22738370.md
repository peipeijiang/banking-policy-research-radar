PROJECT: THE GREAT BRIDGE proposes a sovereign digital-dollar settlement and refinancing architecture designed to address the structural cost of U.S. sovereign debt while preserving the Federal Reserve’s conventional monetary-policy transmission framework. The core mechanism is a Dual-Tranche Treasury Architecture. The conventional Sail Tranche remains subject to market pricing and traditional fixed-income dynamics, while a legally segregated Anchor Tranche provides designated Treasury funding for authorized Great Bridge digital-dollar infrastructure. Under the illustrative framework, the Anchor Tranche carries a policy-defined low Anchor Rate (0.55%), creating a structural funding differential relative to conventional market Treasury yields. The resulting fiscal spread is not designed as discretionary government revenue. Under the proposed Refinancing Mandate, realized fiscal savings are automatically directed into a ring-fenced Debt Destruction Vault for the systematic retirement of higher-cost legacy Treasury obligations. The Great Bridge differs from conventional stablecoin proposals in that it does not assume existing private stablecoin issuers simply adopt a lower-yield reserve model. Instead, it proposes a Sovereign Digital-Dollar Infrastructure Consortium integrating major payment interfaces, global payment networks, commercial banks, institutional capital managers, and related financial infrastructure. The objective is to establish GBC as a native settlement layer embedded within existing payment, banking, and capital-market interfaces. The architecture also introduces a shared-yield model. The illustrative 0.55% Anchor allocation is divided among a U.S. Inflation Shield Fund (0.25%), a Global Participation Dividend (0.05%), and consortium operations and compensation (0.25%). The Global Participation Dividend is intended for the entire GBC user network—including U.S. and international users—as a real-time transaction dividend and/or fee-offset mechanism that rewards active participation and reduces the effective cost of settlement. These internal allocation parameters are provisional and are intended to be independently recalibrated through detailed implementation, cost, risk, and infrastructure studies prior to adoption. Implementation follows a three-stage transition: Cold-Start Ignition, using temporary market-competitive incentives to overcome initial network effects; Transition & Utility Activation, in which payment, tax, securities, FX, and trade utilities progressively replace financial incentives; and a Permanent Stability Regime, in which network utility and scale support the long-term Anchor architecture. The Great Bridge therefore treats digital-dollar infrastructure not as a marginal reform of the existing stablecoin market, but as a potential sovereign refinancing, settlement, and shared-yield network. Its strategic proposition is that global demand for efficient digital-dollar liquidity can be converted into durable Treasury demand, lower sovereign funding costs, systematic legacy-debt retirement, broader distribution of network-generated economic value, and stronger structural influence of the U.S. dollar system. All numerical parameters in this document—including the 0.55% Anchor Rate, illustrative reserve-scale scenarios, and internal yield allocations—are design parameters and sensitivity assumptions rather than forecasts or final implementation determinations. Final rates, allocations, governance structures, eligibility rules, and operating requirements would require independent legal, economic, technical, and policy validation.
