<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

由于贫困人口的死亡率更高，他们在用于衡量贫困的样本中代表性不足。本文通过比较92个发展中国家和最不发达国家的观测贫困人口比率与反事实比率——即如果所有个体都享有最高收入阶层的生存条件时将会出现的比率——来量化这一“缺失贫困人口偏差”。利用各财富五分位组的收入分布和预期寿命，我们发现该偏差相当大且高度异质，从中亚部分地区的可忽略不计，到玻利维亚的15个百分点和不丹的36个百分点。汇总后，校正结果揭示出一个由超过1.16亿未被计入的贫困人口组成的“隐形大陆”，其规模足以颠覆国际贫困排名。以疟疾生态作为卫生系统覆盖率的工具变量，我们进一步表明，全民健康覆盖（universal health coverage）通过缩小收入-死亡率梯度，因果性地降低了该偏差及其陡峭程度。覆盖率每提高十个百分点，偏差降低约一个百分点，是OLS估计值的三倍。因此，迈向全民健康覆盖的进展产生了第二重红利，即校正了一种集中在剥夺最深之处的测量扭曲。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Because the poor face higher mortality, they are under-represented in the samples used to measure poverty. This paper quantifies this missing poor bias across 92 developing and least developed countries by comparing observed headcount poverty rates with counterfactual rates that would prevail if all individuals enjoyed the survival conditions of the top income class. Using income distributions and life expectancy by wealth quintile, we find the bias to be substantial and highly heterogeneous, from negligible in parts of Central Asia to 15 percentage points in Bolivia and 36 in Bhutan. Aggregated, the correction reveals an invisible continent of more than 116 million uncounted poor, large enough to reverse international poverty rankings. Instrumenting health system coverage with malaria ecology, we then show that universal health coverage causally reduces both the bias and the steepness of the income-mortality gradient through which it arises. A ten point gain in coverage lowers the bias by about one percentage point, three times the OLS estimate. Progress toward universal health coverage therefore yields a second dividend, correcting a measurement distortion concentrated where deprivation is deepest.
