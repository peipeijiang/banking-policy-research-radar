# Puzzling Behavior in Monetary Policy Transmission: New Evidence From Central Asian Economies

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

货币政策冲击的误识别通常会在实证研究中引发价格之谜和汇率之谜。向量自回归（VAR）文献中引入了多种识别方法，这些方法已被应用于解决不同国家的异常现象。本研究考察了在中亚经济体（CAEs）高度美元化和浅层金融市场环境下，金融部门处于国家主导地位时，这些识别方法在解决价格之谜和汇率之谜方面的效果。首先，使用Cholesky分解估计了一个具有短期约束的基准向量自回归（VAR）模型，结果在价格和汇率方面均观察到异常现象。随后，应用了具有最小约束的符号约束VAR模型，所得结果与货币政策传导的传统观点一致。此外，通过施加基于理论的零当期约束来识别货币政策冲击。结果表明，一旦根据当前经济理论修改识别方法，三个国家的汇率之谜便消失。同时，吉尔吉斯斯坦和塔吉克斯坦的价格之谜和汇率之谜得到完全解决，而哈萨克斯坦的价格之谜仅在单一货币政策体制下得到解决。因此，这些异常行为似乎是误识别的结果，而非中亚经济体（CAEs）的结构性特征所致。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

The misidentification of monetary policy shocks typically produces the price, and the exchange rate puzzles in empirical studies. Numerous identification approaches have been introduced in the VAR literature, and these approaches have been applied to address anomalies in various countries. This study examines how well these identification approaches have worked in resolving price and exchange rate puzzles under state dominance in the financial sector in a highly dollarized and shallow financial market environment of Central Asian Economies (CAEs). First, a baseline Vector Autoregression (VAR) model with short-run restrictions is estimated using Cholesky factorization, with anomalous results observed for both prices and exchange rates. A sign-restricted VAR model is then applied with minimum restrictions, producing results consistent with conventional views on monetary policy transmission. Moreover, monetary policy shock is identified imposing the theorybased zero contemporaneous restrictions. The results show that the exchange rate puzzles disappear in three countries once identifications were modified relying on current economic theories. Also, the price and exchange rate puzzles in Kyrgyzstan and Tajikistan were fully solved, but the price puzzle in Kazakhstan was only resolved for a single monetary policy regime. Hence, puzzling behaviors appear to be the result of misidentification rather than the structural characteristics of CAEs.
