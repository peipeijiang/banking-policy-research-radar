<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

我们研究一种基于属性的随机选择模型，其中对每个属性的注意力是有限的，并刻画了该模型内的两种选择规则。乘法注意力规则（Multiplicative Attention Rule, MAR）仅因某一备选项在其排名第一的属性上而给予其奖励，并将对这些属性的注意力在属性间以乘法方式组合；它捕捉了由于关注“过多”属性所带来的认知负荷所驱动的选择延迟。加法注意力规则（Additive Attention Rule, AAR）则改为在属性间对注意力取平均，并根据某一备选项在菜单中的完整序数排名来给予其得分。这种聚合方式上的差异正是两种规则在行为上的区分所在：AAR在相关属性跨菜单保持不变时仍能重现吸引效应（Attraction Effect），而MAR只有在属性集本身发生变化时才能做到这一点；不过，AAR反过来又无法容纳妥协效应（Compromise Effect）。这两种规则都由可观察选择数据上的公理来刻画，并且在两种情况下，潜在的注意力参数和基于属性的偏好都能从这些数据中被唯一识别。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

We study an attribute-based model of stochastic choice in which attention to each attribute is limited, and characterize two choice rules within it. The Multiplicative Attention Rule (MAR) rewards an alternative only for the attributes on which it ranks first, combining attention to these multiplicatively across attributes; it captures choice deferral driven by the cognitive load of attending to `too many' attributes. The Additive Attention Rule (AAR) instead averages attention across attributes and credits an alternative by its full ordinal rank within the menu. This difference in aggregation is what separates the two rules behaviourally: AAR reproduces the Attraction Effect while holding the relevant attributes fixed across menus, something MAR can do only when the attribute set itself changes, though AAR, in turn, cannot accommodate the Compromise Effect. Both rules are characterized by axioms on observable choice data, and in both cases the underlying attention parameters and attribute-based preferences are uniquely identified from that data.
