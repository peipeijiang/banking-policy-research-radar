<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

本文研究了离婚立法对已婚夫妇劳动供给行为及福利的动态影响。利用美国家庭面板数据，本文考察了从20世纪70年代的“所有权登记制度”（Title-based Regime, TBR）向20世纪80年代的“公平分配制度”（Equitable Distribution Regime, EDR）的转变。在前一制度下，婚姻资产根据正式所有权进行分割，允许配偶在事前就未来离婚时的资产分配做出承诺。而转向EDR后，法院以更公平的方式分割婚姻资产，这一转变通过两种方式与家庭不确定性相互作用：其一，由于法院决定资产分割方式，家庭无法再对未来资产分配做出承诺；其二，由于法院的裁决并非完全可预测，该政策引入了新的不确定性来源。基于这些原因，EDR增强了配偶双方通过工作积累预防性储蓄以实现自我保险的动机。采用交错双重差分设计，本文实证证实，已婚男性和女性均因政策变化而增加了劳动供给，其中女性劳动供给的增加量占过去70年女性劳动参与率总增长的30%。为合理解释实证证据并理解其福利含义，本文构建并估计了一个包含家庭劳动供给、储蓄及离婚的动态模型。利用该估计模型，本文表明，EDR可能在无意中降低了已婚状态下配偶双方的福利水平。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

This paper studies the dynamic effects of divorce legislation on the labor supply behavior and welfare of married couples. Using U.S. household panel data, I examine the transition from the "Title-based Regime" (TBR) in the 1970s to the "Equitable Distribution Regime" (EDR) in the 1980s. Under the former regime, marital assets are split based on formal ownership rights, allowing spouses to commit ex ante to a future asset allocation in the event of divorce. The shift to EDR, where courts more equitably divide marital assets, interacted in two ways with household uncertainty. First, because the court chooses the division of assets, households can no longer commit to a future asset allocation. Second, because the court's decision is not fully predictable, the policy introduces a new source of uncertainty. For these reasons, EDR increased the incentives for both spouses to self-insure by working to accumulate precautionary savings during marriage. Using a staggered difference-in-differences design, I confirm empirically that both married men and women increased labor supply in response to the policy change, with the women's increase equal to 30% of the total rise in female labor force participation over the last 70 years. To rationalize and understand the welfare implications of the empirical evidence, I develop and estimate a dynamic model of household labor supply, savings, and divorce. I use the estimated model to show that EDR may have inadvertently reduced the welfare of both spouses even while married.
