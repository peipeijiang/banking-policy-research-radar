# Optimal robust comonotonic risk sharing under operational constraints

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

诸如再保险计划和巨灾池之类的风险分担安排必须满足运营约束，包括容量限制、起赔带、自留规则和分出限额。在扭曲风险度量下，无约束最优解将每个损失层分配给该层成本最低的参与方，但运营约束打破了这一逐层规则，且目前没有通用方法来计算约束最优解。我们证明，对于共单调分配，各参与方的扭曲风险度量与运营约束对每个参与方所持有的各损失层数量均为线性关系，因此离散化损失上的约束风险分担问题是一个线性规划问题，标准软件即可求解。其对偶问题给出损失层的支持价格以及放松各运营约束的边际价值。在有效约束集合发生变化的位置，约束最优值可能出现拐点，此时最小和最大最优乘子分别给出放松和收紧某一约束的单侧效应。为使分配对损失模型之间的分歧具有稳健性，我们还考虑了风险分担问题的稳健版本，即在有限多个概率情景下最小化最坏情况的总风险评估。该稳健问题同样是一个线性规划，我们由其最优值量化针对模型不确定性的保护成本。我们以巨灾再保险分层结构为例说明了该方法。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Risk-sharing arrangements such as reinsurance programs and catastrophe pools must satisfy operational constraints, including capacity limits, attachment bands, retention rules and cession limits. Under distortion risk measures, the unconstrained optimum assigns each loss layer to the participant for whom that layer is least costly, but operational constraints break this layerwise rule, and there is no general method to compute the constrained optimum. We show that, for comonotonic allocations, the participants' distortion risk measures and the operational constraints are linear in the amount of each loss layer held by each participant, so the constrained risk-sharing problem on a discretized loss is a linear program that standard software can solve. Its dual gives supporting prices for the loss layers and the marginal value of relaxing each operational constraint. Where the set of binding constraints changes, the constrained optimal value can have a kink, and the smallest and largest optimal multipliers then give the one-sided effects of relaxing and tightening a constraint. To protect the allocation against disagreement between loss models, we also consider the robust version of the risk-sharing problem, that is, minimizing the worst-case total risk assessment over finitely many probability scenarios. This robust problem is again a linear program, and we quantify the cost of protection against model uncertainty from its optimal value. We illustrate the approach with a catastrophe reinsurance tower.
