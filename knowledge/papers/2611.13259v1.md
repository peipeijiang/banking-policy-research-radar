# Choosing Complexity: Cognitive Ability, Overconfidence, and Task Choice

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

员工和组织经常需要在成功与报酬权衡各异的复杂任务之间做出选择。我们从理论和实验两方面研究了认知能力和过度自信如何影响任务复杂度的选择。与我们的模型一致，我们发现认知能力较强的个体倾向于选择更复杂的任务。在控制认知能力的条件下，我们发现过度自信程度较高的个体同样倾向于选择更复杂的任务。在约75%的决策中，受试者选择了使其收益最大化的任务，且认知能力较高与更有效的任务复杂度选择之间存在弱相关关系。然而，错误呈现出显著的片面性：未能做出最优选择的受试者所选取的任务过于复杂。我们的研究结果表明，组织在设置任务菜单和激励措施以鼓励员工选择更复杂任务时，应同时提供信息和反馈，帮助员工评估自身能力与成功概率。这样既能保留自选择的益处，又能减少代价高昂的过度冒险行为。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Workers and organizations routinely choose between complex tasks that differ in their success-payment tradeoff. We study how cognitive ability and overconfidence shape this choice of task complexity, both theoretically and experimentally. Consistent with our model, we find that more cognitively able individuals choose more complex tasks. Conditional on cognitive ability, we find that more overconfident individuals also choose more complex tasks. In about 75% of the decisions, subjects pick their payoff-maximizing task, and higher cognitive ability is weakly associated with more efficient choices of task complexity. But errors are strikingly one-sided: those who fail to choose optimally pick tasks that are too complex. Our findings suggest that organizations should pair task menus and incentives for choosing more complex tasks with information and feedback that help workers assess their ability and probability of success. This preserves the benefits of self-selection while reducing costly overreach.
