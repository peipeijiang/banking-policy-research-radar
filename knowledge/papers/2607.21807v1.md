<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

部分识别在实践中常被搁置，因其产生的识别区域过宽而缺乏实用性，迫使研究者采用以可信度为代价换取点识别的强假设。我们证明，大多数区间值数据集中已存在的信息源无需添加任何假设即可解决这一问题。当结果仅以区间形式报告时——因数据保管者出于保护受访者目的而进行分组、顶部编码或正式私有化处理——同一保管者通常会继续发布该结果的准确总体汇总值，这正是因为此举不会危及任何个体记录。我们构建了一个框架以充分利用此类信息：将数据的可行补全集合限制为与已知汇总值一致的子集，而非限制区间本身，并刻画由此产生的最佳线性预测的尖锐识别区域。我们所研究的限制以截然不同的方式发挥作用——部分限制将区域压缩一个完整维度，另一些则在不改变其形状的前提下收窄区域。我们刻画了每种限制的几何效应，并推导出均值与条件均值情形下识别价值的闭式方向性度量。基于当前人口调查中区间值工资数据的实证表明，该效应绝非边际性的：适度的辅助信息即可恢复通常被认为在结果粗化后丧失的相当一部分识别力。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Partial identification is often set aside in practice because the identification regions it delivers are too wide to be useful, pushing researchers toward strong assumptions that buy point identification at the cost of credibility. We show that a source of information already sitting in most interval-valued datasets can fix this without adding any assumption at all. When an outcome is reported only as an interval---because a data custodian bracketed, top-coded, or formally privatized it to protect respondents---the same custodian typically continues to publish accurate population aggregates of that outcome, precisely because doing so does not compromise any individual record. We develop a framework for exploiting exactly this information: restricting the set of admissible completions of the data to those consistent with a known aggregate, rather than restricting the interval itself, and characterizing the sharp identification region that results for the best linear predictor. The restrictions we study behave in strikingly different ways---some collapse the region by a full dimension, others narrow it while leaving its shape intact. We characterize the geometric effect of each restriction and derive closed-form directional measures of identifying value for the mean and conditional-mean cases. An illustration using interval-valued wages from the Current Population Survey shows that the effect is far from marginal: modest auxiliary information recovers a substantial share of the identifying power usually thought to be lost once an outcome is coarsened.

</details>
