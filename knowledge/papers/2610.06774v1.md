<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

工会是地方层面重要的经济和政治参与者，但关于美国工会会员资格的主要数据来源——当前人口调查（Current Population Survey, CPS）——在州以下层面并不可靠。因此，研究者转向联邦“LM”申报文件中工会报告的会员数据，通过对工会进行地理定位并汇总其报告的会员人数，来估算县、通勤区和国会选区的工会密度。我们追问这种做法是否可靠。我们开发并捍卫以CPS为基础的多层回归后分层法（multilevel regression with poststratification, MrP）作为基准，然后从州一级向下逐级验证基于LM的估算。在州一级，LM、CPS和MrP高度相关，但这种相关性在国会选区、通勤区和县一级大约减半。这种失效部分源于报告中的“块状性”，它产生了不合理的数值，包括超过100%的工会密度。公共部门工会成员的缺失只能解释这种分歧的一小部分。这些问题是具有实质后果的：在一个标准的通勤区“中国冲击”回归中，替换密度测量指标会改变进口暴露系数，足以改变其大小、显著性和符号。我们得出结论：LM记录仍然是研究作为组织的工会的丰富资源，但汇总LM报告的会员数据并不是在精细地理分辨率下测量工会密度的可靠方法。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Unions are important economic and political actors at the local level, but the primary source on US union membership, the Current Population Survey (CPS), is unreliable below the state level. Researchers have therefore turned to union-reported membership in federal "LM" filings, geolocating unions and aggregating their reported membership to estimate union density for counties, commuting zones, and congressional districts. We ask whether this practice is sound. We develop and defend multilevel regression with poststratification (MrP) on the CPS as a benchmark, then validate LM-based estimates against it from the state level down. LM, CPS, and MrP are highly correlated at the state level, but this correlation is roughly halved at the congressional-district, commuting-zone, and county levels. The breakdown is driven in part by reporting "lumpiness," which produces improbable values, including union densities exceeding 100%. Missing public sector union members explains only a small part of this divergence. These issues are substantively consequential: in a standard commuting-zone "China shock" regression, swapping density measures changes the import-exposure coefficient enough to alter its magnitude, significance, and sign. We conclude that LM records remain a rich resource for studying unions as organizations, but aggregating LM-reported membership is not a reliable way to measure union density at fine geographic resolution.
