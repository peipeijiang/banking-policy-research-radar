<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

数字产业的生产率提升可能大规模重塑生产网络结构，并推动跨部门的显著劳动力再配置。我们使用一个校准的基于主体的全球经济模型来考察这些调整。我们考虑两种情景，其数字供应商生产率的累计增幅相同，但在暴露行业数字投入如何纳入生产方面存在差异。关键区别在于数字投入是作为标准中间投入进入生产（低嵌入度），还是直接与初级要素结合（高嵌入度）。在各经济体中，两种情景均带来总产出、家庭效用和GDP的增长，尽管增加值收益较为温和且在各行业间分布不均。高嵌入度通常放大生产响应，尤其是在高度暴露于数字投入的行业中。在结构上，随着数字产业成为生产网络的中心并从其他活动中吸引劳动力，其他行业进一步向上游和下游移动。持续的劳动力再配置压力也表明，在劳动者无法转移到扩张行业的情况下存在潜在失业风险。这些发现强调，除了数字技术进步带来的生产率收益之外，经济和社会后果取决于数字投入如何融入生产以及劳动者适应结构变化的可能性。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Productivity improvements in digital industries could massively reshape the structure of production networks and drive significant labour reallocation across sectors. We examine these adjustments using a calibrated agent-based model of the global economy. We consider two scenarios with the same cumulative increase in digital-supplier productivity, which differ in how digital inputs in exposed industries are incorporated into production. The key difference is whether digital inputs enter as standard intermediate inputs (low embeddedness) or are combined directly with primary factors (high embeddedness). Across economies, both scenarios generate gains in gross output, household utility, and GDP, although value-added gains are more modest and unevenly distributed across sectors. High embeddedness generally amplifies production responses, particularly in industries highly exposed to digital inputs. Structurally, as digital industries become central in the production network and attract labour from other activities, other industries move further upstream and downstream. Persistent labour reallocation pressures also indicate potential unemployment risks where workers cannot move to expanding industries. These findings stress that, beyond productivity gains derived from advances in digital technologies, economic and social consequences depend on how digital inputs integrate into production and on workers' possibilities to adapt to structural change.
