# The Source of Data Value: Consumer Disclosure, Access Design, and Platform Monetization

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22864v1)

## 一句话结论

> 构建委托平台、两个差异化卖家与隐私敏感消费者的博弈模型，分析强制收集与自愿披露下平台数据货币化动机，发现防御性数据购买可被平台利用，而匿名报价上限可保护消费者并限制福利损失。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

数字市场日益通过交易佣金和面向卖家的数据服务获取收入。卖家可能购买数据以增加收入，或避免与掌握信息的竞争对手竞争。我们研究了消费者披露权如何塑造这些动机以及平台提供数据的激励。我们构建了一个包含收取佣金的平台、两个差异化卖家和隐私敏感消费者的博弈论模型。数据通过双边销售-收入增益以及由不对称访问引发的竞争威胁产生价值。在强制收集下，平台可以将防御性采用货币化，即使双边个性化降低了卖家收入。在自愿披露下，匿名购买和严格正的隐私成本使得单边披露在基准情形下缺乏吸引力。因此，排他性访问不产生可用信息，恢复了卖家的无数据拒绝收益，并消除了防御性费用。因此，有利可图的数据提供要求非负的双边收入增益。在完全费用提取下，卖家保留其无数据净利润。这种收入对齐并不保证福利增益：额外交易可能使消费者受益，而在已覆盖市场中的市场分割可能提高匿名价格并降低福利。将匿名报价上限设定为其无数据基准可保护个体消费者，并将正质量的数据提供限制在交易创造区域。扩展分析考虑了访问选择、异质性隐私成本、不完全识别和有限合同可信度。平台应基于双边使用和卖家拒绝结果来评估数据服务，卖家应区分收入增强型投资和防御性投资。监管者应区分平等访问规则与有效的消费者控制，并监测匿名渠道中的定价。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Digital marketplaces increasingly earn revenue from transaction commissions and seller-facing data services. Sellers may purchase data to increase revenue or avoid competing with an informed rival. We examine how consumer disclosure rights shape these motives and the platform's incentive to provide data. We develop a game-theoretic model of a commissioned platform, two differentiated sellers, and privacy-sensitive consumers. Data generates value through bilateral sales-revenue gains and competition threats arising from asymmetric access. Under mandatory collection, the platform can monetize defensive adoption, even when bilateral personalization reduces seller revenue. Under voluntary disclosure, anonymous purchasing and strictly positive privacy costs make unilateral disclosure unattractive in the baseline. Exclusive access thus yields no usable information, restores sellers' no-data rejection payoffs, and eliminates defensive fees. Profitable provision therefore requires nonnegative bilateral revenue gains. Under full fee extraction, sellers retain their no-data net profits. This revenue alignment does not guarantee welfare gains: additional transactions may benefit consumers, whereas segmentation in an already covered market can raise anonymous prices and reduce welfare. Capping anonymous offers at their no-data benchmark protects individual consumers and limits positive-mass provision to the transaction-creation region. Extensions consider access choice, heterogeneous privacy costs, imperfect identification, and limited contractual credibility. Platforms should evaluate data services based on both bilateral-use and seller-rejection outcomes, and sellers should distinguish revenue-enhancing from defensive investments. Regulators should distinguish equal-access rules from effective consumer control and monitor pricing in anonymous channels.
