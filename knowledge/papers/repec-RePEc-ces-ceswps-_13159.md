# Climate Policy Without Commitment

> 论文构建一个企业先投资减排技术、监管者后设定减排政策的模型，证明总量管制与交易总能实现最优，而排放标准可能导致绿色和棕色多重均衡，投资补贴可消除棕色均衡但带来过度投资，并刻画了福利权衡条件。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

企业对减排技术的私人投资往往先于决定企业必须减排多少的政策。受这一观察的启发，我们研究了一个简单模型，其中监管者仅在企业投资于决定其减排成本的技术之后才制定减排政策。我们表明，尽管监管者无法承诺，总量管制与交易（cap-and-trade）始终能实现最优结果（first-best outcome）。然而，在排放标准这一实际中常见的政策下，企业的绿色投资成为策略互补：如果一些企业大量投资，减排变得更便宜，监管者会收紧标准，从而促使其他企业也增加投资。这种策略互动完全是由政策引发的。我们确定了这种反馈何时会产生均衡多重性，即“绿色”均衡与“棕色”均衡并行存在。政策制定者可以通过补贴投资来消除棕色均衡。投资补贴是可信的，因为它们在企业投资时才会被支付，从而规避了承诺问题。然而，消除棕色均衡必然导致相对于最优结果的过度投资。我们刻画了均衡选择带来的福利收益何时超过这一成本。这些结果具有重要意义，因为监管标准在气候政策中普遍存在，包括车辆排放标准、能效要求、燃料混合指令以及诸如淘汰内燃机之类的技术指令。

</details>

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Private investments in abatement technology often precede the policy that determines how much firms must abate. Motivated by this observation, we study a simple model in which the regulator sets abatement policy only after firms invest in a technology that determines their abatement costs. We show that, despite the regulator's inability to commit, cap-and-trade always implements the first-best outcome. Under a — practically common — policy of emissions standards, however, firms' green investments become strategic complements: if some firms invest heavily, abatement becomes cheaper and the regulator tightens the standard, inducing other firms to invest more as well. This strategic interaction is entirely policy-induced. We establish when this feedback generates equilibrium multiplicity, with a "green'' and a "brown'' equilibrium existing in parallel. The policymaker can eliminate the brown equilibrium by subsidizing investment. Investment subsidies are credible because they are paid if and when firms invest, thus circumventing the commitment problem. However, eliminating the brown equilibrium necessarily induces overinvestment relative to the first best. We characterize when the welfare gain from equilibrium selection outweighs this cost. These results are relevant because regulatory standards are pervasive in climate policy, including vehicle-emissions standards, energy-efficiency requirements, fuel-blending mandates, and technology mandates such as the phase-out of internal combustion engines.

</details>
