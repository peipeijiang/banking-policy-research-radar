# Navigating Shocks: The Ripple Effects of Shipping Route Closures

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

当海上咽喉要道受到干扰时，谁来承担成本？结合船舶级AIS轨迹、港口挂靠记录、运费指数、海关数据以及土耳其货物级数据，我们记录了2023年红海危机的贸易影响：受影响国家之间的海运贸易下降，绕行好望角，并在数月内随着运费飙升而恢复，同时部分出口商持续转向空运。我们构建了一个量化贸易模型，其中运费对运输方式和路线选择而言是内生的，受拥堵、全球航运能力约束以及运河当局垄断性收费的影响。该模型以观测到的路线选择和运河收入进行校准，结果表明，红海的永久关闭仅会使全球实际收入温和下降。由于苏伊士运河管理局以收入最大化为目标定价，已攫取了该路线的大部分剩余，损失主要落在收费者身上：埃及损失3.0%的实际收入，其中几乎全部为运河租金，而大型贸易经济体各自损失不到0.02%。相比之下，对霍尔木兹海峡——一个没有海上替代路线的咽喉要道——征收过境费，则会将损失集中于海湾经济体自身。一个咽喉要道是否存在替代路线，以及由谁来定价，共同决定了谁为其中断承担代价。

</details>

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Who bears the cost when a maritime chokepoint is disrupted? Combining ship-level AIS trajectories, port-call logs, freight indices, customs data and Turkish shipment-level data, we document the trade effects of the 2023 Red Sea crisis: seaborne trade between affected country pairs fell, rerouted around the Cape of Good Hope, and recovered within months as freight costs surged, while some exporters shifted persistently toward air freight. We build a quantitative trade model in which freight costs are endogenous to modal and route choice through congestion, a global shipping capacity constraint, and monopoly toll-setting by canal authorities. Calibrated to observed route choices and canal revenues, the model implies that a permanent Red Sea closure lowers global real income only mildly. Because the Suez Canal Authority, pricing to maximize revenue, already captures much of the route's surplus, the loss falls on the toll collector: Egypt forgoes 3.0 percent of real income, almost all of it canal rent, while the large trading economies each lose less than 0.02 percent. A transit fee on the Strait of Hormuz, a chokepoint without maritime substitutes, instead concentrates losses on the Gulf economies themselves. Whether a chokepoint has substitutes, and who prices it, jointly determine who pays for its disruption.

</details>
