<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

寻找托儿服务的父母常常面临漫长的等待名单。与此同时，托儿中心的名额却可能长期空置。我们将这一悖论归因于托儿服务的两个持久特征。第一，由于入园的不确定性，家庭会考虑多个托儿选项，导致出现“幽灵登记”——即使家庭已在别处找到托儿服务或不再需要名额，这些登记仍留在等待名单上。第二，填补一个名额耗时较长：托儿中心只有在发出录取通知并等待回复之后，才能得知一个家庭是否正在积极寻求入园。由于家庭在等待期间同时寻求其他替代方案，且其孩子不断长大，这些延迟和反馈效应放大了需求与可用容量之间的不匹配。

我们研究了两种运营补救措施。第一，我们考察定期核查机制，在发出录取通知之前清除幽灵登记，从而提高入园效率并减少空置。第二，我们研究混龄教室，即为较大儿童指定的一些名额可以由较小儿童灵活使用。这种灵活性提高了利用率，缩短了等待时间，并减少了浪费的录取通知。其益处超越了传统的 pooling（合并池化）：当儿童在年龄组之间流动时，教室可以通过内部流转而非昂贵的外部搜索来填满，进一步减少入园延迟。核查与混合使用相结合，为扩大幼儿教育教室的有效容量提供了一种途径，因为在监管和运营约束下，增加物理容量的成本尤为高昂。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Parents seeking childcare often face long waitlists. At the same time, vacancies at childcare centers can remain unfilled for extended periods. We trace this paradox to two persistent features of childcare services. First, families consider multiple child care options due to enrollment uncertainties, resulting in phantom listings that remain on waitlists even after families have found care elsewhere or no longer need a slot. Second, filling a slot is time-consuming: centers learn whether a family is actively seeking enrollment only after making an offer and waiting for a response. As families pursue alternatives while they wait and their children age, these delays and feedback effects amplify the mismatch between demand and available capacity.
  We study two operational remedies. First, we examine regular verification that removes phantom listings before offers are made, improving the efficiency of enrollment and reducing vacancies. Second, we study mixed-age classrooms, in which some slots designated for older children can be flexibly used by younger children. This flexibility increases utilization, shortens waits, and reduces wasted offers. Its benefits extend beyond conventional pooling: as children move between age groups, classrooms can be filled through internal flows rather than costly external search, further reducing enrollment delays. Together, verification and mixed use offer a way to expand effective capacity in early childhood classrooms, where regulatory and operational constraints make adding physical capacity particularly costly.
