<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

我们研究在面临Value-at-Risk（VaR）型约束下的最优集体投资问题，该约束考虑了未来监管要求的不确定性。该约束包含多个可能的财富阈值，并施加满足这些阈值的最低加权平均概率。我们刻画了最优终端财富，其结构将由监管阈值处财富恒定的区域与Merton型投资区域相结合。与仅含单一阈值的经典VaR约束不同，我们的模型构建了对多个阈值的保护机制。对于两个可能的阈值，我们识别了何时两者均受到保护，以及何时保护集中于较大阈值。数值结果表明，当要求的合规概率较高、即使较小的财富阈值也较高、或初始资本有限时，监管约束能够显著重塑最优收益结构，并相对于无约束投资产生较大的福利损失。在异质性投资者群体中，福利损失强烈依赖于风险厌恶程度的构成以及支配风险分担的Pareto权重。当高度风险厌恶的投资者在群体中占主导地位时，损失尤为显著。此外，出资较少的投资者可能承担总福利损失中不成比例的大份额，而出资较多的投资者所承担的份额可能低于其资本比例份额。因此，监管不确定性不仅降低总福利，还可能在投资者之间造成其成本分配方式的显著差异。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

We study optimal collective investment subject to a Value-at-Risk (VaR)-type constraint that accounts for uncertainty about future regulatory requirements. The constraint incorporates several possible wealth thresholds and imposes a minimum weighted average probability of meeting them. We characterize the optimal terminal wealth, which combines regions of constant wealth at regulatory thresholds with Merton-type investment regions. Unlike a classical VaR constraint with only one threshold, our formulation generates protection against several thresholds. For two possible thresholds, we identify when both are protected and when protection concentrates on the larger one. Numerical results show that the regulatory constraint can substantially reshape the optimal payoff and generate large welfare losses relative to unconstrained investment when the required compliance probability is high, even the smaller wealth threshold is high, or initial capital is limited. In heterogeneous investor pools, welfare losses depend strongly on the composition of the risk aversions and the Pareto weights governing risk sharing. Losses are particularly pronounced when highly risk-averse investors dominate the pool. Moreover, investors with smaller capital contributions can bear disproportionately large shares of the total welfare loss, while larger contributors may bear less than their capital-proportional share. Thus, regulatory uncertainty not only reduces aggregate welfare but can also create substantial disparities in how its costs are distributed among investors.
