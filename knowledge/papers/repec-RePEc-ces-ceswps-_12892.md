<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

本文研究了当观众获取变得可规模化而现场制作仍属劳动密集型时，数字发行如何改变非营利表演艺术组织的融资结构。该组织在盈亏平衡约束下选择质量、现场票价和数字获取方式，以最大化经质量调整的服务；随后，捐赠者在观察到赚取收入及每笔捐赠所支持使命后作出捐赠决策。模型得出三项结果。第一，现场制作工资上升会提高捐赠占比，但会减少捐赠总额、产出及使命产出。第二，在数字策略固定的情况下，更好的发行技术会降低捐赠占比，同时提高捐赠总额，因为赚取收入和观众覆盖面增加了每笔捐赠所支持的使命。第三，当货币化收益超过因获取范围收窄而造成的捐赠者价值损失时，该组织会将数字获取商业化。商业化门槛随鲍莫尔压力上升而提高，随数字获取更易货币化而下降。以内生质量、票价和商业化为对象的数值实验产生了开放、混合和商业三种模式。因此，即使捐赠占运营资源的比例下降，捐赠总额仍可能上升，而数字发行有助于为劳动密集型的现场核心提供资金支持。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

This paper studies how digital distribution changes the financing mix of a nonprofit performing-arts organization when audience access becomes scalable but live production remains labor intensive. The organization chooses quality, live ticket prices, and digital access to maximize quality-adjusted service subject to break-even; a donor then contributes after observing earned revenue and the mission supported by each donated dollar. The model yields three results. First, higher live-production wages raise the contribution share but reduce donations, production, and mission output. Second, for a fixed digital strategy, better distribution technology lowers the contribution share while raising donations because earned revenue and audience reach increase the mission supported by each donated dollar. Third, the organization commercializes digital access when the monetization gain exceeds the donor-valued loss from narrower access. The commercialization threshold rises with Baumol pressure and falls as digital access becomes easier to monetize. Numerical experiments with endogenous quality, ticket prices, and commercialization generate open, mixed, and commercial regimes. Donations can therefore rise even as their share of operating resources falls, while digital distribution helps finance the labor-intensive live core.

</details>
