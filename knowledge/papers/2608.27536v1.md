# Refundable Deposits: How to Restore Cooperation in Finitely Repeated Games

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

虽然无限重复博弈允许存在丰富的纳什均衡集合，有限重复博弈通常只有小得多且往往低效的均衡集合。我们展示了如何利用保证金来扩大这一集合：在每个时期，参与者可以向中立的中间人存入一笔可退还的款项，该款项在博弈结束时返还，若发生偏离则被没收。支付这些保证金是自愿的，并且在每个阶段都满足激励相容条件，因此不假设参与者具有任何承诺，唯一需要的承诺是中间人在博弈开始前固定退款规则。该机制在不改变基础博弈且不进行参与者之间转移支付的情况下，维持了比标准均衡更高效的结果组合。我们在囚徒困境、拥塞博弈和公共品博弈中进行了演示，这些情境在标准有限重复版本中均无法产生合作。我们还将该机制应用于动态公共池资源，表明这一构造可推广至重复阶段博弈之外的情形。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

While infinitely repeated games admit a rich set of Nash equilibria, finitely repeated games typically have a much smaller and often inefficient one. We show how to enlarge this set using deposits: in each period a player may place a refundable sum with a neutral intermediary, returned when the game ends and forfeited following a deviation. Paying these deposits is voluntary and incentive compatible at every stage, so no commitment by the players is assumed, the only commitment required being that of the intermediary to a refund rule fixed before play begins. The mechanism sustains payoff profiles more efficient than those of the standard equilibria, without altering the underlying game and without transfers between players. We demonstrate it on the prisoner's dilemma, a congestion game, and a public goods game, all settings where cooperation cannot emerge in the standard finitely repeated version. We also apply it to a dynamic common-pool resource, suggesting that the construction extends beyond repeated stage-games.
