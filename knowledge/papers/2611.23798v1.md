<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

本文构建了经济进步指数（Economic Progress Index, EPI），该综合指标基于地球观测（Earth Observation, EO）和开放地理空间数据，采用无监督主成分分析（Principal Component Analysis, PCA）赋权。该指数刻画了地方经济可观测的生产能力与赋能能力，以及经济空间的使用强度。发展中经济体的次国家级监测受制于数据稀疏：地区GDP估计存在多年滞后，依赖价格平减指数和自上而下的分配方法，且在地方性政策所针对的次地区（taluka/tehsil）尺度上并不存在。EPI融合了多传感器卫星数据、网格化人口数据以及OpenStreetMap道路与设施网络，覆盖印度马哈拉施特拉邦357个次地区、46个季度（2015年第一季度至2026年第二季度）。采用LOESS季节性-趋势分解（Seasonal-Trend decomposition using LOESS, STL）将长期趋势与季风及作物周期季节性分离。非负主成分分析给出的权重符号一致、可解释，其排序与标准PCA几乎完全相同（$ρ$ = 0.998）。在地区层面，该指数解释了NFHS-5财富指数变异的74%、家庭消费（HCES 2023-24）变异的52%，以及实际地区GDP水平变异的约78%（对数形式为65%），但并不追踪短期产出增长。由于该指数衡量的是结构性能力而非年度产出，其在2020-21财年COVID-19经济收缩期间仍持续上升。在马哈拉施特拉邦六个税收区中，相对不平等下降了44%，而领先区与落后区之间的绝对差距扩大了32%；地区层面的$β$收敛意味着半衰期约为14年。该框架为数据稀疏经济体的结构性监测提供了一种可扩展、无需平减指数的基础。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

This paper develops an Economic Progress Index (EPI), a composite measure built from Earth Observation (EO) and open geospatial data with unsupervised Principal Component Analysis (PCA) weighting. The index captures the observable productive and enabling capacity of local economies and the intensity with which economic space is used. Sub-national monitoring in developing economies is held back by sparse data: district GDP estimates arrive with multi-year lags, rely on price deflators and top-down allocation, and do not exist at the subdistrict (taluka/tehsil) scale where place-based policy is targeted. The EPI fuses multi-sensor satellite data, gridded population and OpenStreetMap road and amenity networks across 357 subdistricts of Maharashtra, India, over 46 quarters (2015 Q1 - 2026 Q2). Seasonal-Trend decomposition using LOESS (STL) separates long-run trends from monsoon and crop-cycle seasonality. A non-negative PCA gives sign-consistent, interpretable weights and rankings nearly identical to standard PCA ($ρ$ = 0.998). Across districts, the index explains 74% of the variation in the NFHS-5 wealth index, 52% in household consumption (HCES 2023-24) and about 78% in real district GDP levels (65% in logs), but does not track short-run output growth. Because it measures structural capacity rather than annual output, the index kept rising through the FY2020-21 COVID-19 contraction. Across Maharashtra's six revenue divisions, relative inequality fell by 44% even as the absolute gap between leading and lagging divisions widened by 32%; district-level $β$-convergence implies a half-life of about 14 years. The framework offers a scalable, deflator-free basis for structural monitoring in data-sparse economies.
