<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

我们使用基于主体的模型来研究一个抽象政府提高合作水平的政策。在背叛是理性选择的情境中，如果主体选择合作，他们将获得奖励。政府通过对重复囚徒困境博弈中主体的收益征税来建立预算，以支付奖励以及相关的行政成本。我们考虑了识别合作者的成本，以及减轻背叛行为后果的成本。政府干预并非万无一失，即合作者与背叛者只能以一定准确率被正确识别。因此，促使主体合作所需的临界奖励取决于识别准确率，也取决于当前合作水平。我们分析了政府支付临界奖励的成本约束，以及根据合作水平调整准确率的不同政策。我们发现完全合作变得不可能。相反，通过最优调整政策可以实现高水平的合作。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

We use an agent-based model for studying policies of an abstract government to increase the level of cooperation. In situations where defection is the rational choice, agents receive a bonus if they choose to cooperate. The government taxes agents' payoffs from a repeated prisoner's dilemma game to establish a budget that covers the payment of bonuses, but also the administrative costs involved. We consider costs to identify cooperators, and costs to mitigate the consequences of defective behavior. Governmental interventions are fallible, i.e. cooperators and defectors are correctly identified only with a certain accuracy. Hence, the critical bonus needed to let agents cooperate depends on the accuracy, but also on the current level of cooperation. We analyze the cost constraints for the government to pay the critical bonus and different policies to adjust the accuracy dependent on the level of cooperation. We find that perfect cooperation becomes impossible. Instead, high levels of cooperation can be achieved with optimal adjustment policies.

</details>
