<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

生成式人工智能揭示了一个更广泛的组织难题：资源受限的年轻企业可以依托更广泛领域中所产生的知识，来解决超出其内部经验的问题。我们将其中一种机制理论化为社会模仿：一个分布式且递归的过程，社区通过该过程观察各组织的实践与结果，将反复出现的要素提炼为可迁移的策略，使其流通传播，并随着采用产生新证据而修订集体策略库。这一视角将模仿的对象内生化。组织不仅仅是在复制实践；社区集体生产了可供复制的内容。
  我们将候选策略的来源（直接观察到的组织或集体）与其采用方式（盲目或知情）加以区分，并在一个基于主体的组织搜索模型中考察了四种模仿形式。仅凭集体来源并不能提升绩效。其价值取决于情境化评估：评估候选策略是否契合采用者的配置。随着相互依赖程度增加，知情社会模仿提升了群体绩效以及所发现的最优解。随着问题规模增大，它更有可能优于从一个或几个直接观察到的同伴那里进行的知情模仿，因为集体提炼维持了更广泛的候选策略库。大部分收益来自对一个小规模候选集的评估，而且当只有少数组织具备评估能力时，群体绩效仍可提升。因此，社会模仿揭示了一种认知分工：社区扩展了组织可以考虑的策略，而组织通过判断哪些策略契合来创造价值。它解释了企业如何能够调动超越其边界之外的知识，以及为什么对集体知识的广泛获取并不会产生均等的收益。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Generative artificial intelligence illustrates a broader organizational puzzle: young, resource-constrained firms can build on knowledge produced across a wider field to address problems that exceed their internal experience. We theorize one mechanism as social imitation: a distributed and recursive process through which communities observe practices and outcomes across organizations, distill recurrent elements into portable strategies, circulate them, and revise the collective repertoire as adoption produces new evidence. This perspective endogenizes the object of imitation. Organizations do not merely copy practices; communities collectively produce what becomes available to copy.
  We distinguish the source of a candidate (directly observed organizations or a collective) from its mode of adoption (blind or informed), and examine four forms of imitation in an agent-based model of organizational search. Collective sourcing alone does not improve performance. Its value depends on situated evaluation: assessing whether a candidate fits the adopter's configuration. As interdependence increases, informed social imitation improves population performance and the best solution discovered. As problems grow, it becomes more likely to outperform informed imitation from one or several directly observed peers because collective distillation sustains a broader candidate repertoire. Most gains arise from evaluating a small candidate set, and population performance can improve when only a minority of organizations possesses evaluative capability. Social imitation thus reveals a division of cognitive labor: communities expand the strategies organizations can consider, while organizations create value by determining which strategies fit. It explains how firms can mobilize knowledge beyond their boundaries, and why widespread access to collective knowledge does not produce equal benefits.
