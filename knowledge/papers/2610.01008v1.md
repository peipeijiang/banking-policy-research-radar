<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

机器学习能很好地预测结果，但预测准确性并不能确保可靠的反事实响应。我们以海运二氧化碳排放为例，研究如何将机器学习与理论相结合以进行测量和反事实分析，其中物理学提供了基准的速度响应。我们将干散货船和集装箱船的逐小时追踪数据与欧盟法规下报告的年度燃料消耗数据进行匹配，比较了工程计算、结构回归、混合模型和机器学习方法。在样本外，所有估计模型的预测值与报告总量之间的误差均在几个百分点以内，优于标准工程计算。然而，纯机器学习和无限制结构回归隐含了衰减的速度响应。混合模型通过从机器学习组件中排除与速度相关的输入，保留了结构组件的速度响应。对减速的成本效益分析说明了政策利害关系：衰减的速度响应可能使净收益的符号发生逆转。因此，准确的总体预测无法替代对决定反事实响应的约束条件的审视。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Machine learning predicts outcomes well, but predictive accuracy does not ensure reliable counterfactual responses. We examine how to combine machine learning and theory for measurement and counterfactual analysis, using maritime CO2 emissions where physics provides a benchmark speed response. Matching hourly tracking data for dry bulk and container ships to annual fuel consumption reported under EU regulations, we compare engineering calculations, structural regressions, hybrid models, and machine learning. Out of sample, all estimated models predict within a few percent of reported totals, outperforming standard engineering calculations. Yet pure machine learning and unrestricted structural regression imply attenuated speed responses. Hybrids preserve the structural component's speed response by excluding speed-related inputs from the machine learning component. A cost-benefit analysis of speed reductions illustrates the policy stakes: an attenuated speed response can flip the sign of net benefits. Accurate aggregate predictions therefore cannot substitute for scrutiny of the restrictions determining counterfactual responses.
