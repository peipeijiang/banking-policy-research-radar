# Do Customer Disclosures Affect Suppliers' Internal Capital Allocation Decisions?

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27598v1)

## 一句话结论

> 利用SFAS 131作为外生冲击，研究发现客户披露增加会通过竞争威胁渠道促使供应商企业将资本重新配置到增长机会较弱的部门，以维护客户关系。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

本研究探讨客户信息披露是否影响供应商企业跨业务部门的资本配置。客户信息披露可通过两条相互竞争的渠道影响供应商的投资决策：其一，信息渠道——改善供应商对下游需求的了解，帮助其将资本配置与增长机会相匹配；其二，竞争威胁渠道——削弱现有供应商的私人信息优势，诱使其进行高成本投资以维护客户关系。本文以SFAS 131的采纳作为客户层面信息披露的外生冲击。面临客户信息披露扩大的供应商，其产品市场竞争加剧，并将资本重新配置到增长机会信号相对较弱的部门。偏离增长信号所预测配置的供应商，更有可能在后续年份保持市场份额并扩大客户基础。通过一种将供应商部门与客户部门相连接的新方法，本文表明这种重新配置是由受影响部门产能投资驱动的，而非对先前投资不足的修正。与竞争威胁渠道一致，投资调整在关联更大客户的部门以及经营于更集中行业的部门中更为显著。进行这些投资的部门随后资产回报率（ROA）下降，这与供应商为维护客户关系而接受较低盈利能力的解释一致。总体而言，研究结果表明，信息披露能够影响经济关联企业内部的资本配置方式。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

This study examines whether customer disclosures affect how supplier firms allocate capital across business segments. Customer disclosures can shape supplier investment decisions through two competing channels. They can improve suppliers' information about downstream demand, helping suppliers align capital with growth opportunities ("information channel"), or erode incumbent suppliers' private information advantage, inducing costly investments to defend customer relationships ("competitive-threat channel"). I use the adoption of SFAS 131 as a customer-level disclosure shock. Suppliers exposed to expanded customer disclosures experience increased product-market competition and reallocate capital toward segments with relatively weak growth-opportunity signals. Suppliers that deviate from allocations predicted by growth signals are more likely to preserve market share and expand their customer base in subsequent years. Using a novel approach to link supplier segments to customer segments, I show that this reallocation is driven by investing in capacity in affected segments, rather than by a correction of prior under-investment. Consistent with the competitive-threat channel, the investment adjustment is stronger for segments linked to larger customers and for segments operating in more concentrated industries. Segments making these investments subsequently experience lower ROA, consistent with suppliers accepting lower profitability to defend customer relationships. Overall, the findings show that disclosures can shape how economically linked firms allocate capital internally.
