# The Market and the Jungle

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

我们研究界定产权和经济交易的不同制度如何塑造职业选择与福利。在我们的社会中，个体选择决定其生产与消费机会的职业，并预期要么是由公共执行支持的匿名、自愿市场交换（市场），要么是通过随机强制性等级制度（丛林）进行分配且没有货币补偿。我们建立了市场中和丛林中职业均衡存在的条件，并比较了它们的绩效。在共同的职业配置下，市场使总剩余最大化，而丛林中在货币持有固定时的配置是帕累托有效的，但不一定使剩余最大化。内生职业选择引入了制度差异的额外来源。我们发现，在总量意义上，市场职业均衡优于丛林职业均衡，需满足两个条件：(i) 丛林中的每一种职业在市场中都可得；(ii) 市场是完全竞争的，即价格不能通过单方面的职业变更而被操纵。我们还建立了在没有这种价格不变条件下的个体福利比较：在相同个体之间，每个人都弱偏好市场中的职业均衡而非丛林中的对称职业均衡。我们的分析将交换的制度基础置于福利比较的中心，并提供了一个研究与市场并存且持续存在的强制性制度的框架。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

We study how different institutions governing property rights and economic transactions shape occupational choices and welfare. Individuals in our society choose occupations that determine their production and consumption opportunities, anticipating either anonymous, voluntary market exchange supported by public enforcement (the market), or allocations through a random coercive hierarchy (the jungle) without monetary compensation. We establish conditions for occupational equilibria to exist in the market and in the jungle, and compare their performance. At a common occupational profile, the market maximizes aggregate surplus, whereas allocations in the jungle are Pareto efficient with monetary holdings fixed but need not maximize surplus. Endogenous occupational choices introduce an additional source of institutional differences. We find that a market occupational equilibrium outperforms a jungle occupational equilibrium, in aggregate terms, under two conditions: (i) every occupation in the jungle is available in the market, and (ii) the market is perfectly competitive, in the sense that prices cannot be manipulated by unilateral changes in occupations. We also establish an individual welfare comparison without this price-invariance condition: among identical individuals, everyone weakly prefers an occupational equilibrium in the market to a symmetric occupational equilibrium in the jungle. Our analysis places the institutional foundations of exchange at the center of the welfare comparison and provides a framework for studying coercive institutions that survive alongside markets.
