# Organizational Lifespan as Commitment: The Case of Foundations

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

一个组织可以通过对其自身存续期限的承诺来约束未来的决策者。本文研究慈善基金会中的这一选择。基金会可以承诺终止、承诺制度性永续，或将终止决定留给未来的自由裁量。自由裁量保留了状态依存的停止选择。而永续则提高了对基金会特定组织资本的投资，其回报在终止时会部分丧失。当未来治理机构从组织存续中获取私人利益时，终端承诺限制了过度延续。一个经典模型给出了每种制度均为最优的非空区域，而有限期界的扩展则使终止日期内生化。当组织资本相对于在不利状态下继续运营的成本具有足够高的生产率时，永续是最优的；当停止灵活性具有价值且治理激励一致时，自由裁量是最优的；当存续激励造成足够高昂的固守成本时，有限存续期是最优的。因此，组织存续期限是一个独特的承诺边际。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

An organization may bind future decision makers through a commitment over its own lifespan. This paper studies that choice for philanthropic foundations. A foundation may commit to terminate, commit to institutional continuity, or leave termination to future discretion. Discretion preserves state-contingent stopping. Perpetuity instead raises investment in foundation-specific organizational capital whose return is partly lost upon termination. A terminal commitment limits excessive continuation when future governing bodies obtain private benefits from organizational survival. A canonical model yields nonempty regions in which each regime is optimal, while a finite-horizon extension endogenizes the terminal date. Perpetuity is optimal when organizational capital is sufficiently productive relative to the cost of continuing in bad states; discretion is optimal when stopping flexibility is valuable and governance is aligned; a finite lifespan is optimal when survival incentives create sufficiently costly entrenchment. Organizational lifespan is therefore a distinct commitment margin.

</details>
