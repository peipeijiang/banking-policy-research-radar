<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

因果森林（Causal Forests，Athey and Imbens，2016）是估计异质性处理效应的一种广泛使用的方法。标准实现采用基于均值的分裂准则，当结果分布表现良好且不受污染（如离群值或重尾）时，该准则表现良好。然而，现实世界中的数据很少是干净的。结果扰动可能影响基于均值的分裂规则所选择的树结构，并导致不可靠的处理效应估计。在本研究中，我们系统地检验了因果森林对结果分布中不规则性的稳健性。基于Maßmann和Gliszczyńska-Schroeder（2026）提出的基于中位数的分裂准则，我们实证研究了三种基于中位数的分裂准则：中位数绝对偏差（Median Absolute Deviation，MAD）、最小中位数平方（Least Median of Squares，LMS）和中位数平方偏差（Median Squared Deviation，MSD）。我们进行了蒙特卡洛实验，并将这些方法应用于因果推断文献中的实证基准数据集，以比较基于中位数的方法与标准因果森林的性能。我们还使用Little Bags自助法和无穷小刀切法检验了点态置信区间的覆盖率。结果表明，基于中位数的分裂准则在特定场景下可以降低对结果不规则性的敏感性，特别是在存在极端重尾噪声和非线性处理效应异质性的情况下。然而，这些益处依赖于具体场景，并不能一致地导致更准确的条件平均处理效应（Conditional Average Treatment Effect，CATE）估计。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Causal forests (Athey and Imbens, 2016) are a widely used method for the estimation of heterogeneous treatment effects. Standard implementations use mean-based splitting criteria, which perform well when the outcome distribution is well-behaved and free of contamination, such as outliers or heavy tails. However, real-world data are rarely clean. Outcome perturbations can affect the tree structure selected by mean-based splitting rules and lead to unreliable treatment effect estimates. In this study, we systematically examine the robustness of causal forests to irregularities in the outcome distribution. Building on the median-based splitting criteria introduced by Maßmann and Gliszczyńska-Schroeder (2026), we empirically investigate three median-based splitting criteria: Median Absolute Deviation (MAD), Least Median of Squares (LMS), and Median Squared Deviation (MSD). We conduct Monte Carlo experiments and apply the methods to empirical benchmark datasets from the causal inference literature to compare the performance of the median-based approaches with standard causal forests. We additionally examine pointwise confidence interval coverage using the Bootstrap of Little Bags and the infinitesimal jackknife. The results indicate that median-based splitting criteria can reduce sensitivity to outcome irregularities in specific scenarios, particularly in the presence of extreme heavy-tailed noise and nonlinear treatment effect heterogeneity. However, these benefits are scenario-dependent and do not consistently lead to more accurate conditional average treatment effect (CATE) estimates.
