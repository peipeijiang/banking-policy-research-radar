<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

项目评估通常发现对低技能儿童的投资回报更大，而技能形成的结构模型通常发现技能与投资是互补品。我证明这些发现并不矛盾：它们测量的是不同的对象。贝克尔式回报曲线（BSR）是产出相对于某一投入的弹性，保持所有投入固定在特定值；平均边际回报（AMR）则将该弹性在投入的联合分布上取平均。重新审视Cunha、Heckman和Schennach（2010）基于NLSY79儿童样本的儿童技能形成模型，我发现技能与投资是互补品，但低技能儿童获得的投资少得多，以至于他们在幼儿期对投资的AMR更高——这一模式在较晚年龄发生逆转。该框架还提供了一种自然的方法来确定含交互项的对数线性模型中曲率所导致的偏差方向，这类模型混淆了BSR和AMR，且两者都未能准确估计；加入二次项既减少了这一偏差，又往往增强了估计的交互效应。在方法论上，我用有序概率测量系统对离散代理变量建模——将其视为连续变量会使AMR估计产生偏差，并无法检测到低技能儿童在幼儿期的AMR高于高技能儿童——并引入了有效因子，这是一种缩放系统，允许潜在因子在同一时期产生不同技能时采用不同的有效尺度。最后，在控制母亲永久收入的条件下，当前家庭收入对父母投资没有预测能力，这削弱了标准控制函数方法所依赖的外生变异，并提醒对我的估计应作描述性解读。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Program evaluations routinely find larger returns to investment in low-skill children, while structural models of skill formation typically find that skill and investment are complements. I show that these findings are not in tension: they measure different objects. The Beckerian schedule of returns (BSR) is the elasticity of output with respect to an input, holding all inputs at fixed values; average marginal returns (AMR) average that elasticity over the joint distribution of inputs. Revisiting Cunha, Heckman, and Schennach (2010)'s model of child skill formation on the Children of the NLSY79, I find that skill and investment are complements, but that low-skill children receive so much less investment that their AMR to investment is higher in early childhood --- a pattern that reverses at later ages. The framework also provides a natural way to sign bias from curvature in log-linear models with interaction terms, which conflate the BSR and AMR and accurately estimate neither; adding quadratic terms both reduces this bias and often strengthens estimated interaction effects. Methodologically, I model discrete proxies with an ordered probit measurement system --- treating them as continuous biases AMR estimates and fails to detect that low-skill children have higher AMR than high-skill children in early childhood --- and introduce effective factors, a scaling system that allows latent factors to take different effective scales when producing different skills in the same period. Finally, conditional on maternal permanent income, current household income has no predictive power for parental investment, undermining the exogenous variation on which the standard control-function approach relies and cautioning a descriptive interpretation of my estimates.
