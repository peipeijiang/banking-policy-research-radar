引言：我们提出一个框架，通过分解价值并检验异质性来评估医疗干预的经济价值。我们将其应用于英格兰人群筛查的早期卫生经济模型，该模型采用多癌种早期检测（MCED）测试。此类技术的价值通常包括与早期检测相关的获益，同时也涉及假阳性或过度诊断等潜在危害。价值在不同个体之间也存在差异，包括不同癌种和分期之间的差异。理解这些组成部分及其异质性对于评估总体价值和确定未来研究的优先顺序至关重要。方法：我们调整了一个现有的决策分析模型，以模拟无症状人群中每年一次的Galleri筛查，从英国国家医疗服务体系（NHS）视角（2024年）以质量调整生命年（QALYs）衡量结局。我们将“无成本上限价值”（假设检测无成本）分解为关键组成部分：癌症识别（诊断前和诊断后）、假阳性、过度诊断和错误分类。诊断后价值进一步按癌种分解以探索异质性。结果：我们的分析预测，每名个体的总体无成本上限价值估计为0.135个QALYs。早期癌症识别是主要贡献因素，主要由健康获益而非成本节省驱动。结肠/直肠癌、肺癌和卵巢癌是最大的贡献者，占总体价值的50%。这些结果在情景分析中保持稳健。结论：价值分解可通过阐明价值驱动因素、评估总体估计的合理性、探索异质性以及确定未来模型和证据开发活动的优先顺序，为决策提供指导。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Introduction: We present a framework to assess the economic value of healthcare interventions by disaggregating value and examining heterogeneity. We applied it to an early health-economic model of population screening in England with a multi-cancer early detection (MCED) test. Value for such technologies often includes benefits, such as those associated with earlier detection, alongside potential harms from, for example, false positives or overdiagnosis. Value also varies between individuals, including across cancer types and stages. Understanding these components and heterogeneity is crucial for assessing overall value and prioritising future research. Methods: We adapted an existing decision-analytic model to simulate annual Galleri screening in an asymptomatic population, measuring outcomes in Quality Adjusted Life Years (QALYs) from an English NHS perspective (year of 2024). We disaggregate headroom value (assuming no cost to the test) into key components: cancer identification (pre- and post-diagnosis), false positives, overdiagnosis, and misclassification. Post diagnosis value was further disaggregated by cancer type to explore heterogeneity. Results: Our analysis predicts an overall estimated headroom value of 0.135 QALYs per individual. Early cancer identification was a major contributor, driven by health gains rather than cost savings. Cancers of the colon/rectum, lung and ovary were the largest contributors, accounting for 50% of overall value. These results remained robust across scenario analyses. Conclusion: The value disaggregation can guide decision making by clarifying value drivers, assessing plausibility of overall estimates, exploring heterogeneity, and prioritising future model and evidence development activities.
