# Speculative Imbalance Positions by Non-Physical Traders: Impacts on Electricity System Balancing in Great Britain

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28075v1)

> 利用2021-2024年英国电力结算期主体级不平衡数据做反事实模拟，发现非物理交易者的投机性不平衡头寸降低了系统平衡量、平衡成本与供应商不平衡成本，从而可能降低消费者账单。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

英国（GB）电力系统允许参与者故意生产或消费与其交易头寸不同的电量，并自2015年起通过单一不平衡价格对这一行为予以激励。特别是，非物理交易商（non-physical traders, NPTs）经常在无意实际生产或消费电力的情况下持有敞口头寸以形成不平衡。然而，出于对供需平衡可行性和成本的担忧，新的政府提案可能会限制这一做法。本文首次对英国电力系统中NPTs的投机性不平衡头寸（speculative imbalance positioning, SIP）进行了实证分析，考察其在2021—2024年期间对不平衡电量、平衡成本、不平衡价格及相关结算成本的影响。本研究利用每个结算周期的主体级不平衡数据，对不含NPT总不平衡量的电力平衡机制进行反事实模拟。结果表明，在分析期间内，NPTs的SIP使所需的总净平衡电量减少了3.1—3.9 TWh（16—20%），使系统不平衡标准差降低了48—65 MWh（13—17%），使平衡成本减少了3.47—3.96亿英镑（5.9—6.7%），并使供应商不平衡成本减少了1.93—3.25亿英镑（65—75%）。这些结果表明，若无NPTs的SIP，消费者账单将会更高，因为额外成本很可能通过征费转嫁给消费者，尽管批发市场（主要是日内后期市场）可能存在的影响使这一图景更加复杂。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

The electricity system in Great Britain (GB) allows participants to deliberately produce or consume a different volume to their traded position and has incentivised this practice with a single imbalance price since 2015. In particular, non-physical traders (NPTs) often take an open position to imbalance without any intention to produce or consume power. However, new government proposals could restrict this practice due to concerns over supply-demand balancing feasibility and costs. This article presents the first empirical analysis of speculative imbalance positioning (SIP) by NPTs in the GB electricity system through its effects on imbalance volumes, balancing costs, the imbalance price, and associated settlement costs over the period 2021-2024. Our research uses party-level imbalance data in each settlement period to run counterfactual simulations of the electricity balancing mechanism without the aggregate NPT imbalance. Our results indicate that SIP by NPTs reduced the total net balancing volume required by 3.1-3.9 TWh (16-20%), reduced system imbalance standard deviation by 48-65 MWh (13-17%), reduced balancing costs by £347-396m (5.9-6.7%), and reduced supplier imbalance costs by £193-325m (65-75%) over the analysis period. These results suggest that consumer bills would have been higher without SIP by NPTs, since the extra costs would likely have been passed on to consumers through levies, although the potential for effects in the wholesale (primarily late-stage intraday) markets complicates this picture.
