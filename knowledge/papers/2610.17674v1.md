<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

人工智能（AI）能够将分散的信息综合为平衡且信息丰富的内容，这引发了AI可能有助于减少极化的可能性。我们表明，这一前景可能被内容生产者的策略性反应所削弱。我们构建了一个博弈论模型，其中消费者获取信息以做出下游决策，内容生产者在具有广泛价值的纵向信息与横向差异化信息之间分配有限的信息容量，而AI提供了另一种综合信息的来源。随着AI可靠性的提高，其影响在三个阶段中逐步展开。当可靠性较低时，AI对市场没有影响。在中等可靠性水平下，AI约束了价格，但并未吸引消费者或改变内容。当AI变得足够可靠时，消费者开始转向使用AI，内容生产者则通过将信息容量从纵向维度转向横向差异化内容来应对，从而加剧了内容极化。该机制通过受众构成发挥作用：AI吸引了与单个生产者匹配度较低的消费者，使生产者有更强的激励去迎合其剩余受众。极化效应随AI可靠性的提高而加剧，但随着内容生产者之间竞争的加剧而减弱。我们的发现凸显了AI的直接效应与均衡效应之间可能存在落差：一种为消费者提供更平衡信息的技术，可能间接使更广泛的内容生态系统更加极化。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Artificial intelligence (AI) can synthesize dispersed information into balanced and informative content, raising the possibility that AI may help reduce polarization. We show that this promise can be undermined by the strategic response of content producers. We develop a game-theoretic model in which consumers acquire information to make downstream decisions, content producers allocate limited informational capacity between broadly valuable vertical information and horizontally differentiated information, and AI provides an alternative source of synthesized information. The effects of AI unfold in three phases as its reliability increases. When reliability is low, AI has no effect on the market. At intermediate levels, AI disciplines prices without attracting consumers or changing content. When AI becomes sufficiently reliable, consumers begin to substitute toward it, and content producers respond by shifting informational capacity away from the vertical dimension and toward horizontally differentiated content, thereby increasing content polarization. The mechanism operates through audience composition: AI attracts consumers who are less well matched to individual producers, leaving producers with stronger incentives to cater to their remaining audiences. The polarization effect intensifies with AI reliability but weakens with greater competition among content producers. Our findings highlight a potential wedge between AI's direct and equilibrium effects: a technology that provides consumers with more balanced information can indirectly make the broader content ecosystem more polarized.
