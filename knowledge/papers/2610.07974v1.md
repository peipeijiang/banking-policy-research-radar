<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

中等收入陷阱通常通过将一个国家的收入与某一阈值进行比较来识别。这种方式揭示了趋同已经停滞，但并未揭示障碍所在，也无法解释为何在已经越过阈值的国家中，这一陷阱仍然存在争议。本研究将这一陷阱视为一个配置问题，从三个维度——未来潜力、外部潜力和内部潜力——衡量发展，并使用基于标准化指标构建的指数。这些指数针对34个OECD成员国在2000年、2010年和2020年的情况进行计算，从而使配置的变化变得可见，并通过因子分析对由此产生的国家聚类进行解释。研究揭示出两种陷阱和两种转型策略。第一种策略吸引外国投资和技术，但本身可能成为陷阱：国内附加值仍然有限，而工资上涨侵蚀出口竞争力。第二种策略则增加了一个专注于教育质量的发展型国家，构建能够提高生产率和创新能力的人力资本。到2010年，OECD的所有六个中东欧成员国都已采用第一种策略；到2020年，只有爱沙尼亚完成了第二种策略。配置水平及其变化与相对收入趋同相一致。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

The middle-income trap is usually identified by comparing a country's income to a threshold. This reveals that convergence has stalled but not where the blockage lies, and it leaves unexplained why the trap is still debated in countries that have already passed the threshold. This study treats the trap as a configuration problem, measuring development along three dimensions - future, outside, and inside potential - with indices built from standardised indicators. The indices are calculated for 34 OECD members in 2000, 2010, and 2020, so that changes in configuration become visible, and the resulting country clusters are interpreted through factor analysis. Two traps and two transition strategies emerge. The first strategy attracts foreign investment and technology, but can itself become a trap: domestic value added stays limited and rising wages erode export competitiveness. The second adds a developmental state focused on the quality of education, building the human capital that raises productivity and innovation. All six CEE members of the OECD had adopted the first strategy by 2010; by 2020 only Estonia had completed the second. Configuration levels and changes track relative income convergence.
