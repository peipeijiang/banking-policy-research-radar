# The Noise Is the Signal: Correlated Sampling Error Is Rank-Informative for Proxy Metric Selection

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08194v1)

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

诸如客户终身价值这样的北极星指标，往往变化太慢、噪声太大，难以用于决定一个短期的A/B测试。因此，团队依赖代理指标，通常是根据其效应在过去实验中与北极星指标效应的接近程度来选择。验证这一选择，或任何做出该选择的方法，都很困难：唯一的基准是有噪声的北极星指标，而且可用的历史实验数量有限。此外，代理指标效应和北极星指标效应是在同一批客户上估计的，因此它们的抽样误差是相关的。近期在大型实验平台上的研究将这种共享误差视为污染并加以移除，从而改进对真实效应协方差的估计。然而，选择代理指标是一个排序问题，而更好的估计未必会给出更好的排序。我们通过在每个实验客户的两个不相交随机半样本上估计这两种效应，来衡量不受共享误差影响的一致性。在一个包含262个实验和69个候选代理指标的档案中，共享误差对这些候选指标的排序与这种一致性排序相似（Spearman相关系数0.65）：它携带了关于代理指标质量的信息。某种校正移除的共享误差越多，排序反而越差，因为移除会丢弃一部分信号，却未触及排序噪声的主要来源，即有噪声的北极星指标和有限的实验数量。在已知正确排序的、经档案校准的模拟中，即使每次校正都获得真实的抽样协方差，这一点也得到了证实。在留出的真实实验中，由于是在不相交的客户半样本上评估，因此共享误差不会使比较产生偏差，结果 closely reproduce 了预测排序（Spearman相关系数0.93）。如果有更多实验，校正仍然可能带来收益，但所需实验数量会随着北极星指标噪声的增加而急剧上升。我们刻画了这一交叉点，并为平台团队提供了三种低成本检查方法，以便根据他们自己的档案判断是否应进行校正以及校正强度应有多大。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

North-star metrics such as customer lifetime value are often too slow and noisy to decide a short A/B test. Teams therefore rely on a proxy metric, commonly chosen by how closely its effects tracked the north star's across past experiments. Validating that choice, or any method for making it, is hard: the only benchmark is the noisy north star, and the number of available past experiments is limited. In addition, proxy and north-star effects are estimated on the same customers, so their sampling errors are correlated. Recent work at major experimentation platforms removes this shared error as contamination, improving estimates of the true-effect covariance. Choosing a proxy, however, is a ranking problem, and a better estimate need not give a better ranking. We measure agreement free of shared error by estimating the two effects on disjoint random halves of each experiment's customers. In an archive of 262 experiments and 69 candidate proxies, the shared error ranks the candidates in a similar order to this agreement (Spearman correlation 0.65): it carries information about proxy quality. The more of it a correction removes, the worse the ranking because removal discards part of the signal but leaves the main sources of ranking noise, the noisy north star and the limited number of experiments, untouched. Archive-calibrated simulations, in which the correct ranking is known, confirm this even when every correction receives the true sampling covariance. Held-out real experiments, evaluated on disjoint customer halves so that shared error cannot bias the comparison, closely reproduce the predicted ordering (Spearman correlation 0.93). Correction can still pay off with more experiments, but the number needed rises steeply with the north star's noise. We map this crossover and give platform teams three inexpensive checks for deciding from their own archive whether and how strongly to correct.
