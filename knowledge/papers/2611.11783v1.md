# Fairness Drift: Assessing Algorithmic Fairness in a Moving Market

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11783v1)

## 一句话结论

> 论文利用Cook County分阶段替换财产评估算法的准自然实验，用双重差分估计评估-价格比随售价和社区少数族裔比例的变化，发现算法公平性会随市场变动而漂移，单次部署评估可能误判算法功过。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

灵活的模型和更丰富的数据可以减少系统性估值误差并提高公平性。然而，算法的估值在两次更新之间保持不变，而市场却在变动，因此新算法在扭曲保持完整时收益最大，而在不均衡升值已侵蚀或刚刚逆转扭曲的地方收益最小。我们研究房产评估，它决定税单，且长期以来对较便宜房屋存在高估。库克县在其三年周期中以梯度提升模型和更丰富的数据取代了乡镇回归和人工调整：芝加哥在2021年，北郊在2022年，南郊在2023年。我们通过双重差分法估计评估值与价格之比如何随销售价格和社区少数族裔比例变化。在芝加哥，改革将较便宜房屋的相对高估减半，并在同等价格的房屋中削弱了与社区种族构成的联系。在郊区，价格梯度没有变化：北部的遗留估值已经准确，而南部的廉价社区在冻结估值下升值更快，纠正了部分扭曲，随后在重新评估年停滞，导致新估值在价格上涨的地方过高，准确性未获改善。因此，公平性在两次更新之间随市场漂移，单次部署评估可能将市场的作为归功或归咎于算法。

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Flexible models and richer data can reduce systematic valuation errors and improve fairness. Yet algorithmic values stay fixed between updates while the market moves, so a new algorithm gains most against an intact distortion and least where uneven appreciation has eroded or just reversed it. We study property assessment, which sets tax bills and has long over-assessed cheaper homes. Cook County replaced township regressions and manual adjustments with a gradient-boosted model and richer data on its triennial cycle: Chicago in 2021, northern suburbs in 2022, southern in 2023. We estimate difference-in-differences in how the assessment-to-price ratio varies with sale price and neighborhood minority share. In Chicago, the reform halved the relative over-assessment of cheaper homes and, among equally priced homes, weakened the link to neighborhood racial composition. In the suburbs the price gradient was unchanged: the North's legacy values were already accurate, and the South's cheap neighborhoods had appreciated faster under frozen values, correcting part of the distortion, then stalled in the reassessment year, leaving the new values too high where prices had gained and accuracy unimproved. Fairness thus drifts with the market between updates, and single-deployment evaluations may credit or blame the algorithm for what the market did.

</details>
