<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

本研究考察了1990年至2025年间尼日利亚原油价格（COP）冲击的宏观经济传导机制。研究描绘了这些冲击如何通过四个结构性渠道传播：增长、价格、外汇和投资。为克服单独单方程模型的局限性，我们采用Zellner的看似不相关回归（SUR）框架。该模型捕捉了五个因变量之间的同期相关性：实际国内生产总值（RGDP）、通货膨胀（INF）、汇率（EXR）、利率（INT）和失业率（UNE）。系统诊断在Breusch-Pagan LM检验的支持下，证实了使用SUR方法相较于普通最小二乘法（OLS）的效率增益。实证结果表明，全球原油价格飙升10%会使实际产出（RGDP）扩张1.24%，证实了正向增长渠道的存在。然而，这一意外收益也引发了成本推动型通胀压力，对总体通胀（INF）产生显著正向影响。外汇渠道显示，正向石油冲击会提振奈拉（-0.485），而价格下跌则导致货币贬值。在投资和劳动力渠道中，正向石油冲击会降低政策利率（-1.850）并降低失业率（-2.480）。相反，负向冲击则导致货币紧缩和失业率上升。这些发现表明，尽管石油意外收益支持短期增长，但它们也会助推结构性通胀和周期性失业。我们建议建立结构性逆周期财政缓冲、全面放开国内炼油能力，并针对降低石油驱动的通胀制定货币政策。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

This study investigates the macroeconomic transmission mechanisms of crude oil price (COP) shocks in Nigeria from 1990 to 2025. It maps how these shocks propagate through four structural channels: growth, price, foreign exchange, and investment. To address the limitations of separate single-equation models, we use Zellner’s Seemingly Unrelated Regression (SUR) framework. This model captures the contemporaneous correlation across five dependent variables: Real Gross Domestic Product (RGDP), Inflation (INF), Exchange Rate (EXR), Interest Rate (INT), and Unemployment (UNE). System diagnostics, supported by the Breusch-Pagan LM test, confirm the efficiency gains of using the SUR approach over Ordinary Least Squares (OLS). The empirical results show that a 10% surge in global crude prices expands real output (RGDP) by 1.24%, confirming a positive growth channel. However, this windfall also triggers cost-push inflationary pressures, with a significant positive impact on headline inflation (INF). The foreign exchange channel shows that positive oil shocks strengthen the Naira (-0.485), whereas price drops lead to currency depreciation. In the investment and labor channels, positive oil shocks reduce policy interest rates (-1.850) and lower unemployment (-2.480). Conversely, negative shocks lead to monetary tightening and higher unemployment. These findings show that while oil windfalls support short-term growth, they also feed structural inflation and cyclical unemployment. We recommend setting up structural counter-cyclical fiscal buffers, fully deregulating domestic refining capacity, and targeting monetary policy to reduce oil-driven inflation.
