<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

韩国家庭在补习班（hagwon，即为学生备战大学入学考试的私立补习学校）上花费巨大。本文探讨了在住房市场中获取此类补习班资源的价值，以及该溢价是否包含预期升值。研究利用2015年至2026年间首尔2，197，510笔公寓买卖和全租房（jeonse，一次性押金租赁）合同数据，通过新的营业地址地理编码器进行定位，在同一初中学区划片和同一月份内对公寓进行比较分析。靠近为高中生备战高考的补习班的公寓，其售价和租金更高：在500米范围内，从补习班中位数小区移动到第97百分位，对应售价高出11.8%、全租押金高出7.3%；若固定小区规模，则分别为5.6%和5.9%。一旦控制上述因素，面向低龄儿童的补习班不再带来售价溢价。跨小区比较中，押金溢价大于价格溢价，反映了便利性：购房者比租房者支付更少的溢价来居住在商店和服务设施附近，尤其是维修和个人服务。对于相同户型、小区和月份，补习班可达性对全租价格比（jeonse-to-price ratio）没有可检测到的影响，尽管检验能够检测到旧建筑的重建价值；附近商店则会提高该比率。因此，补习班可达性按比例资本化进入价格和押金，符合当前流量型便利设施的特征，不包含额外预期升值。新租户相比续租租户不支付持久溢价，押金也未对重大教育政策公告作出反应。住房溢价是教育竞赛的一项成本，而课外辅导支出的衡量指标并未将其纳入。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

South Korean families spend heavily on hagwons, private cram schools that prepare students for the university entrance exam. This paper asks what access to them is worth in housing and whether the premium includes expected appreciation. Using 2,197,510 Seoul apartment sales and jeonse (lump-sum deposit lease) contracts from 2015 to 2026, located with a new business-address geocoder, the analysis compares flats within the same middle-school assignment zone and month. Flats near hagwons preparing high-school students for the exam sell and lease for more: moving from the median complex to the 97th percentile of such hagwons within 500 meters is associated with sale prices 11.8% and jeonse deposits 7.3% higher, or 5.6% and 5.9% with complex size held fixed. Hagwons teaching younger children add no sale premium once these are held fixed. A larger premium in deposits than in prices across complexes reflects convenience: buyers pay less than renters to live near shops and services, above all repair and personal services. For the same flat type, complex and month, hagwon access produces no detectable change in the jeonse-to-price ratio, although the test detects the reconstruction value of old buildings; nearby shops raise it. Hagwon access is thus capitalized in proportion into prices and deposits, as expected of a current-flow amenity without extra expected appreciation. New tenants pay no lasting premium over renewing tenants, and deposits did not respond to major education-policy announcements. The housing premium is a cost of the education race that measures of tutoring spending omit.
