<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

在精算科学与定量金融中，凸序（convex order）为比较具有相同均值的风险提供了一种自然方式。在一维情形下，凸序通过若干刻画已得到充分理解。在更高维情形下，一种自然的方法是比较所有一维投影，但由此得到的条件虽然是必要的，一般而言却不足以保证多元凸序。Pinelis 给出的一个简单反例利用了 Popoviciu 不等式。我们使用规范函数（gauge functions）重新审视这一例子，并表明，对于对称分布而言，规范函数本身不足以刻画多元凸序，即使在二维情形下也是如此。随后我们表明，径向情形使这一失效的来源变得清晰，并在每个至少为二的维度中构造了径向反例。尽管如此，投影检验对某些重要的分布族而言是充分的。特别地，对于共同径向分布随机向量的线性变换，情况正是如此。这一框架包括由共同径向分布生成的椭球分布，尤其包括中心高斯分布。除了高斯分布之间的比较之外，Jourdain 和 Pagès 最近研究了高斯分布与高斯混合分布的比较，得到了必要条件与充分条件，而其等价性在一般情况下仍悬而未决。在本文中，我们解决了中心高斯分布与两个中心高斯分布的混合分布之间的比较问题：在每个有限维度中，高斯分布被该混合分布在多元凸序下支配，当且仅当所有一维投影均按凸序排列。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

In actuarial science and quantitative finance, convex order provides a natural way to compare risks with the same mean. In dimension one, convex order is well understood through several characterisations. In higher dimensions, a natural approach is to compare all one-dimensional projections, but, although necessary, the resulting condition is in general not sufficient for multivariate convex order. A simple counterexample due to Pinelis exploits Popoviciu's inequality. We revisit this example using gauge functions, and show that gauge functions themselves do not suffice to characterise multivariate convex order for symmetric distributions, even in dimension two. We then show that the radial case makes the source of the failure transparent and construct radial counterexamples in every dimension at least two. Nevertheless, projection tests are sufficient for some important families of distributions. In particular, this is the case for linear transformations of a common radially distributed random vector. This framework includes elliptical distributions generated from a common radial distribution and, in particular, centred Gaussian distributions. Beyond comparisons between Gaussian distributions, Jourdain and Pagès recently studied the comparison of a Gaussian distribution with a Gaussian mixture, obtaining necessary conditions and sufficient conditions whose equivalence was left open in general. In this paper, we settle the comparison of a centred Gaussian distribution with a mixture of two centred Gaussian distributions: in every finite dimension, the Gaussian is dominated by the mixture in multivariate convex order if and only if all one-dimensional projections are ordered in convex order.
