<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

一个在线市场决定消费者、企业和平台如何分担产品退货的成本。我们研究了当平台赚取佣金并出售醒目位置时的这一决策。消费者检查、试用并退回产品；企业竞标第一位的位置，然后设定价格。较早的展示吸引更多试用，但可能保留其中较小的比例。对于较小的正损失，将退货成本分配给消费者相对于以相同金额补偿企业而言，提高了醒目位置的价值。因此，广告将平台对给定退货账单的偏好分配转向消费者。我们刻画了较小正退货损失下的联合分配，并给出了平台不支付任何费用的成本条件。在退货频繁的市场中，平台反而可以与消费者分担账单：更高的佣金使其支付更多以鼓励购物和保留销售。有限损失下的结果表明，广告可以将最优政策从企业支付的免费退货变为消费者全额支付，从而降低佣金和消费者剩余。这种由广告引起的政策变化可以降低或提高总福利，取决于来自更少试用和退货的节省是否抵消了参与减少带来的损失。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

An online marketplace decides how consumers, firms, and the platform share the cost of product returns. We study this decision when the platform earns commissions and sells prominent placement. Consumers inspect, try, and return products; firms bid for the first position and then set prices. Earlier display attracts more trials but can retain a smaller fraction of them. For small positive losses, assigning return costs to consumers raises the value of prominence relative to reimbursing firms by the same amount. Advertising therefore shifts the platform's preferred division of a given return bill toward consumers. We characterize the joint allocation for small positive return losses and give a cost condition under which the platform pays nothing. In markets with frequent returns, the platform can instead share the bill with consumers: a higher commission makes it pay more to encourage shopping and retained sales. Results at finite losses show that advertising can change the optimal policy from firm-paid free returns to full consumer payment, reducing both commissions and consumer surplus. This advertising-induced policy change can lower or raise total welfare, depending on whether savings from fewer trials and returns offset the loss from reduced. participation.

</details>
