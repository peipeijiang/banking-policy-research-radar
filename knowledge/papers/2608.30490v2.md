<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

“我们发现没有证据表明X影响Y”这类表述在应用金融文献中屡见不鲜，然而，此类论断究竟属于“证据缺失”还是“无证据可证”，完全取决于其置信区间。“统计上不显著”这一术语通常被解读为零经济效应，但更诚实的描述应为：零值无法被拒绝，同时一系列其他系数效应量也无法被排除。关键问题在于，该区间内的效应量是否具有实际意义。本文区分了两种在标准回归表中难以分辨的不显著结果：有界零假设，即置信区间排除了具有实际意义的效应量，从而构成真正的发现；以及空洞零假设，即即使具有实际意义的效应量也未被排除，因而未能确立任何结论。我提出了一种适用于应用金融领域回归结果的最低报告标准，即明确陈述最小实际意义效应量（以决策单位为基准，对应自变量的指定增量），并附上描述性统计，同时将其与零假设置信区间的相关边界进行比较。仅利用已报告的系数和标准误，作者即可区分那些排除了实际意义效应量的有界（信息性）零假设，与那些因数据或识别策略不足而未能提供任何信息的空洞零假设。对称的“无效应”表述尤其掩盖了一种常见的分裂性结论：在一个方向上为有界，在另一个方向上则为空洞。在后续研究中，我将此框架应用于顶级金融期刊中已发表的零假设论断，首先从我自己的研究开始。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Claims of the form "we find no evidence that X affects Y" appear throughout the applied finance literature, yet whether such a claim contains evidence of absence or absence of evidence depends entirely on its confidence interval. The term "statistically insignificant" is routinely read to mean zero economic effect. However, a more honest description is that zero could not be rejected along with a range of other coefficient effect sizes. The crucial question is whether effect sizes in that range are consequential. This note distinguishes two kinds of insignificant results that are indistinguishable in a standard regression table: bounded null claims where the intervals reject effect sizes of consequence and thus represent a genuine finding, and vacuous null claims where even consequential effects remain unrejected and therefore establish nothing. I propose a minimal reporting standard for regression results in applied finance, where the smallest consequential effect size is stated (in the units of the decision, per a named increment of the regressor) alongside the descriptive statistics and compared with the relevant edges of the confidence intervals of null claims. Using only the reported coefficient and standard error, authors can distinguish bounded (informative) null claims that reject consequential effect sizes from vacuous null claims that establish no information, perhaps due to deficiencies in data or the identification strategy. The symmetric phrase "no effect" conceals, in particular, the frequent split verdict: bounded in one direction, vacuous in the other. In ongoing work, I apply this framework to published null claims in leading finance journals, beginning with my own.
