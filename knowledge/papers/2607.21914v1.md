<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

设 $p(x)$ 为变量 $X$ 的联合密度，并令 $ψ(x)=\nabla_x\log p(x)$ 为其得分场。仅由 $p$ 和 $ψ$ 构建的几何结构无法识别因果方向：具有相同观测分布的结构模型具有相同的得分几何。本文发展了一种干预类比。硬干预 $\operatorname{do}(X_k=ξ)$ 并非仅对联合律进行重新加权；它将分布限制在子流形 ${x_k=ξ}$ 上。因此，其得分应定义在剩余的 $d-1$ 个自由坐标上。我将因果影响 $X_k\rightsquigarrow X_j$ 定义为 $X_j$ 的干预边际分布随 $ξ$ 的变化，并证明边际干预得分的相应导数给出了影响的局部充分条件。将观测得分投影到可允许的干预方向上通常无法恢复因果响应：两个模型可能共享相同的观测得分和可允许集，但响应却不同。因此，我引入了一个由结构信息提供的干预响应场。因果度量被定义为针对具有共同目标的一系列干预的Fisher信息度量，从而避免了跨目标的不恰当比较。该框架为随机试验、工具变量和条件独立性设计提供了一种几何词典，阐明了每种方法能识别和不能识别的内容。一个二元高斯示例给出了两个具有相同观测得分但不同干预得分导数的模型。该框架梳理了设计、干预和得分场之间的关系，但在基本假设之外并未增加任何可识别性。在Pearl的因果阶梯中，观测得分几何属于关联层，干预索引得分场属于干预层，而单位级反事实几何则留待未来研究。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Let $p(x)$ be the joint density of variables $X$, and let $ψ(x)=\nabla_x\log p(x)$ be its score field. Geometry constructed from $p$ and $ψ$ alone cannot identify causal direction: structural models with the same observational distribution have the same score geometry. I develop an interventional analogue. A hard intervention $\operatorname{do}(X_k=ξ)$ does not merely reweight the joint law; it restricts the distribution to the submanifold ${x_k=ξ}$. Its score should therefore be defined on the remaining $d-1$ free coordinates. I define causal influence $X_k\rightsquigarrow X_j$ as variation of the interventional marginal distribution of $X_j$ with $ξ$, and show that the corresponding derivative of the marginal interventional score gives a local sufficient condition for influence. Projecting the observational score onto admissible intervention directions does not generally recover causal response: two models may share the same observational score and admissible set yet respond differently. I therefore introduce an interventional response field supplied by structural information. A causal metric is defined as the Fisher information metric on a family of interventions with a common target, avoiding ill-posed comparisons across targets. The framework yields a geometric dictionary for randomized trials, instrumental variables, and conditional-independence designs, clarifying what each does and does not identify. A bivariate Gaussian example gives two models with the same observational score but different interventional score derivatives. The framework organizes relations among designs, interventions, and score fields, but adds no identification beyond the underlying assumptions. In Pearl's Ladder of Causation, observational score geometry belongs to association, intervention-indexed score fields to intervention, and unit-level counterfactual geometry is left for future work.
