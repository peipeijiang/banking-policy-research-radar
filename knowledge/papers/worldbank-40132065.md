<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

乌兹别克斯坦正在推进一揽子全面的市场化改革，作为其加入世界贸易组织（World Trade Organization, WTO）战略的一部分，包括废除垄断、贸易自由化（取消一系列与出口相关的补贴和税收优惠）、消除公共采购中对国内生产者的优惠待遇，以及预期中的外国直接投资增长。本文采用可计算一般均衡—微观模拟相结合的综合框架，将世界银行MANAGE-WB模型与2024年乌兹别克斯坦家庭预算调查（Uzbekistan Household Budget Survey）的住户层面数据相结合，评估改革对宏观经济和分配的影响。结果表明，协调推进的改革方案产生了显著的效率收益，使2025年至2040年间年度国内生产总值增长率在5.8%的基准水平上提高约1.0个百分点，若辅以外国直接投资增长，还可额外提高0.4个百分点。消费者价格累计下降幅度最高达19.5%，福利改善主要通过增强购买力而非名义工资增长来实现。废除垄断成为最主要的宏观经济驱动力，而贸易自由化支撑长期生产率增长，取消采购优惠则发挥补充作用。这些收益在性别、行业和地区等群体之间分布不均，性别工资差距基本保持不变。研究结果凸显了竞争政策、贸易自由化和外国直接投资促进之间互补性的重要性，同时也表明需要有针对性的人力资本、劳动力流动和社会保护政策，以确保改革转化为广泛包容的成果。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Uzbekistan is advancing a comprehensive package of market-oriented reforms as part of its World Trade Organization accession strategy, including the abolition of monopolies, trade liberalization (removal of a range of export-related subsidies and tax incentives), the elimination of domestic producer preferences in public procurement, and an anticipated rise in foreign direct investment. This paper evaluates the macroeconomic and distributional impacts of reforms using an integrated computable general equilibrium–microsimulation framework that combines the World Bank’s MANAGE-WB model with household-level data from the 2024 Uzbekistan Household Budget Survey. The results show that the coordinated reform package generates substantial efficiency gains, raising annual gross domestic product growth by approximately 1.0 percentage point above a 5.8 percent baseline between 2025 and 2040, with an additional 0.4 percentage point gain when complemented by a foreign direct investment boost. Consumer prices fall cumulatively by up to 19.5 percent, and welfare improvements are driven primarily through enhanced purchasing power rather than nominal wage increases. The abolition of monopolies emerges as the dominant macroeconomic driver, while trade liberalization sustains long-term productivity growth and the removal of procurement preferences plays a complementary role. These gains are uneven across groups including gender, sectors, and location, and gender wage gaps remain largely unchanged. The findings underscore the importance of complementarities between competition policy, trade liberalization, and foreign direct investment promotion, as well as the need for targeted human capital, labor mobility, and social protection policies to ensure that reforms translate into broadly inclusive outcomes.
