<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

2021-2022年能源危机后，北欧竞价区的日前价格不再同步变动。我们探究这种分化是否也改变了价格所承载的信息。这一问题的答案对整合可变可再生能源（VRE）具有重要意义，因为它有助于识别哪些地区的风能和太阳能预测值得生产、发布和使用。我们测量了2018-2025年间挪威和瑞典各竞价区与德国-卢森堡（DE-LU）区的联动性。随后，我们使用基于2024年数据选定的单一模型，对2025年前一日11：00的价格进行预测，并检验哪些输入变量提高了各区的预测精度。2019年之前各区价格走势一致，但瑞典在2020年、挪威在2021年出现联动性分化，且这一分化持续存在。2024年，四个南部竞价区与DE-LU呈强联动（日内相关系数为0.54-0.71），而四个北部竞价区呈弱联动（0.25-0.30）。然而，价格信息并未随这一分化而改变。前一日邻近区域价格在任何竞价区最多将平均绝对误差降低2.2%，在耦合区和孤立区效果相当。移除输电系统运营商（TSO）的风能和太阳能预测后，挪威的平均绝对误差最多增加6%，瑞典增加8-16%，DE-LU增加31%，且该排序在2024年保持不变。在挪威境内，这些预测的价值未表现出对水库蓄水率的可检测依赖性。因此，联动性的分化并未伴随价格信息的分化，后者主要因国家发电系统不同而存在差异。由此可见，某一竞价区与欧洲大陆的耦合程度并不能指示该区域VRE预测的价值。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

After the 2021-2022 energy crisis, day-ahead prices in Nordic bidding zones no longer move together. We ask whether this divergence also changes the information carried by prices. The answer matters for integrating variable renewable energy (VRE), because it can help identify where wind and solar forecasts are worth producing, publishing, and using. We measured the co-movement of each Norwegian and Swedish zone with Germany-Luxembourg (DE-LU) in 2018-2025. We then used a single model, selected on 2024 data, to forecast 2025 prices at 11:00 on the previous day and tested which inputs improved accuracy in each zone. The zones moved alike until 2019, but co-movement diverged in Sweden in 2020 and in Norway in 2021, and the split has persisted. In 2024, four southern zones co-moved strongly with DE-LU (intraday correlations of 0.54-0.71), while four northern zones co-moved weakly (0.25-0.30). Yet price information did not follow this split. Previous-day neighbouring prices reduced mean absolute error by at most 2.2% in any zone, equally in coupled and isolated zones. Removing the transmission system operators' wind and solar forecasts increased mean absolute error by at most 6% in Norway, 8-16% in Sweden, and 31% in DE-LU, and the ordering held in 2024. Within Norway, the value of these forecasts showed no detectable dependence on reservoir filling. The divergence in co-movement was therefore not matched by a divergence in price information, which differed mainly between national generation systems. Thus, a zone's coupling to the continent does not indicate the value of VRE forecasts there.
