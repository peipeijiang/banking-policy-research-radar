# Experimentally Validated Digital Twins: Evaluating Workforce Training Programs in the Age of Big Data

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

现代项目评估优先采用随机对照试验（randomized controlled trials, RCTs）的证据，因为此前的研究发现，项目影响的观察性估计往往与实验基准存在显著偏差。我们在大规模行政数据时代重新审视这一基于小型调查数据集的发现。利用覆盖1999年至2025年间所有美国成年人的匿名所得税记录，我们通过机器学习模型，基于既往工作经历、人口统计学特征和居住地等丰富数据预测反事实，从而估计劳动力培训项目对收入的影响。我们并非检验观察性估计是否无偏，而是利用37项RCT来估计其偏差的分布。我们发现，我们的机器学习估计与RCT估计高度吻合，其均方根误差（root mean squared error, RMSE）与RCT本身的误差相当。相较于此前研究中使用的参数估计方法，我们的方法将RMSE降低了60%，因为更大的样本量和更丰富的协变量允许我们构建更灵活、更高维的反事实模型。将我们的方法应用于实验样本之外的更广泛站点和入学年份，我们发现，即使在同一个项目内，处理对收入的影响在不同地点和时间上也存在显著差异。将更多参与者安置到目标行业岗位的项目产生了最大的收入增益，且这些影响即使在参与者离开初始工作后仍然持续。这些发现为改进职业培训项目提供了指导，更广泛地说，展示了一种可扩展的项目评估方法，即利用观察性数据构建经实验验证的“数字孪生”（digital twins）。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Modern program evaluation prioritizes evidence from randomized controlled trials (RCTs) because prior research found that observational estimates of program impacts often diverge substantially from experimental benchmarks. We revisit this finding --- which was based on small survey datasets --- in the era of large-scale administrative data. Using anonymized income tax records covering all American adults between 1999 and 2025, we estimate the effects of workforce training programs on earnings by using machine learning models to predict counterfactuals from rich data on prior work histories, demographics, and residential locations. Rather than testing whether our observational estimates are unbiased, we use 37 RCTs to estimate the distribution of their bias. We find that our machine learning estimates closely match the RCT estimates, with root mean squared error (RMSE) comparable to that of the RCTs themselves. Our method reduces RMSE by 60% relative to parametric estimators used in prior work because our larger samples and richer covariates permit more flexible, higher-dimensional models of the counterfactual. Applying our method to a broader set of sites and enrollment years beyond the experimental sample, we find that treatment effects on earnings vary substantially across locations and over time, even within a given program. Programs that place more participants into target-sector jobs generate the largest earnings gains, with impacts that persist even after participants leave their initial jobs. These findings provide guidance for improving job training programs and, more broadly, illustrate a scalable approach to program evaluation that uses observational data to construct experimentally validated ``digital twins.''
