<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

当卖方选择一组产品质量和价格菜单时，消费者应当如何学习？我们研究信息设计问题，其中卖方在观察学习政策之后、但在选择其菜单之前，并不观察消费者的私人信号。在成本曲率条件下，我们刻画了当消费者剩余比利润获得更大权重时的最优学习。即使在只有两种估值的情况下，每个最优后验分布都将一个后验类型区间与一个位于最高估值处并被有效服务的原子相结合。所诱导出的菜单具有一个连续统的差异化产品。引人注目的是，最优学习还可以诱导出一个较低的后验原子，并通过一个支撑间隙与该区间分离。这个原子中的买家要么购买一个孤立的低质量产品，要么被排除在外，即使与每个买家交易都会产生收益。对于相同的先验和福利权重，这两种配置都可能是最优的。具有不同后验类型的被服务买家从不会在同一质量上被归并。一般有限先验可以产生若干个分离区域和交易原子。我们还刻画了学习与基于估值的分割的联合最优设计，并表明，除其他结果外，每一个从交易中获得正收益的后验类型都必须被服务。与仅面向买家最优的私人学习相比，联合设计可以提高消费者剩余和利润。即使是将消费者随机分配到具有不同学习政策和独立菜单的细分市场中，也可能比任何产生相同利润的单一市场给消费者带来更多剩余。因此，额外的价格歧视可以使消费者受益，即使隐私限制要求分割不能向卖方揭示任何关于估值的信息。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

How should consumers learn when a seller chooses a menu of product qualities and prices? We study information design when the seller observes the learning policy, but not consumers' private signals, before choosing its menu. Under cost-curvature conditions, we characterize optimal learning when consumer surplus receives greater weight than profit. Even with two valuations, every optimal posterior distribution combines an interval of posterior types with an efficiently served atom at the highest valuation. The induced menu features a continuum of differentiated products. Strikingly, optimal learning can also induce a lower posterior atom, separated from this interval by a support gap. Buyers in this atom either purchase an isolated low-quality product or are excluded, even when trade with every buyer creates gains. Both configurations can be optimal for the same prior and welfare weight. Served buyers with distinct posterior types are never bunched at the same quality. General finite priors can yield several separating regions and trading atoms. We also characterize the optimal joint design of learning and valuation-dependent segmentation, showing, among other results, that every posterior type with positive gains from trade must be served. Joint design can raise both consumer surplus and profit relative to buyer-optimal private learning alone. Even random assignment to segments with different learning policies and separate menus can give consumers more surplus than any single market yielding the same profit. Additional price discrimination can therefore benefit consumers, even when privacy restrictions require segmentation to reveal nothing about valuations to the seller.
