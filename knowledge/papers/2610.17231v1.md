<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

我们研究知名投资者的股票披露是否会引发异常市场反应，且这种反应超出与之相当但不知名管理者所产生的反应。我们将“巴菲特效应”定义为：因披露投资者获得公众知名度而带来的、对已披露新建仓位的增量异常收益。我们利用维基百科页面浏览量和ProQuest新闻报道构建了一个知名度指数。使用2000年至2025年的数据，我们比较了24只知名自主管理基金的披露与24只匹配控制基金的披露所引发的市场反应。知名管理者披露新建仓位会在申报日及随后几个交易日产生正向披露溢价，且该溢价随投资者知名度上升而增加。我们构建了日历时间组合，机械地复制各组披露的买入，并发现复制知名度最高的投资者可获得显著阿尔法，且该阿尔法持续数月且不发生反转。证据与一种纠缠的注意力—信息渠道最为一致：投资者知名度将稀缺的投资者注意力引向被选中的披露，而这些披露所揭示的信息随后被资本化进价格。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

We study whether stock disclosures by recognizable investors generate abnormal market reactions beyond the reactions produced by comparable but non-recognizable managers. We define the Buffett effect as the incremental abnormal return to a disclosed new position that is attributable to the public recognition of the disclosing investor. We construct a recognition index from Wikipedia pageviews and ProQuest news mentions. Using data from 2000 to 2025 we compare market reaction to disclosures of 24 recognizable discretionary funds with disclosures of 24 matched control funds. New-position disclosures by recognizable managers generate a positive disclosure premium over the filing date and the following trading days, and the premium increases with investor recognition. We construct calendar-time portfolios that mechanically copy each group's disclosed purchases and find that copying the most recognized investors earns a significant alpha that persists for months and does not reverse. The evidence is most consistent with an entangled attention-information channel: investor recognition directs scarce investor attention toward selected disclosures, and the information revealed in those disclosures is then capitalized into prices.
