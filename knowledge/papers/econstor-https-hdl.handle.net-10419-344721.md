标题：抵抗迁移？西巴尔干地区青年和年轻成年人的农村留守锚点

作者：Dufhues, Thomas; Möllers, Judith; Miftari, Iliriana; Simonovska, Ana; Grymshi, Desjana; Tuna, Emelj

摘要：在世界各地，许多年轻人正在离开农村地区以寻求更好的机会，但也有其他人抵制迁移并选择留下。理解为什么一些人尽管面临强烈的离开激励却仍然留下，已成为当代移民研究和农村研究的核心问题。本研究考察西巴尔干地区年轻人留在农村地区的意愿，特别关注心理和情感锚点，尤其是惯性和地方依恋（place attachment）。利用来自阿尔巴尼亚、波斯尼亚和黑塞哥维那、科索沃、黑山、北马其顿和塞尔维亚1848名15-40岁个体的调查数据，我们将层次聚类分析与按年龄分层的OLS回归相结合。聚类分析识别出四种锚定特征类型，从具有象征性依恋的较年轻受访者，到以职业为锚的年轻成年人，再到以家庭、工作、财产、日常惯例和归属感为特征的深度扎根成年人。惯性是所有年龄组中留守意愿最强且最一致的关联因素。地方依恋在较年轻年龄组中尤为重要，在聚类分析中表现为象征性依恋，在二十出头的受访者中则表现为有意义的预测因素。沉没成本和生计锚点，特别是有偿工作和与财产相关的承诺，在较年长受访者中更为突出。性别单独与留守意愿并不一致相关，尽管在某些模型中其关联因国家背景而异。研究结果表明，农村留守最好通过多维度的锚定模式来理解。本文强调了依恋、日常惯例、工作、财产、家庭组建和个人倾向如何在不同的年龄组中以不同方式组合，从而塑造留守意愿。

Title: Resisting the move? Anchors of rural staying among youth and young adults in the Western Balkans Authors: Dufhues, Thomas; Möllers, Judith; Miftari, Iliriana; Simonovska, Ana; Grymshi, Desjana; Tuna, Emelj Abstract: Across the world, many young people are leaving rural areas in search of better opportunities, yet others resist the move and choose to stay. Understanding why some remain despite strong incentives to leave has become a central question in contemporary migration and rural studies. This study examines intentions of young people to stay in rural areas in the Western Balkans, with particular attention to psychological and emotional anchors, especially inertia and place attachment. Using survey data from 1848 individuals aged 15–40 across Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Kosovo, Montenegro, North Macedonia, and Serbia, we combine hierarchical cluster analysis with age-stratified OLS regression. The cluster analysis identifies four anchoring profiles that range from symbolically attached younger respondents to occupationally anchored young adults and deeply rooted adults characterised by family, work, property, routine, and belonging. Inertia is the strongest and most consistent correlate of staying intentions across all age groups. Place attachment matters especially among younger age groups, appearing as symbolic attachment in the cluster analysis and as a meaningful predictor among respondents in their early twenties. Sunk costs and livelihood anchors, particularly paid work and property-related commitments, are more prominent among older respondents. Gender alone is not consistently associated with staying intentions, although its association varies across national contexts in some models. The findings demonstrate that rural staying is best understood through multidimensional patterns of anchoring. The paper highlights how attachment, routine, work, property, family formation, and personal dispositions combine differently across age groups to shape intentions to remain.
