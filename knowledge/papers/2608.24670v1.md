<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

人工智能（AI）与气候之间存在多种相互作用方式，但目前缺乏一个统一的分析框架来阐释这一复杂互动关系。为使AI投资与气候政策相协调，我们提出了这样一个框架，将AI对排放、产出及气候损害的影响整合进DICE模型之中。我们区分了类似信息通信技术（ICT）的AI前景与类似工业革命（IR）的AI前景。根据现有最佳证据进行校准后，我们发现AI发展总体上是增加污染的。在当前低减排水平下，类似ICT的AI将使2100年升温增加0.1摄氏度，而类似IR的AI则将增加0.8摄氏度。相应的气候成本分别抵消了AI经济收益的约五分之一和四分之一。实现2摄氏度温控目标将使最优的类似ICT（IR）AI投资率到2100年从低减排情景下的3.3%（5.1%）提升至3.7%（12.7%），这表明减缓气候变化与AI发展是互补的。我们进一步表明，AI与减排之间的投资权衡主要受AI的经济前景驱动，而非其排放足迹。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Artificial intelligence (AI) interacts with climate in various ways, while a unified analytical framework of this intricate interplay is lacking. To align AI investment with climate policy, we propose such a framework integrating AI's impact on emissions, output, and climate damages into the DICE model. We distinguish between ICT-like and Industrial Revolution (IR)-like AI prospects. Calibrated to the best available evidence, we find that AI development is net polluting. Under current low abatement, ICT-like AI adds 0.1 degree C to 2100 warming, while IR-like AI adds 0.8 degree C. The associated climate costs offset roughly one-fifth and one-quarter of AI's economic gains, respectively. Meeting the 2 degree C target saves the optimal ICT(IR)-like AI investment rate by 2100 from 3.3% (5.1%) under the low-abatement scenario to 3.7% (12.7%), indicating that mitigation is complementary to AI development. We further show that the investment trade-off between AI and abatement is driven primarily by AI's economic prospects, not by its emissions footprint.
