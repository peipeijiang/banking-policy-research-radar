<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

本研究考察了制约乌兹别克斯坦伊斯兰金融发展的制度性障碍，基于对金融部门利益相关者的原始访谈。本文对八位制度性利益相关者进行了聚焦的定性分析，采用半结构化访谈方式，受访者分别代表伊斯兰教法咨询与伊斯兰小额信贷、伊斯兰窗口、商业银行、一家伊斯兰融资公司、金融分析以及乌兹别克斯坦中央银行。主题分析产生了39个编码，归纳为八个主题。制度性证据表明，主要制约因素集中在供给端：难以获得廉价且稳定的符合伊斯兰教法（Shariah）的资金、法律与监管明确性不足、标准化与税收问题尚未解决、伊斯兰教法治理安排薄弱或仍处于发展阶段、合格人员与运营能力短缺，以及面向更复杂产品的基础设施有限。受访者还表示，穆拉巴哈（murabaha）和伊贾拉（ijara）目前较易实施，而穆沙拉卡（musharaka）和穆达拉巴（mudaraba）则面临审计、监督、审慎监管、透明度及资源配置方面的制约。研究结果支持采取分阶段的制度建设路径，即资金基础设施、详细监管、治理、人力资本和流动性机制与产品扩展同步发展。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

This study examines institutional barriers constraining Islamic finance development in Uzbekistan, drawing on original interviews with financial-sector stakeholders. The paper applies a focused qualitative analysis of semi-structured interviews with eight institutional stakeholders representing Shariah advisory and Islamic microfinance, Islamic windows, commercial banking, an Islamic financing company, financial analysis, and the Central Bank of Uzbekistan. Thematic analysis produced 39 codes grouped into eight themes. The institutional evidence indicates that the principal constraints are concentrated on the supply side: limited access to cheap and stable Shariah-compliant funding, incomplete legal and regulatory clarity, unresolved standardization and tax issues, weak or still-developing Shariah governance arrangements, shortages of qualified personnel and operational capacity, and limited infrastructure for more complex products. Respondents also reported that murabaha and ijara are currently easier to implement, whereas musharaka and mudaraba face audit, monitoring, prudential, transparency and resource-allocation constraints. The findings support a phased, institution-building approach in which funding infrastructure, detailed regulation, governance, human capital and liquidity mechanisms develop alongside product expansion.
