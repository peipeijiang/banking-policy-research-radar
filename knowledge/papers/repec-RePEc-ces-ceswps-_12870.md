<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

宗教教养是否塑造了成年后的人类发展，还是观察到的差异主要反映了个体在生命后期持续参与宗教活动的影响？我利用“全球繁荣研究”的三轮数据来研究这一问题，该数据覆盖了21个国家及中国香港地区的202,898名成年人。这些数据将约12岁时的宗教礼拜出席情况与童年条件的详细测量、当前的宗教活动以及重复测量的成年结果联系起来。童年时期的宗教出席与更高的成年繁荣度、健康水平、意义感、人际关系、品格以及部分经济结果相关联，尽管在控制家庭背景和父母宗教信仰后，这些差异显著缩小。生命历程比较使解释更加清晰。那些童年结束后便不再频繁参与的成年人并未展现出广泛的优势，而当前频繁参与的成年人，无论童年时期是否频繁出席，其表现特征相似。从童年持续参与宗教活动与总体非金融繁荣、健康、人际关系及部分经济结果上的适度额外优势相关。这些“激活对比”在同期、前瞻性及个体内分析中均保持相同的方向，但相对于当前参与所对应的更广泛差距而言，其效应较小，且无法与坚持参与的自我选择性或对童年出席情况的回忆偏差相分离。个体固定效应估计进一步表明，收入与繁荣在个体内部同步变动，但童年宗教形成并未系统性地调节这一关系。因此，宗教作为一种持续的生命历程制度发挥作用，而非一个可独立识别的童年期处理因素。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Does religious upbringing shape adult human development, or do observed differences primarily reflect continued participation later in life? I study this question using three waves of the Global Flourishing Study, covering 202,898 adults across 21 countries and Hong Kong. The data link religious-service attendance around age 12 to detailed measures of childhood conditions, current religious activity, and repeated adult outcomes. Childhood attendance is associated with higher adult flourishing, health, meaning, relationships, character, and selected economic outcomes, although these differences narrow substantially after accounting for family background and parental religiosity. Life-course comparisons sharpen the interpretation. Adults whose frequent participation ended after childhood display no broad advantage, while adults who currently participate frequently have similar profiles whether or not they attended frequently as children. Continued participation from childhood is associated with a modest additional advantage in aggregate nonfinancial flourishing, health, relationships, and some economic outcomes. These activation contrasts retain the same sign across contemporaneous, prospective, and within-person specifications, but they are small relative to the broader gap associated with current participation and cannot be separated from selection into persistence or correlated recall of childhood attendance. Individual fixed-effects estimates further show that income and flourishing move together within person, but childhood religious formation does not systematically moderate this relationship. Thus, religion functions as a continuing life-course institution rather than an independently identified childhood treatment.
