# Will national renewable costs continue declining?

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

自20世纪80年代以来，全球平均太阳能光伏（PV）成本下降了约两个数量级，而全球平均陆上风电成本下降了约一个数量级。但全球平均值只是部分情况：对于太阳能和风能，各国之间的成本差异约为一个数量级。我们策划了一个全面的国家成本数据库，并通过将平准化度电成本（LCOE）在国家层面分解为其各组成部分并分别建模，构建了未来成本的预测模型。对于太阳能，我们发现自1990年以来，组件成本和系统平衡（BOS）成本均以每年约12%的速度呈指数级下降。相比之下，风力涡轮机成本以每年约4%的速度呈指数级下降，而BOS成本则完全没有下降。这表明全球风电总成本将趋近于约35美元/兆瓦时的下限成本，到2050年达到约43美元/兆瓦时，而全球太阳能成本将继续呈指数级下降，到2050年达到约8美元/兆瓦时。对于太阳能，国家LCOE成本横截面差异中约有一半归因于容量系数的差异，尽管资本成本也起着重要作用。对于风能，横截面差异在容量系数、资本成本和投资成本之间大致均匀分布。国家BOS成本以约5-7年的时间尺度回归至全球成本，而其他因素的差异则更为持久。我们开发了一个简单的模型来预测与全球成本的偏差，并表明该模型能够以可预测的误差做出合理预测。最后，我们将我们的预测与综合评估模型的最新预测进行了比较，并表明中国仅占太阳能成本整体下降的约0.05%。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Since the 1980s, the global average solar photovoltaic (PV) cost has decreased by about two orders of magnitude while the global average onshore wind cost has decreased by about one order of magnitude. But global averages are only part of the story: For both solar and wind, costs vary between countries by about an order of magnitude. We curate a comprehensive database of national costs and build predictive models for future costs by decomposing the levelized cost of electricity (LCOE) into its components at the national level and modeling them separately. For solar we find that since 1990 the module cost and the balance of system (BOS) cost have both declined roughly exponentially at rates of 12% per year. In contrast, wind turbine cost has declined roughly exponentially by 4% per year, while BOS cost has not declined at all. This suggests that total global wind cost will approach a floor cost of about 35 USD/MWh, reaching about 43 USD/MWh in 2050, whereas global solar cost will continue to decline exponentially, reaching about 8 USD/MWh in 2050. For solar, about half of the cross-sectional variations in national LCOE costs is due to variations in capacity factors, though capital costs also play an important role. For wind, the cross-sectional variations are split approximately equally between capacity factor, capital costs, and investment costs. National BOS costs revert to global costs with a timescale of about 5-7 years, while variations in other factors are more persistent. We develop a simple model for predicting the deviations from global costs and show that it makes reasonable predictions with predictable errors. Finally, we compare our predictions to recent projections of integrated assessment models, and show that China has only accounted for about 0.05% of the overall decline in solar energy costs.
