<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

我们提出了一个关于最优贸易制裁的理论，涉及三个策略性参与者：本国（制裁方）、外国（目标方）和旁观者。旁观者可以单方面行动，或在参与约束下与任一方结盟。我们得出三个新结果。第一，在Stackelberg时序下或当转移支付不完全时，联盟成员通常会设定不同的关税。第二，随着制裁收紧，由于策略性互动，本国的福利甚至可以超过单边贸易条件最优水平。第三，我们刻画了当本国和外国争夺旁观者支持时的可行制裁目标。竞争下会出现一种替代实施方式：本国让外国获胜，但外国对旁观者的转移支付实现了制裁目标。我们将模型应用于对俄罗斯原油的制裁。在校准练习中，印度、中国和俄罗斯之间的合作最符合观测数据。如果中国和印度单方面行动，或者制裁联盟向它们提出更好的条件，制裁效果本可以大约提高70%。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

We provide a theory of optimal trade sanctions with three strategic players: Home (sanctioner), Foreign (target), and Bystander. Bystander may act unilaterally or ally with either party subject to a participation constraint. We derive three novel results. First, members of a coalition generically set different tariffs either under Stackelberg timing or when transfers are imperfect. Second, as sanctions tighten, Home's welfare can improve even beyond the unilateral terms-of-trade optimum due to strategic interactions. Third, we characterize feasible sanctions targets when Home and Foreign compete for Bystander's support. An alternative implementation arises with competition: Home lets Foreign win, but Foreign's transfer to Bystander implements the sanctions target. We apply our model to sanctions against Russian crude. In a calibration exercise, cooperation among India, China, and Russia best matches observed data. Sanctions could have been roughly 70% more effective had China and India acted unilaterally or had the sanctioning coalition made them a better offer.
