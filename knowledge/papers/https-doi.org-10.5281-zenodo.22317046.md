<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

This paper integrates a Gibbs-calibrated reassessment of six macroeconomic puzzles with the Scretching Economic Coordinate Model (SECM) and the Scretching Economic Fine Structure framework (SEFSC). Fourier operations are restricted to empirically smooth regimes, while Fejér and Lanczos robustness tests are used to measure and suppress spectral ringing near structural breaks and other discontinuities. Updated Okun–Phillips–Harvey horse races are combined with diagnostics from sovereign bonds, equities, money and credit, foreign-exchange markets, sovereign financing, and gold to construct conditional global scenarios for 2026–2036. The resulting Gloom, Balanced Sustainability, and Boom trajectories are presented as stress-test envelopes rather than deterministic point forecasts. The framework identifies nonlinear regime risk when adverse changes in fiscal composition, inflation persistence, labor-market conditions, term premia, exchange rates, and external financing reinforce one another. It also separates central-bank liquidity problems from sovereign-solvency problems: Federal Reserve swap lines address foreign-currency liquidity pressures, whereas sovereign stabilization may require domestic fiscal adjustment, multilateral lending, coordinated central-bank facilities, or International Monetary Fund support. IMF resources are therefore situated within a multilayer global financial safety net rather than treated as unlimited financing for governments or central banks. The analysis characterizes the 2026 bond-market episode primarily as a global repricing of duration, inflation, sovereign supply, and fiscal credibility. Although severe, the episode had not become an established systemic market-functioning failure as of the study’s data cutoff. The Federal Reserve consequently faces a two-dimensional policy problem: its policy rate exerts its strongest influence over short maturities, while debt issuance, inflation uncertainty, fiscal credibility, and term-premium movements increasingly determine long-term yields. Continued M2 expansion and comparatively accommodative credit conditions weaken the case for rapid monetary easing, even as employment growth loses momentum. The verified SECM configuration depicts the United States as an economy with a large domestic-absorption core and a negative net-export share. This expenditure composition changes how output growth is transmitted to unemployment, inflation, interest rates, and external balances. SEFSC results further show that macroeconomic coefficients must be estimated by regime. In particular, the post-1985 φ–inflation coefficient is comparatively weak, indicating that energy costs, inflation expectations, exchange-rate movements, fiscal credibility, and supply conditions are more influential in the current inflation regime. Gold is modeled as a conditional monetary and financial asset whose repricing depends on real interest rates, reserve diversification, exchange-rate movements, tail-risk demand, central-bank purchases, and confidence in fiscal and monetary institutions. Under the Gloom scenario, mutually reinforcing fiscal, inflationary, currency, and financing shocks produce higher term premia, weaker equity valuations, stronger safe-haven demand, and potentially discontinuous gold repricing. The Balanced Sustainability scenario combines gradual disinflation, credible debt management, moderate growth, and contained financial stress. The Boom scenario is driven by productivity gains, expanding investment, improved debt-service capacity, and growth strong enough to offset valuation pressure from moderately higher real yields. The international results reject mechanical transfers of U.S. coefficients to other economies. Japan, Europe, emerging Asia, Africa, and other regions require locally consistent national-account definitions, country-specific SECM coordinates, and separately estimated Okun, Phillips, term-structure, exchange-rate, and financing-response functions. The paper therefore proposes a regime-sensitive global monitoring system that distinguishes ordinary yield normalization from fiscal-dominance risk, liquidity disruption, sovereign-solvency stress, and broader systemic instability. ## 深度解读

> 分析依据：**全文深读**

### 核心结论

本文整合了吉布斯校准的六宏观经济谜题的体制评估与SECM和SEFSC框架。傅里叶操作限于平滑体制，而Fejér和Lanczos测试量化并抑制不连续处的振铃。更新后的Okun-Phillips-Harvey竞赛与债券、股票、货币、汇率、主权融资和黄金市场诊断相结合，构建2026-2036年全球条件情景。结果包括悲观、平衡可持续和繁荣路径，作为压力测试包络而非点预测。模型识别出财政构成、通胀持续性、劳动力市场疲软、期限溢价压力和外部融资压力相互强化时的非线性体制风险。它还区分了美联储互换额度流动性与主权偿付能力支持，将IMF资源置于多层全球金融安全网中，并将黄金重定价建模为实际收益率、储备多元化、汇率、尾部风险需求和政策可信度的函数。

### 主要创新

- 提出SECM会计架构，用φ和κ坐标精确表示支出构成，并推导出内部比率ρ和τ。
- 引入SEFSC框架，使宏观经济系数依赖于状态和体制，而非固定常数。
- 使用Fejér和Lanczos测试量化并抑制傅里叶操作中的振铃现象。
- 将Okun-Phillips-Harvey模型与SECM坐标结合，进行状态依赖的回归分析。
- 构建2026-2036年三种条件情景（悲观、平衡可持续、繁荣），作为压力测试工具。

### 研究方法

本文采用理论推导与实证分析相结合的方法。首先建立SECM会计恒等式，推导支出份额。然后基于SEFSC框架，对Okun和Phillips方程进行状态依赖和体制依赖的修正。使用傅里叶变换分析平滑体制，并用Fejér和Lanczos滤波器抑制振铃。最后，结合债券、股票、汇率、黄金等市场数据，构建条件情景分析。

### 关键结果

美国2026年Q2的φ值为0.850628，κ为0.149372，表明吸收核心占主导。；Okun方程的有效斜率β_eff为-0.133215，表明GDP增长对失业率的影响随φ变化。；Phillips方程中，φ系数在1985年前显著（-30.3），之后不显著（-3.14），表明体制变化。；2026年9月3日美国国债收益率曲线显示10年期为4.77%，30年期为5.25%，期限溢价上升。；M2从3月至7月增长5776亿美元，年化约5.4%，但通胀影响需结合其他因素。
