<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

医院使用重复的风险评分来决定何时发出警报。一些评估通过取这些评分的最小值来概括它们，并将该最小值作为回归断点设计（RD）中的运行变量。最小值能正确反映患者是否曾被警报。然而，它可能包含在较早的警报已经改变了诊疗之后记录的评分。本文研究了当早期干预改变后期评分时，最小值RD估计的是什么。我们使用一个两次评估模型，推导出一个公式，将RD对比分解为因果效应和一个组成项。当反馈使评分朝一个方向移动时，组成项等于断点处最小值密度的相对跳跃乘以一个预后对比。因此，密度恰好连续意味着无偏，而总体密度跳跃界定了偏倚的大小。在给定的敏感性假设下，对跳跃的有效推断将其转化为一个置信界。在该模型中，连续密度背后的偏倚要求两个方向都存在反馈。对于这种情况，我们用总边界移动来界定偏倚，并在明确的敏感性和平滑性假设下为二值结局构建保守的置信集。我们给出了独立均匀评分和相关高斯评分的闭式解。在一个例子中，四个百分点的真实风险降低表现为一个百分点的增加。模拟验证了这些公式。更窄的带宽、稳健的偏倚校正以及机器学习协变量调整都不能消除该偏倚。当早期和晚期治疗效果相同时，对第一个评分进行模糊RD可以恢复因果效应。我们的结果关注的是运行变量是如何构建的。我们不对任何已发表的临床研究进行评估。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Hospitals use repeated risk scores to decide when to send an alert. Some evaluations summarize these scores by their maximum and use the maximum as the running variable in a regression discontinuity (RD) design. The maximum shows correctly whether a patient was ever alerted. However, it can include scores recorded after an earlier alert has already changed care. In this paper, we study what the maximum-score RD estimates when an earlier intervention changes a later score. We use a two-assessment model and derive a formula that splits the RD contrast into the causal effect and a composition term. When feedback moves scores in one direction, the composition term equals the relative jump in the density of the maximum at the cutoff times a prognostic contrast. Therefore, an exactly continuous density means no bias, and the population density jump bounds the bias. Valid inference on the jump turns this into a confidence bound under a stated sensitivity assumption. Within this model, bias behind a continuous density requires feedback in both directions. For this case, we bound bias by total boundary movement and construct conservative confidence sets for binary outcomes under explicit sensitivity and smoothness assumptions. We give closed forms for independent uniform scores and for correlated Gaussian scores. In one example, a true risk reduction of four percentage points appears as a one-point increase. Simulations confirm the formulas. Narrower bandwidths, robust bias correction, and machine-learning covariate adjustment do not remove the bias. A fuzzy RD on the first score recovers the causal effect when early and late treatment have the same effect. Our results concern how the running variable is built. We do not assess any published clinical study.
