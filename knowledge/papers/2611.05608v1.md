# Debiased Conditional Independence Tests for Causal Effects

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05608v1)

## 一句话结论

> 论文提出三种去偏条件独立性检验（条件随机化检验、双残差化检验和基于增广逆倾向加权估计的检验），用于存在处理选择时检验高维结果与处理分配的条件独立性，模拟显示在重尾、异常值、混合分布和异质效应等非线性设定下比联合Wald检验更有功效。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

我们提出针对潜在高维结果变量上因果效应的稳健检验方法，其中可能存在处理选择偏倚。这些检验通过考察处理变量能否比仅用协变量更准确地从结果变量中预测出来，来衡量结果变量与处理分配之间的条件独立性。我们设计了三种条件独立性检验。第一，我们提出一种基于逆倾向加权均方误差检验统计量的条件随机化检验（conditional randomization test）。若倾向得分（propensity score）已知，该检验在有限样本下可控制检验水平。第二，我们提出一种双重残差化方法（double-residualization approach），即从处理和基于结果的处理预测中分别剔除协变量效应，并检验残差之间的相关性是否为零。第三，我们提出一种基于增广逆倾向加权估计量（augmented inverse propensity weighted estimator）的检验，该估计量建立在双重稳健、Neyman正交的总体矩之上，而这些总体矩是条件独立性的必要且充分条件。模拟结果表明，在许多非线性设定下，尤其是在重尾、离群值、混合分布、异质性效应以及效应符号相反的情形下，这三种检验相对于基于看似不相关回归（seemingly unrelated regression）的联合Wald检验均具有更高的检验功效。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

We propose robust tests for causal effects on potentially high-dimensional outcomes where there may be selection into treatment. These tests measure conditional independence of outcomes from treatment assignment by asking whether treatment can be predicted from outcomes more accurately than from covariates alone. We design three such tests of conditional independence. First, we propose a conditional randomization test based on an inverse propensity weighted mean squared error test statistic. If the propensity score is known, the test controls size in finite samples. Second, we propose a double-residualization approach, where we partial out covariate effects from both treatment and outcome-based treatment predictions, and test whether the correlation between the residuals is zero. Third, we propose a test based on an augmented inverse propensity weighted estimator built around doubly robust, Neyman-orthogonal population moments that are necessary and sufficient for conditional independence. Simulations show that all three tests improve power over a joint Wald test based on a seemingly unrelated regression across many non-linear settings, particularly under heavy tails, outliers, mixtures, heterogeneous effects, and effects with opposite signs.
