# University as catalyst of public R&D expenditures? An empirical assessment on EU NUTS 3 regions

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08004v1)

> 论文利用2007-2020年欧盟NUTS 3区域面板数据，采用控制函数法解决公共研发支出的内生性，发现大学存在并不能系统性放大公共研发支出对专利产出的正向效应，但在发展陷阱风险高和持续停滞的区域，大学的调节作用更明显。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

本研究考察大学是否塑造了公共研发支出的区域创新效应，以及这一作用是否因区域发展轨迹的不同而存在差异。我们分析了2007至2020年间1166个欧盟NUTS 3区域的面板数据，整合了欧洲公共研发资助、专利绩效、大学存在以及区域发展陷阱等方面的数据。为解决公共研发支出的内生性问题，我们采用了控制函数方法。结果表明，公共研发支出对大学区域和非大学区域的专利绩效均有正向影响，但二者之间的差异在统计上并不显著。因此，大学的存在并未系统性地放大公共研发的回报。然而，在面临发展陷阱风险的区域以及经历持续停滞的区域，其调节作用更为明显，而关于陷阱强度的证据则较弱。这些发现表明，大学充当的是公共研发的条件性催化剂，而非普遍性催化剂。其贡献在脆弱且持续停滞的区域发展轨迹中尤为突出，但在抵消严重结构性约束方面能力较弱。本研究通过揭示研究资助的有效性不仅取决于制度性存在，还取决于该制度能力所嵌入的发展轨迹，从而为关于公共研发地理学与区域发展陷阱的讨论作出了贡献。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

This study examines whether universities shape the regional innovation effects of public R&D expenditure and whether this role varies across regional development trajectories. We analyse a panel of 1166 EU NUTS 3 regions over the period 2007-2020, combining data on European public R&D funding, patenting performance, university presence and regional development traps. Toa ddress the endogeneity of public R&D expenditure, we employ a control function aproach. The results show that public r&D expendirue positively affects patenting performance in both university and non-university, but the difference between them is not statistically significant. University presence therefore does not sistematically amplify the returns to public R&D. However, its moderating role becomes more pronounced in regions exposed to development trap risk and in regions experiencing persistent stagnation, while evidence is weaker for trap intensity. These fundings suggest that universities act as contingent rather than unviersal catalyst of public R&D. Their contribution appears particularly relevant in vulnerable and persistently stagnant regional trajectories, but less able to offset sever structural constraints. The study contributes to debates on the geography of public R&D and regional development traps by showing that the effectiveness of research funding depends not only on institutional presence, bu also on the development rajectory in which that institutional capacity is embedded.
