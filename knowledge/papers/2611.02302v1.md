# Owning What Can Quit

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02302v1)

## 一句话结论

> 利用2015-2026年57起编辑集体辞职的编码数据、110种经济金融会计期刊的编辑任期及出版收入数据，分析期刊所有权与编辑社区分离对科学治理的影响，发现辞职无法逆转所有者决定、继任期刊成功依赖学术团体支持、编辑任期限制由出版商酌情执行且短期提升产出。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

学术期刊是科学基础设施的一部分，然而大多数学术期刊的刊名归商业出版商所有，而其学术地位却依赖于可能离职的主编和编委会。本文探讨这种分离对科学治理意味着什么。利用Retraction Watch记录的2015年至2026年间全部57起编委会集体辞职的编码数据集、110种经济学、金融学和会计学期刊的主编任期数据，以及期刊层面的出版和收入数据，我记录了三种模式。第一，辞职并不能逆转所有者的决定：在涉及更换主编的19起事件中，没有任何一起是所有者在该刊编委会辞职后做出让步的，因此编委会群体的影响力仅存在于其离职之前。第二，离任编委会能否带走期刊的学术地位，取决于编委会之外的外部机构：继任期刊最常出现在有学术团体支持编委会的情况下，而在医学领域则很少出现，因为该领域非营利性主办机构稀缺；与此同时，期刊排名列表评价的是刊名而非编辑人员，使声誉继续归属于所有者。第三，关于主编任期的明文限制由出版商酌情执行；在实施该限制的地方，产出增长了近四倍，这是一种短期收益，其对期刊学术地位的长期影响尚不清楚。我据此提出了对学术团体、资助机构、出版商以及编制期刊排名的机构的启示。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Academic journals are part of the infrastructure of science, yet most journal titles are owned by commercial publishers while their standing rests on editors and boards who can leave. This paper asks what that separation means for the governance of science. Using a coded dataset of all 57 editorial mass resignations recorded by Retraction Watch from 2015 to 2026, data on editor tenure at 110 economics, finance and accounting journals, and journal-level publication and revenue data, I document three patterns. First, resignation does not reverse an owner's decision: in none of the 19 episodes involving an editor's replacement did the owner back down after the board resigned, so editorial communities have influence only before they leave. Second, whether a departing board can take a journal's standing with it depends on institutions outside the board: successor journals follow most often where a scholarly society backs the board and rarely in medicine, where non-profit hosts are scarce, while journal ranking lists, which rate titles rather than the people who edit them, keep reputation with the owner. Third, a stated limit on editor tenure was applied at the publisher's discretion; where it was applied, output grew almost fourfold, a short-run gain whose long-run effect on the journal's standing is not yet known. I draw implications for scholarly societies, funders, publishers, and the bodies that compile journal rankings.
