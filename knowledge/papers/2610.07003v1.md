# Reliability of AI Agents: Rater Effects, Drift, and the Return to an Evaluation Program

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

企业越来越多地使用重复的人工评分来评估已部署的AI代理，然而观察到的分数变化可能既反映了测量过程的变化，也反映了代理本身的变化。企业同样很少知道，随着已部署系统的演进，一次评估能在多长时间内保持信息价值。我们将可靠性作为一个潜在状态进行研究，使用了来自一个已部署语音与视频面试代理的2，611份按评分标准打分的生产面试记录、重叠的人工评审员、代理的变更日志以及支持工单。评审员的严厉程度差异显著：两位评审员评估同一批次时，在综合指数上相差0.79个标准差，而评审员构成的变动使原始趋势趋于平坦。在调整评审员效应并对批次级估计进行平滑处理后，评估可靠性从2026年3月到8月提高了0.53个标准差。可靠性在记录的部署之间也发生了实质性变化。在估计的未解释变动速率下，预测不确定性在大约五周后达到最大观测部署对比的幅度，尽管这一时间范围估计不精确，且基于少量阶跃式变化。一项独立的运营结果朝同一方向变动：与面试相关的支持工单相对于技术问题工单每月下降约10%。这些数据并未识别评估对绩效的因果效应。相反，它们表明，纵向AI评估需要三项检查：评审员之间的可比性、漂移下的证据时效性，以及与 consequential operational outcomes（有实质影响的运营结果）的相互印证。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Firms increasingly evaluate deployed AI agents using repeated human ratings, yet observed score changes may reflect the measurement process as much as changes in the agent itself. Firms also rarely know how long an evaluation remains informative as deployed systems evolve. We study reliability as a latent state using 2,611 rubric-scored production interviews from a deployed voice-and-video interviewing agent, overlapping human reviewers, the agent's change log, and support tickets. Reviewer severity varied substantially: two reviewers assessing the same batches differed by 0.79 standard deviations on the composite index, and a shift in reviewer composition flattened the raw trend. After adjusting for reviewer effects and smoothing batch-level estimates, evaluated reliability increased by 0.53 standard deviations from March to August 2026. Reliability also shifted materially across logged deploys. At the estimated rate of unaccounted movement, forecast uncertainty reaches the magnitude of the largest observed deploy contrast after about five weeks, although this horizon is imprecisely estimated and based on a small number of step-like changes. An independent operational outcome moved in the same direction: interview-related support tickets fell by about 10% per month relative to technical-issue tickets. These data do not identify a causal effect of evaluation on performance. Instead, they show that longitudinal AI evaluation requires three checks: comparability across raters, evidence currency under drift, and corroboration with consequential operational outcomes.
