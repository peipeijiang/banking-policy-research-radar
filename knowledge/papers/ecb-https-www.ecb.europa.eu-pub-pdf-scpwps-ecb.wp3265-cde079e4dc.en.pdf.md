<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

石油供给新闻冲击如何传导至实际经济活动、金融状况和区域劳动力市场，这一问题重新成为宏观经济研究议程的核心。为回答这一问题，我们引入了因子贝叶斯加性回归树（FABART）模型——一种非线性因子增广向量自回归模型，并将其应用于包含外部识别石油供给新闻冲击的大型美国宏观金融数据集。该框架将大型宏观金融信息集与灵活的非参数测量方程相结合，使得非线性传导能够从数据中自然涌现，而非通过预先设定的函数形式强加。我们发现，不利的石油供给新闻冲击所引发的实际经济活动收缩，比同等规模有利冲击所带来的扩张更为强烈且更具持续性，其中工业生产、金融变量和股票价格方面的差异尤为显著。各州就业反应表现出高度异质性，制造业密集地区的收缩幅度明显更大，而能源生产州在面对不利石油供给新闻冲击时则呈现出部分抵消性动态。就冲击幅度而言，非线性主要出现在极小与中等油价变动之间：小冲击产生的响应较弱且估计精度不高，而中等冲击已能对工业生产和区域就业产生具有经济意义的影响。更大规模的冲击并未在变量和冲击方向上系统性地产生按比例更强的响应。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

The question of how oil supply news shocks transmit to real activity, financial conditions, and regional labor markets is back at the center of the macroeconomic research agenda. To answer this question, we introduce the Factor Bayesian Additive Regression Tree (FABART) model, a nonlinear factor-augmented vector autoregression model, and apply it to a large U.S. macro-financial dataset with externally identified oil supply news shocks. The framework combines a large macro-financial information set with a flexible nonparametric measurement equation, allowing nonlinear transmission to emerge from the data rather than being imposed through a pre-specified functional form. We find that adverse oil supply news shocks generate stronger and more persistent contractions in real activity than the expansions associated with favorable shocks of comparable magnitude, with especially pronounced differences in industrial production, financial variables, and equity prices. Employment responses are highly heterogeneous across U.S. states, with substantially stronger contractions in manufacturing-intensive regions, while energy-producing states display partially offsetting dynamics following adverse oil supply news shocks. Across shock magnitudes, nonlinearities arise mainly between very small and moderate oil-price movements: small shocks generate weak and imprecisely estimated responses, while moderate shocks already produce economically meaningful effects on industrial production and regional employment. Larger shocks do not systematically generateproportionally stronger responses across variables and shock signs.
