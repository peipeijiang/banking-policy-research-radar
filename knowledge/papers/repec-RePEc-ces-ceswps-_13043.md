# Global Flood Risk in the 2026/27 El Niño: An Empirical Estimate from the Historical Relationship

[查看原文](https://ideas.repec.org/p/ces/ceswps/_13043.html)

## 一句话结论

> 利用1980-2025年104国月度面板数据，估计Niño3.4海温异常对洪水事件数量的影响，发现厄尔尼诺重新分配而非增加全球洪水总数，并预测2026/27年部分国家洪水频率显著上升。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

我们估计了104个国家已记录洪水活动对Niño3.4海表温度异常的响应，使用的是基于EM-DAT（1980–2025年）和NOAA Niño3.4序列构建的月度国家面板数据。识别策略来自指数在国内、同一日历月内的变异，并剔除了报告强度的线性趋势。主要结果是已记录洪水的期望数量，采用准泊松回归估计；同时报告了洪水发生的逻辑斯蒂模型，其结果几乎一致。厄尔尼诺重新分配了洪水活动，但并未显著改变全球总量。分析发现，44个国家的洪水数量增加，其中10个国家的增加具有统计显著性。国家效应较大且在地理上具有一致性，从乌拉圭的每摄氏度2.26到多哥的0.46不等。将ECMWF Niño3.4展望应用于中性反事实情景，这些国家已记录洪水的期望数量在2026年上升至2.05倍，2027年上升至2.88倍。与此同时，分析表明60个国家的洪水数量减少，其中19个国家的减少具有统计显著性。从绝对数量来看，在主要情景下，正信号国家预计在2026年额外记录+42.9次洪水，2027年额外记录+57.1次；而负信号国家预计在2026年减少31.0次洪水，2027年减少30.6次。两者在很大程度上相互抵消，净变化分别为+11.8次和+26.5次事件。作为比较，在估计效应显著的29个国家中，本研究的估计结果与Ward等（2014）基于模型的厄尔尼诺洪水信号在86%的国家中符号一致。期望洪水数量减少并不意味着风险降低。这些估计描述的是各国进入灾害登记册的期望速率的变化；它们并未对单次事件的严重程度设定任何界限。在每个ENSO相位中都会记录到造成损害的洪水，在洪泛区暴露度高的国家，一个受抑制季节内的单次事件可能大幅超过年均损失。研究结果不支持降低任何备灾水平。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

We estimate the response of recorded flood activity to the Niño3.4 sea-surface-temperature anomaly for 104 countries, using a monthly country panel built from EM-DAT (1980–2025) and the NOAA Niño3.4 series. Identification comes from within-country, within-calendar-month variation in the index, net of a linear trend in reporting intensity. The headline outcome is the expected number of recorded floods, estimated by quasi-Poisson regression; a logistic model of flood occurrence is reported alongside and gives near-identical answers. El Niño redistributes flood activity but does not change significantly the world total. The analysis finds an increase in the number of floods in 44 countries, with this increase significant in 10 countries. Country effects are large and geographically coherent, from 2.26 per °C in Uruguay to 0.46 in Togo. Applying the ECMWF Niño3.4 outlook against a neutral counterfactual, the expected number of recorded floods in these countries rises by 2.05× in 2026 and 2.88× in 2027. In parallel, the analysis suggests a decrease in the number of floods in 60 countries, with this decrease significant in 19 countries. In absolute terms, the countries with a positive signal are expected to record +42.9 additional floods in 2026 and +57.1 in 2027 on the main scenario, while the countries with a negative signal are expected to record 31.0 fewer floods in 2026 and 30.6 fewer in 2027. The two largely offset, leaving net changes of +11.8 and +26.5 events. As a comparison, the estimates agree in sign with the model-based El Niño flood signal of Ward et al. (2014) for 86% of the 29 countries whose estimated effect is significant. A reduced expected flood count does not imply reduced risk. The estimates describe a shift in the expected rate at which countries enter the disaster register; they place no bound on the severity of individual events. Damaging floods are recorded in every ENSO phase, and in countries with high floodplain exposure a single event within a suppressed season can exceed the annual average loss by a large margin. The results do not support any reduction in preparedness.
