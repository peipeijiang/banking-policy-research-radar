# Do wind and solar curtail at negative electricity prices? Incentives and evidence across two decades of German renewable support schemes

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10053v1)

## 一句话结论

> 论文利用德国二十年可再生能源支持政策数据，逐小时估计风电和光伏在负电价下的弃电激励，并用再分析天气数据构建市场化弃电估计，发现2025年负电价下多数风电有激励却仅半数弃电，光伏因上网电价保护而暴露不足，导致补贴支付膨胀和电网运行困难。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

在许多电力系统中，风能和太阳能发电越来越频繁地超过电力需求。在这些时段削减可再生能源发电，对价格和电网的物理稳定性都至关重要。关闭风力涡轮机和太阳能电池板在技术上比降低大型发电站的出力更容易，但支持政策往往给可再生能源提供经济激励，使其在负电价时仍继续发电。本文研究德国的风能和太阳能。对于每一批发电机组，本文逐小时估算了两十年支持政策所隐含的激励。随后，本文利用基于再分析天气数据构建的市场化弃电新估计，将这些激励与观察到的行为进行对照。我发现，在2025年，当价格低于-50欧元/兆瓦时时，几乎所有风力发电机组都有停止发电的激励，但只有一半实际这样做了。太阳能则相反：近三分之二的潜在弃电没有任何削减激励，主要是因为其获得的上网电价补贴使其免受批发电价的影响。在剩余暴露于市场价格的部分中，仅略超过五分之一削减了发电量。低暴露率和低响应率推高了补贴支付，并使电力系统更难以安全运行。我的结论是，风能和太阳能的进一步扩张要求它们对价格信号作出响应。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

In many power systems, wind and solar generation increasingly often exceeds electricity demand. Curtailing renewable generation in those hours matters both for prices and for the physical stability of the grid. Turning off wind turbines and solar panels is technically easier than ramping down a large power station, yet support schemes often give renewables an economic incentive to keep producing at negative prices. This paper studies wind and solar energy in Germany. For each cohort of generators it estimates, hour by hour, the incentive implied by two decades of support policy. It then sets those incentives against observed behavior, using a new estimate of market-based curtailment built from reanalysis weather data. I find that in 2025, at prices below -50 EUR/MWh, almost all wind generators had an incentive to stop producing, but only half of them did. Solar is the opposite case: nearly two thirds of the potential had no incentive to curtail at all, mostly because it receives a feed-in tariff that shields it from wholesale prices. Of the exposed remainder, just over a fifth cut production. Low exposure and response rates inflate subsidy payments and make the power system harder to operate safely. I conclude that a further expansion of wind and solar requires them to respond to price signals.
