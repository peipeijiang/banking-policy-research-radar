<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

为什么成绩优异、低收入的学生不选择就读选择性大学？利用限制使用数据，我们表明这一差距出现在申请阶段：低收入学生的申请量要少得多，而录取和入学方面的差异则较为有限。我们构建并估计了一个均衡模型，其中异质性的学生和大学各自对能力持有带噪声的评估，受借贷约束的学生在录取和助学金风险下选择申请组合，而席位稀缺的大学通过录取标准和学费方案进行竞争。由于只有能力最高的低收入学生才会申请，大学向他们提供慷慨的资助。扩大资助会提高学费和录取标准，而非提高入学人数。更好的信息，而非补贴，才能将人才放到其应有的位置。

</details>

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Why do high-achieving, low-income students not attend selective colleges? Leveraging restricted-use data, we show the gap arises at the application stage: lower-income students apply much less, while admission and enrollment differences are modest. We develop and estimate an equilibrium model in which heterogeneous students and colleges each hold noisy assessments of ability, borrowing-constrained students choose application portfolios under admission and financial-aid risk, and colleges with scarce seats compete through admission standards and tuition schedules. Because only the highest-ability low-income students apply, colleges offer them generous aid. Expanding aid raises tuition and standards, not enrollment. Better information, not subsidies, puts talent where it belongs.
