# Do commodity prices signal inflation under quantitative easing? Evidence from interest rate co-movements

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

本文考察了在美联储量化宽松（QE）及新冠疫情期间政策下，实际大宗商品价格变动与利率变动之间的动态关系。我们采用小波相干性和相位差分析方法，对2003年1月至2025年12月期间的数据进行分析，从时频角度刻画了这些变量在非常规货币政策体制下的协同变动特征。我们发现，在QE和新冠疫情期间，利率变动与金属、能源、农产品及食品类大宗商品的实际价格变动之间均存在稳健的短期正相关关系。贵金属在次贷危机期间呈现负相关，反映了保值需求。关键的是，难以储存的大宗商品的实际价格变动往往领先于利率变动，这与大宗商品价格预判货币政策行动的规律一致。为区分通胀相关渠道与更广泛的混杂因素，我们实施了以货币增长和核心通胀为条件变量的偏小波相干分析。在这些条件下，相关性显著减弱，这支持了货币数量论和费雪效应，表明所观察到的协同变动在很大程度上与通胀预期相关联。我们的研究具有直接的政策含义：当零利率下限抑制了常规利率渠道时，大宗商品价格充当了通胀预期演变的高频晴雨表。这要求政策制定者在非常规货币政策实施期间将能源、金属和食品市场视为主要的领先指标。作为对上述政策洞见的补充，我们引入了一种基于小波的方法论，通过揭示标准计量经济学方法系统性压制的非线性、时变动态特征，显著推进了现有文献。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

This paper examines the dynamic relationship between real commodity price changes and interest rate changes during the U.S. Federal Reserve’s quantitative easing (QE) and COVID-19-era policies. Employing wavelet coherency and phase difference analysis over the period from January 2003 to December 2025, we provide a time–frequency characterisation of how these variables co-move under unconventional monetary regimes. We find robust short-term positive correlations between interest rate changes and real price changes across metals, energy, agriculture, and food commodities during both the QE and COVID-19 periods. Precious metals exhibit negative correlations during the subprime crisis, reflecting demand for value preservation. Critically, real price changes in hard-to-store commodities frequently lead interest rate changes, consistent with commodity prices anticipating monetary policy actions. To distinguish inflation-related channels from broader confounders, we implement partial wavelet coherency conditioned on money growth and core inflation. The significant attenuation of correlations under these conditions supports the quantity theory of money and the Fisher effect, indicating that the observed co-movements are substantially linked to inflation expectations. Our findings carry a direct policy implication: when the zero lower bound stifles conventional interest rate channels, commodity prices act as high-frequency barometers of evolving inflation expectations. This necessitates that policymakers treat energy, metals, and food markets as primary leading indicators during episodes of unconventional monetary policy. Complementing this policy insight, we introduce a wavelet-based methodology that significantly advances the literature by exposing the nonlinear, time-varying dynamics that standard econometric approaches systematically suppress.
