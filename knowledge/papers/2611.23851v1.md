# Match Rate Is Not Match Quality

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23851v1)

## 一句话结论

> 论文利用选民档案与移动广告标识符的匹配数据，比较两家身份解析供应商的匹配质量，发现匹配率虽高但条件身份级精确度仅约13%-15%，主张匹配率不能替代匹配质量。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

第三方身份解析（identity-resolution）供应商通常将匹配率——即其能够关联到用于广告激活的标识符的提交记录所占比例——作为其核心质量指标加以强调。然而，高匹配率并不必然意味着这些匹配到的标识符属于所提交的记录（即目标对象）。本文探讨将匹配质量作为匹配率的替代指标，利用一个独特的数据集来评估匹配质量。我们能够比较提交记录（选民档案）中的两个人口统计特征——年龄和性别——与由两家领先身份解析供应商关联至移动广告标识符的自我报告调查中相同人口统计特征的一致性。数据及匹配过程来自2019-20年。

返回记录的匹配质量存在相当大的模糊性。首先，两家供应商的激活文件每次匹配均包含多个设备标识符。其次，经审计的配对（供应商A）以及明确标注为个体层面匹配（供应商B）的匹配质量分别仅为21.8%和23.6%。利用一个假设正确链接在这两个人口统计特征上始终一致、而错误链接有10%概率一致的混合模型，这些比率意味着条件身份层面精确度分别约为15%和13%。

本文所使用的身份解析产品已有数年历史，但我们所探讨的问题仍具有根本性：仅凭匹配率无法确定广告主是否触达了其 intended targets（目标受众）。评估身份解析需要独立于匹配率的匹配质量证据。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Third-party identity-resolution vendors commonly emphasize match rate, the share of submitted records they can link to identifiers for advertising activation, as their core metric of quality. Yet a high match rate does not necessarily mean that those matched identifiers belong to the submitted records (i.e., the intended targets). In this paper we explore match quality as an alternative metric to match rate, utilizing a unique data set to evaluate match quality. We are able to compare two demographics, age and gender, from a submitted record (voter files) to the same demographics in self-reported surveys matched to mobile advertising identifiers linked together by two leading identity-resolution vendors. The data and matching was from 2019-20.
  There is substantial ambiguity in match quality of the returned records. First, both vendors' activation files contain multiple device identifiers per match. Second, match quality among audited pairs (Vendor A) and for explicitly labeled individual-level matches (Vendor B) is only 21.8% and 23.6%, respectively. Utilizing a mixture model that assumes that correct links always agree on these two demographics and incorrect links agree 10% of the time, these rates imply conditional identity-level precisions of approximately 15% and 13%, respectively.
  This paper utilizes identity-resolution products that are several years old, but the question we address remains fundamental: match rate alone cannot establish whether advertisers are reaching the people they intended. Evaluating identity resolution requires independent evidence of match quality alongside match rate.
