扩张（Dilation，Seidenfeld and Wasserman 1993）指的是这样一种现象：无论新信息揭示的内容是什么，仅凭以新信息为条件进行更新就只会增加不确定性。扩张通常被视为模糊性模型的一个不受欢迎的特征，并引发了关于信念更新的持续争论。本文表明，遵循既定的临床指南可能诱发扩张，即使对于满足常规性能基准的诊断检测也是如此。我们刻画了检测何时会诱发扩张，并证明性能研究中一个普遍存在的特征——使用不完美的参考标准来评估检测——为扩张创造了空间。为了便于在临床使用之前进行检测并为监管标准提供依据，我们开发了一种有限样本有效的假设检验，用于检验在此情境下诊断检测不诱发扩张的原假设。将该方法应用于一项在COVID-19胸部CT筛查临床采纳之前开展且对其产生推动作用的重要研究，我们发现数据与扩张相一致。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Dilation (Seidenfeld and Wasserman 1993) occurs when conditioning on new information \textit{only} increases uncertainty regardless of what the information reveals. Often viewed as an undesirable feature of models of ambiguity, dilation has sparked sustained debate about belief updating. This paper shows that following established clinical guidance can induce dilation, even for diagnostic tests that meet conventional performance benchmarks. We characterize when a test is dilation-inducing and show that a ubiquitous feature of performance studies -- evaluating tests against an imperfect reference -- creates scope for dilation. To facilitate detection before clinical use and inform regulatory standards, we develop a finite-sample valid hypothesis test of the null that a diagnostic test is dilation-inducing in this context. Applying the method to an influential study that preceded and contributed to the clinical adoption of chest CT screening for COVID-19, we find that the data are consistent with dilation.
