<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

人工智能的进步何时会带来经济转型？我们构建了一个模型，刻画智能——即实现目标的能力——如何影响组织这一经济生产的关键单元。具有多维能力的主体（人类或AI）内生地分选进入相互竞争的企业。企业的生产率取决于其主体的能力；我们区分了能力（competence）与协调（coordination）：前者改变生产率水平，后者决定生产率如何随规模扩展。AI与人类之间的两个差异驱动了转型：AI的专业技能是可复制的，而人类的技能供给是固定的；且AI可能不受人类在协调方面所面临的相同限制。我们分析了随着AI能力提升，均衡市场结构、生产率、就业和工资如何演变。当AI比普通人更有能力时，由互补性人类组成的组织仍可能胜过AI。AI能力的提升可以改变企业内劳动者的构成并缩小企业规模。然而，随着AI协调能力的改善，这可能突破企业规模的科斯天花板（Coasean ceiling），市场结构将经历向大型、集中的AI密集型企业相变的过程。当AI在人类-AI混合企业中替代工人时，人类就业的变化是渐进的；而当AI企业击败人类企业时，变化则是突发的。我们将工资变化分解为技术效应——即改变人类在现有企业中的价值——和重组效应——即企业调整规模与构成。重组可以逆转技术效应对工资的影响。

</details>

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

When will advances in AI lead to economic transformation? We develop a model of how intelligence, the ability to achieve goals, matters for organizations, the key unit of economic production. Agents (human or AI) with multidimensional capabilities sort endogenously into competing firms. A firm's productivity depends on its agents' capabilities; we distinguish competence, which shifts productivity levels, from coordination, which governs how productivity scales with size. Two differences between AI and humans drive transformation: AI expertise is reproducible while humans' are in fixed supply, and AIs may not face the same human limits on coordination. We analyze how equilibrium market structure, productivity, employment, and wages evolve as AI capabilities improve. When AI is more capable than the average human, organizations of complementary humans can nonetheless outperform AI. Increases in AI competence can change the composition of workers within firms and decrease firm size. As AI coordination improves, however, this can break the Coasean ceiling on firm size and the market structure undergoes a phase transition toward large, concentrated AI-heavy firms. Changes in human employment are gradual when AI displaces workers within mixed human-AI firms, or sudden when AI firms outcompete human firms. We decompose wage changes into a technology effect, which changes the value of humans within existing firms, and a reorganization effect as firms adjust size and composition. Reorganization can reverse the technology effect on wages.
