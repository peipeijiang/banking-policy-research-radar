<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

地缘政治紧张局势有可能将全球经济割裂为相互对立的集团。作为这种割裂的驱动因素，我们提出一个基于社会认同理论的需求侧渠道：消费者对商品的估值部分取决于其原产国所体现的政治和经济价值观。我们将这一机制嵌入一个贸易模型，模型包含两个政治价值观相似的国家和一个政治价值观不同的国家，并以美国、欧盟和中国作为基准示例。我们表明，一场提升政治价值观相对于经济价值观显著性的地缘政治冲击，会将贸易转向价值观相似的那对国家，并且还能使它们之间原本缺乏吸引力的标准协调协议变得互利共赢。随后，若这对相似国家因政治价值观分化或贸易战措施而疏远，则可以逆转上述两种效应，并推动世界经济从两个集团走向三个集团。我们还将该框架应用于美国与欧盟和加拿大的疏远情形。美国在政治价值观上的分化以及贸易战措施都会将贸易转向欧盟—加拿大这对组合，但只有前者才能使原本缺乏吸引力的欧盟—加拿大协议变得互利共赢。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Geopolitical tensions threaten to fragment the global economy into rival blocs. As a driver of this fragmentation, we propose a demand-side channel grounded in social identity theory: consumers value goods partly by the political and economic values embodied in their origin. We embed this in a trade model with two countries that are similar in terms of political values and one dissimilar country, with the US, the EU and China as our benchmark example. We show that a geopolitical shock raising the salience of political over economic values diverts trade toward the similar pair and can also make a previously unattractive standards-harmonization agreement between them mutually beneficial. A subsequent estrangement of the similar pair, through diverging political values or trade-war measures, can reverse both effects and push the world economy from two blocs toward three. We also apply the framework to an estrangement of the US from the EU and Canada. Both a US divergence in political values and trade-war measures divert trade toward the EU-Canada pair, but only the former can make a previously unattractive EU-Canada agreement mutually beneficial.
