<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

亚太地区沿海区域经济活动集中，同时面临日益加剧的洪水风险，然而谁最为暴露——以及暴露程度与当地财富之间的关系——仍鲜为人知。我们将5公里和500米分辨率的卫星估算财富数据与10个国家多种情景下的海岸淹没预测地图相结合，发现沿海地区平均比内陆地区富裕0.75个标准差——这是一项覆盖城市和农村社区的广泛红利。然而，在沿海地带内部，受洪水暴露的人群比其邻居贫困0.26至0.39个标准差。这一总体结果掩盖了显著的异质性：孟加拉国、印度和缅甸面临贫困与暴露的持续双重负担，而柬埔寨的财富差距在高排放情景下进一步恶化。在菲律宾、东帝汶、印度尼西亚和巴基斯坦，暴露日益集中在较富裕地区。因此，气候风险与社会经济动态以国别特有的方式相互交织，随着排放上升重塑着谁最为脆弱。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Coastal regions in Asia and the Pacific concentrate economic activity while facing intensifying flood risk, yet who is most exposed—and how exposure relates to local wealth—remains poorly understood. Combining satellite-derived wealth estimates at 5 km and 500 m resolution with projected coastal inundation maps under multiple scenarios across 10 countries, we find that coastal areas are on average 0.75 standard deviations wealthier than inland areas—a broad based dividend spanning urban and rural communities. Within coastal zones, however, flood exposed populations are 0.26–0.39 standard deviations poorer than their neighbors. This aggregate masks sharp heterogeneity: Bangladesh, India, and Myanmar face a persistent double burden of deprivation and exposure while the wealth gap in Cambodia worsens under high emissions. In the Philippines, Timor-Leste, Indonesia, and Pakistan, exposure increasingly concentrates in wealthier areas. Climate risk and socioeconomic dynamics are thus intertwined in country-specific ways, reshaping who is most vulnerable as emissions rise.
