<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

一个国家在结构上能够维持多少个政党？有效政党数（effective number of parties, ENP）等标准指标无法回答这一问题。这些指标是描述性的、事后性的：它们仅在选举之后对竞争状况进行总结，且依赖于选举结果数据，而在许多发展中民主国家，尤其是非洲，这些数据往往缺失、中断或不可靠。它们也无法提供一种共同的尺度，用以比较政党登记规模与实际选举碎片化程度。我们提出结构性政党数（Structural Number of Parties, SNP），这是一个与选举结果无关的基准，源自两个普遍可获取的量：人口规模和领土面积。基于席位乘积模型（seat product model）的逻辑和议会规模的立方根定律（cube-root law of assembly size），SNP提供了一个可在任何国家和年份计算的事前基准，包括在选举数据缺失的情况下。将SNP应用于非洲国家，能够实现结构性差距诊断，从而区分碎片化不足的体制（以霸权政党主导为特征）与过度碎片化的体制（以过度原子化为特征）。因此，该框架提供了一个透明且可复现的工具，可为整个非洲大陆的制度改革和政党体系合理化提供参考。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

How many parties can a country structurally sustain? Standard measures such as the effective number of parties (ENP) cannot answer this question. They are descriptive and post hoc: they summarize competition only after an election, and they depend on electoral returns that are often missing, interrupted, or unreliable in many developing democracies, particularly in Africa. They also offer no common scale for comparing the size of party registers with realized electoral fragmentation. We introduce the Structural Number of Parties (SNP), an outcome-independent baseline derived from two universally available quantities: population size and territorial area. Building on the logic of the seat product model and the cube-root law of assembly size, the SNP gives an ex ante benchmark that can be computed for any country and year, including where electoral data are absent. Applied to African states, the SNP enables a structural gap diagnosis that distinguishes under-fragmented systems, marked by hegemonic-party dominance, from over-fragmented ones, marked by excessive atomization. The framework thus provides a transparent and reproducible tool to inform institutional reform and the rationalization of party systems across the continent.
