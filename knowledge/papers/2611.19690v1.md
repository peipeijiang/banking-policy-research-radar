<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

我们比较两样本汇总数据工具变量（IV）模型中的三个估计量：逆方差加权（IVW）、去偏逆方差加权（dIVW）和轮廓得分（PS）估计量。该框架假设样本独立且工具变量特异性汇总估计，两样本孟德尔随机化是其主要应用。我们比较现有的固定工具变量和许多弱工具变量结果，以及三个估计量的充分条件。在轮廓得分文献所使用的方差缩放下，PS和dIVW的主要集体强度条件是等价的，尽管它们在工具变量集中度和增长方面的额外限制有所不同。然后，我们比较它们在固定工具变量数和发散工具变量数下的渐近方差公式。对于固定$L$，IVW和dIVW具有相同的一阶渐近方差。在共同的方差比条件下，PS方差公式比IVW方差超出一个$n^{-2}$阶的正项，而主导方差为$n^{-1}$阶。对于发散的$L$，我们建立了跨工具变量强度机制的方差阶数，以及在相同方差比条件下的精确排序$\mathsf{V}_{\mathtt{IVW}} < V_{\mathtt{PS}} \leq \mathsf{V}_{\mathtt{dIVW}}$。当$\sqrt{L}κ\to\infty$时，绝对方差差距消失，而相对等价性当且仅当$κ\to\infty$时成立。这些比较涉及渐近方差公式，而非有限样本效率或均方误差。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

We compare three estimators in a two-sample summary-data instrumental variables (IV) model: inverse-variance weighting (IVW), debiased inverse-variance weighting (dIVW), and the profile score (PS) estimator. The framework assumes independent samples and instrument-specific summary estimates, with two-sample Mendelian randomisation as a leading application. We compare existing fixed- and many-weak-instrument results and the sufficient conditions for the three estimators. Under the variance scaling used in the profile-score literature, the principal collective-strength conditions for PS and dIVW are equivalent, although their additional restrictions on instrument concentration and growth differ. We then compare their asymptotic variance formulae for fixed and diverging numbers of instruments. For fixed $L$, IVW and dIVW have the same first-order asymptotic variance. Under a common variance-ratio condition, the PS variance formula exceeds the IVW variance by a positive term of order $n^{-2}$, compared with the order-$n^{-1}$ leading variance. For diverging $L$, we establish variance orders across instrument-strength regimes and the exact ordering $\mathsf{V}_{\mathtt{IVW}} < V_{\mathtt{PS}} \leq \mathsf{V}_{\mathtt{dIVW}}$ under the same variance-ratio condition. Absolute variance gaps vanish when $\sqrt{L}κ\to\infty$, whereas relative equivalence holds if and only if $κ\to\infty$. These comparisons concern asymptotic variance formulae rather than finite-sample efficiency or mean squared error.
