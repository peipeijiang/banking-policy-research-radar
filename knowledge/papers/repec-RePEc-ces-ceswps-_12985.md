<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

对事实和法律的不知情可能为自利性报告行为提供借口，即使冒着陈述不实信息的风​​险。本文研究了决策者在不知道其真实应得经济利益的情况下会报告什么、为何如此报告，以及由此产生的不知情困境如何得以缓解。在一项理论指导的在线实验中，我表明，相对于完全信息的基准情形，不知情会大幅增加自利性但可能不真实的报告行为。三种机制——两种行为机制和一种制度机制——驱动了这一转变：(i) 决策者对其真实应得权益的信念，(ii) 支配不知情情况下报告行为的社会规范，以及(iii) 提供确定性陈述的要求。我评估了两种干预措施：允许获取信息和提供对未知权益的公平买断。两者都能缓解不知情情况下的自利性报告，但公平买断涉及一种权衡：它具有增加知情决策者不诚实行为的意外副作用。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Ignorance of facts and laws may provide an excuse for self-serving reporting behavior, even at the risk of telling the untruth. This paper examines what decision-makers report when they do not know their true entitlement to a financial gain, why they do so, and how the resulting dilemma under ignorance can be mitigated. In a theory-guided online experiment, I show that ignorance substantially increases self-serving but potentially untruthful reporting behavior relative to a full-information benchmark. Three mechanisms – two behavioral and one institutional – drive this shift: (i) decision-makers' beliefs about their true entitlement, (ii) social norms governing reporting under ignorance, and (iii) the requirement to provide a definite statement. I evaluate two interventions: allowing information acquisition and offering a fair buyout of the unknown entitlement. Both mitigate self-serving reporting under ignorance, but the fair buyout involves a trade-off: it has the unintended side effect of increasing dishonesty among informed decision-makers.

</details>
