<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

我们研究企业如何在跨国子公司网络中部署组织能力和知识型能力。利用2006年至2021年韩国跨国企业详细的企业层面面板数据，我们分析了国内研发投资、海外子公司扩张与跨国生产结构演变之间的关系。本文有别于将外商直接投资主要视为一系列孤立区位决策的方法。相反，我们将跨国企业解释为在 geographically dispersed affiliate networks（地理分散的子公司网络）中积累和部署无形能力的组织系统。在这一框架下，国内研发投资产生组织和技术能力，从而促进对日益复杂的国际运营的管理。我们记录了三个主要发现。第一，国内研发、专利活动和无形资本的增加与企业海外投资头寸的增加密切相关。第二，国内研发与跨国活动的扩展边际（extensive margin）关系尤为密切：在国内积累更多知识的企业会扩展到更多海外子公司和目的地市场。第三，在使用国外行业研发的工具变量泊松设定中，扩展边际关系仍然为正且被精确估计，同时企业层面FDI估计在工具变量和动态面板模型中也为正。我们的发现通过记录国内无形投资如何与跨国活动的规模和地理分布相关联，为关于跨国企业、组织资本和全球生产网络的文献作出了贡献。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

We study how firms deploy organizational and knowledge-based capabilities across multinational affiliate networks. Using detailed firm-level panel data on Korean multinational firms from 2006 to 2021, we analyze the relationship between domestic R&D investment, foreign affiliate expansion, and the evolution of multinational production structures. The paper departs from approaches that view foreign direct investment primarily as a sequence of isolated location decisions. Instead, we interpret multinational firms as organizational systems that accumulate and deploy intangible capabilities across geographically dispersed affiliate networks. In this framework, domestic R&D investments generate organizational and technological capabilities that facilitate the management of increasingly complex international operations. We document three main findings. First, increases in domestic R&D, patenting, and intangible capital are strongly associated with increases in firms’ foreign investment positions. Second, domestic R&D is especially closely related to the extensive margin of multinational activity: firms that accumulate more knowledge at home expand into more foreign subsidiaries and destination markets. Third, the extensive-margin relationship remains positive and precisely estimated in IV Poisson specifications using foreign sectoral R&D, while firm-level FDI estimates are also positive in instrumental-variable and dynamic-panel models. Our findings contribute to the literature on multinational firms, organizational capital, and global production networks by documenting how domestic intangible investment is related to the scale and geography of multinational activity.
