<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

经济学中许多感兴趣的参数依赖于机器学习（ML）生成的结果。这些生成的结果随后被用于标准计量经济学估计，并通常被视为观测到的变量，这种做法威胁到下游推断的有效性。实证相关的案例包括：序列外生性下的动态处理效应、使用替代变量的长期效应、具有ML预测结果的半线性模型、无限制生产函数下的生产率持久性与构造工具变量，以及对混杂变异进行残差化处理的结果。本文开发了一个三步局部稳健GMM框架，用于当因变量由ML生成时进行有效推断。我们构造了一个去偏矩条件，该条件用第一步和第二步影响函数增广识别矩，使得对感兴趣参数的推断在局部上对两个步骤中的正则化和模型选择偏差不敏感。我们建立了所提出估计量的一致性和渐近正态性，并通过蒙特卡洛模拟展示了其有限样本性能。我们还将该方法应用于美国抵押贷款数据，研究在种族盲ML违约风险评分中的条件性黑白差异。该评分总体上能很好地预测后续违约，但评分中嵌入的条件性差异远小于实际严重拖欠中对应的差异；考虑第一步估计还提高了评分差异的朴素样本外估计。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Many parameters of interest in economics depend on outcomes generated by machine learning (ML).These generated outcomes are subsequently used in standard econometric estimation and typically treated as observed, a practice that threatens the validity of downstream inference. Empirically relevant cases include dynamic treatment effects under sequential exogeneity, long-term effects with surrogates, the partially linear model with an ML-predicted outcome, productivity persistence with an unrestricted production function and constructed instruments, and outcomes residualized of confounding variation. This paper develops a three-step locally robust GMM framework for valid inference when the dependent variable is generated by ML. We construct a debiased moment condition that augments the identifying moment with first- and second-step influence functions, making inference on the parameter of interest locally insensitive to regularization and model selection biases at both steps. We establish consistency and asymptotic normality of the proposed estimator and illustrate its finite-sample performance through Monte Carlo simulations. We also apply the method to U.S. mortgage data to study conditional Black--White disparities in a race-blind ML default-risk score. The score predicts subsequent default well overall, but the conditional disparity embedded in the score is much smaller than the corresponding disparity in realized serious delinquency; accounting for first-step estimation also raises the naive out-of-sample estimate of the score disparity.
