<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

我们遵循基于报纸的Baker-Bloom-Davis（BBD）方法，构建了波兰2000年1月至2026年6月的连续月度经济政策不确定性（EPU）指数。该指数使用六份具有深度历史报道的波兰报纸（《共和国报》《市场报》《选举报》《每日法律报》《每日波兰报》和《商业脉搏》），按文章数量进行缩放，在固定的2000—2024年基线期（300个月）内标准化为单位标准差，对每期可获得的报纸取平均值，并进行归一化处理，使基线均值等于100。

由于各报纸进入面板的日期不同，简单的扩展面板平均可能在每个进入点因构成变化而非真实不确定性而出现跳跃。我们采用BBD附录A的拼接程序对此进行修正：从最丰富的六报锚定面板进行向后链式拼接，通过乘性比例因子匹配离散度，并在每个重叠窗口内通过乘性比率匹配水平，从而得到一个在所有进入点上水平连续的指数。

该指数在COVID-19封锁、2022年能源危机和2023年政府更迭期间大幅上升，其最大反应出现在2016年英国脱欧公投之后——这一外部事件对波兰的经济政策环境具有重大影响。在月度环比变化方面，波兰EPU与美国EPU指数（0.26）和全球EPU指数（0.25）呈中度相关。结合基于事件的证据，这些温和的相关性支持了我们的核心发现：EPU Index PL所捕捉的经济政策不确定性主要是国内性的，主要由波兰自身经济政策的发展所驱动，而不仅仅是国际溢出效应，尽管国际冲击只要引发了对波兰自身政策决策的不确定性，就仍然具有相关性。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

We construct a continuous monthly Economic Policy Uncertainty (EPU) index for Poland spanning January 2000 to June 2026, following the newspaper-based Baker-Bloom-Davis (BBD) methodology. The index uses six deep-history Polish newspapers (Rzeczpospolita, Parkiet, Gazeta Wyborcza, Dziennik Gazeta Prawna, Gazeta Polska Codziennie, and Puls Biznesu), scaled by article volume, standardized to unit standard deviation over a fixed 2000-2024 baseline (300 months), averaged across available papers each period, and normalized so the baseline mean equals 100.
  Because newspapers enter the panel at different dates, a naive expanding-panel average can jump at each entry point from a change in composition rather than real uncertainty. We correct for this using the BBD Appendix A splicing procedure: a backward chain-splice from the richest six-paper anchor panel that matches dispersion via a multiplicative scale factor and matches level via a multiplicative ratio over each overlap window, yielding an index that is level-continuous across all entry points.
  The index rises sharply during the COVID-19 lockdown, the 2022 energy crisis, and the 2023 change of government, with its largest reaction following the 2016 Brexit referendum -- an external event with major implications for Poland's economic-policy environment. In month-on-month changes, Poland's EPU is moderately correlated with the US (0.26) and global (0.25) EPU indices. Together with the event-based evidence, these modest correlations support our central finding: EPU Index PL captures economic policy uncertainty that is predominantly domestic, driven mainly by developments in Poland's own economic policy rather than merely international spillovers, though international shocks remain relevant insofar as they create uncertainty about Poland's own policy decisions.
