# No Data Is Not No Risk: Visibility Aware Graph-Based Inference of Business Conduct Risk

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26859v1)

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

商业行为风险的监测受到数据稀疏、分布不均以及可见性偏差的阻碍。已有研究表明，商业行为风险信息与媒体报道会通过供应链、同行及公司结构网络进行传播，但许多企业的事件记录仍不完整。因此，未报告事件可能反映的是覆盖范围有限，而非潜在商业行为风险的缺失。本文探讨企业间关系能否改善对未来已记录行为相关事件的预测，尤其是在先前可见性较低的企业中。我们将该任务构建为企业所有权图上的正-无标签节点分类问题，其中存在记录事件的企业被视为标注正样本，而无记录事件的企业则保持未标注状态。随后，我们提出一种可见性与关系感知的GCNII框架，该框架将关系特定的消息传递与非负正-无标签学习相结合，以处理未标注集中的正样本污染问题。在前瞻性评估中，相较于非图基与简单图基基准方法，所提方法在排序性能上取得了最优结果。研究进一步表明，图基推理在先前无记录事件的企业中仍能保持其预测价值。这些发现证实了企业间关系结构作为补充信息源在扩展风险优先级排序中的价值。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

The monitoring of business conduct risk is hindered by sparse, uneven, and visibility-biased data. Prior studies show that business conduct risk information and media coverage propagate through supply chain, peer, and corporate structure networks, yet incident records remain incomplete for many firms. As a result, the absence of reported events could reflect limited coverage rather than the absence of underlying business conduct risk. This paper examines whether inter-firm relationships can improve the prediction of future recorded conduct related incidents, particularly among firms with limited prior visibility. We formulate the task as Positive--Unlabeled node classification on a corporate ownership graph, where firms with recorded incidents are treated as labeled positives and firms without recorded incidents remain unlabeled. We then propose a visibility- and relation-aware GCNII framework that combines relation specific message passing with non-negative Positive--Unlabeled learning to account for positive contamination in the unlabeled set. In a forward-looking evaluation, the proposed approach achieved the strongest observed ranking performance relative to non-graph- and simple graph-based benchmarks. The results further show that graph-based inference retains its predictive value among firms without prior recorded incidents. These findings demonstrate the value of inter-firm relational structure as a complementary source of information for extending risk prioritization
