# When Is Prosocial Behavior Intuitive? The Moderating Role of Empathic Perspective Taking

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

为什么亲社会行为对某些人来说是直觉性的，而对另一些人来说则是审慎性的？我们考察共情性观点采择是否调节反思性加工与亲社会选择之间的关联。我们开展了一项有652名参与者参加的实验室实验，使用四人分配博弈（Distribution Game）测量亲社会性，该博弈保持总收益不变，并将分配方案从自利型到平等型进行排序。我们将参与者的选择与人际反应指针量表（Interpersonal Reactivity Index）的观点采择分量表、五项认知反思测试（Cognitive Reflection Test）以及分配决策中选择阶段的反应时相结合。观点采择显著调节反应时与亲社会性之间的关联。在观点采择水平较高时，较长的反应时与略低的亲社会性相关；在观点采择水平较低时，该关联为正但不具有统计显著性。认知反思测试表现未出现可比的调节效应。这些结果与以下可能性一致：当他人的结果易于被表征时（高观点采择），亲社会选择可能相对较快地产生，而额外的决策特异性加工可能与对其私人成本的更多关注相关。当他人的结果不那么凸显时（低观点采择），额外的加工反而可能有助于将其纳入考量。这些发现凸显了在解释亲社会行为是否表现为直觉性时，区分与特定决策邻近的加工和领域一般性分析反思的重要性。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Why is prosocial behavior intuitive for some individuals but deliberative for others? We examine whether empathic perspective taking moderates the association between reflective processing and prosocial choice. We run a laboratory experiment with 652 participants, where we measure prosociality using a four-player Distribution Game that holds aggregate payoffs constant and orders allocations from self-regarding to egalitarian. We combine participants' choices with the Perspective Taking subscale of the Interpersonal Reactivity Index, a five-item Cognitive Reflection Test, and choice-stage response time in the allocation decision. Perspective taking significantly moderates the association between response time and prosociality. At high levels of perspective taking, longer response times are associated with modestly lower prosociality; at low levels, the association is positive but not statistically significant. No comparable moderation emerges for Cognitive Reflection Test performance. These results are consistent with the possibility that, when others' outcomes are readily represented (high perspective taking), prosocial choices may arise relatively quickly, while additional decision-specific processing may be associated with greater attention to their private cost. When others' outcomes are less salient (low perspective taking), additional processing may instead help bring them into consideration. The findings highlight the importance of distinguishing processing proximal to the specific decision from domain-general analytic reflection when explaining whether prosocial behavior appears intuitive.
