# Occupational Uniqueness and Wage Growth

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28133v1)

## 一句话结论

> 论文构建任务结构框架，利用1977-2018年DOT与O*NET的196563条任务陈述，度量任务扩散与职业独特性，发现职业独特性下降与工资增长降低相关，且任务增减的工资效应取决于任务在跨职业中的普及程度。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

职业任务内容的变化可能具有不同的经济含义，这取决于所获得或失去的任务是职业特有的还是已在经济中广泛分布的。现有的职业任务变化测量方法在很大程度上忽略了这一区分，将职业视为自包含的任务束，而非考虑任务如何在职业之间分布。我们开发了一个框架来测量职业变化的一个独特维度——超越任务专长或任务内容的维度：任务结构。利用这一追踪跨职业和跨时间任务的新框架，我们将任务数量的变化与职业独特性的变化区分开来，后者定义为某一职业的任务束相对于其他职业的独特性。使用涵盖1977-2018年《职业名称词典》和O*NET的196563条任务陈述的协调数据集，我们测量了任务扩散、职业独特性以及任务增加和移除的不同维度。独特性降低的职业也与较低的工资增长相关，而任务增加和移除的工资关联取决于受影响任务在其他地方被执行的广泛程度。增加高度扩散的任务与较低的工资增长相关，移除职业特有任务也是如此。然而，在经济整体维度上，模式有所不同：新任务的增加与工资增长正相关，而任务的移除则与工资增长负相关。综合来看，这些结果表明，一个职业所执行任务数量的类似变化可能反映经济上不同的过程。因此，纳入任务的跨职业分布为职业变化及其与工资结构的关系提供了更丰富的解释。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Changes in occupational task content may have different economic implications depending on whether the tasks gained or lost are occupation-specific or already widespread across the economy. Existing measures of occupational task change largely abstract from this distinction, treating occupations as self-contained task bundles rather than accounting for how tasks are distributed across occupations. We develop a framework to measure a distinct dimension of occupational change, one that goes beyond task expertise or task content: task structure. Using this new framework that tracks tasks across occupations and over time, we separate changes in task quantity from changes in occupational uniqueness, defined as the distinctiveness of an occupation's task bundle relative to other occupations. Using a harmonized dataset of 196563 task statements from the Dictionary of Occupational Titles and O*NET spanning 1977-2018, we measure task diffusion, occupational uniqueness, and distinct dimensions of task addition and removal. Occupations that become less unique are also associated with lower wage growth, and the wage associations of task additions and removals depend on how widely the affected tasks are performed elsewhere. Adding highly diffused tasks is associated with lower wage growth, as is removing occupation-specific tasks. At the economy-wide dimension, however, the pattern differs: the addition of new tasks is positively associated with wage growth, whereas the removal of tasks is negatively associated with it. Taken together, these results show that similar changes in the number of tasks performed by an occupation can reflect economically distinct processes. Incorporating the cross-occupational distribution of tasks, therefore, provides a richer account of occupational change and its relationship to the wage structure.
