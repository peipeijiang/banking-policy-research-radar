<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

气候政策会改变生产成本，并可能重塑生产的地理格局。我们在一个聚焦模型中研究这一空间渠道：流动性的工业活动对地方吸引力作出反应，集聚效应增强吸引力，而碳政策降低了排放密集型区位的盈利能力。该模型给出了一个明确的碳政策阈值，将空间不稳定与空间稳定区分开来。当排放强度随地方工业密度上升时，即使工业活动总量不变，空间集中也会提高总排放量。将这些动态嵌入一个两辖区政策博弈中，会在区域激励与集体激励之间产生楔子：每个政府看重位于自身辖区内的工业活动，而总福利则内部化了政策对全经济集中度的影响。在可转移效用下，合作可以选择位于阈值稳定一侧的政策，而非合作政策则位于不稳定一侧。一个数值练习说明了这种体制变化。基于NUTS2层面工业排放和总增加值的欧洲区域证据，记录了一个正的排放—活动关联，并识别出衡量迁移和集中效应所需的经验量。该分析将气候合作与生产的空间组织联系起来，并为政策引致的产业迁移建立了一个基准。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Climate policy changes production costs and can reshape the geography of production. We study this spatial channel in a focused model where mobile industrial activity responds to local attractiveness, agglomeration strengthens attractiveness, and carbon policy lowers the profitability of emissions-intensive locations. The model yields an explicit carbon-policy threshold separating spatial instability from stability. When emissions intensity rises with local industrial density, spatial concentration raises aggregate emissions even with unchanged total industrial activity. Embedding these dynamics in a two-jurisdiction policy game creates a wedge between regional and collective incentives: each government values the industrial activity located within its own territory, while aggregate welfare internalizes the effect of policy on economy-wide concentration. With transferable utility, cooperation can select a policy on the stable side of the threshold while the non-cooperative policy lies on the unstable side. A numerical exercise illustrates this regime change. European regional evidence, based on industrial emissions and gross value added at NUTS2 level, documents a positive emissions-activity association and identifies the empirical quantities needed to measure relocation and concentration effects. The analysis links climate cooperation to the spatial organization of production and establishes a benchmark for policy-induced industrial relocation.
