# Localizing Mean Shifts in High-Dimensional Vector Autoregressive Models

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16556v1)

## 一句话结论

> 论文提出三步程序在高维VAR中定位均值变点，先忽略依赖结构获得初步估计，再用稀疏VAR滤波精化，并推导单变点与多变点的定位速率及边界效应修正，最后应用于美国宏观经济指标面板。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

我们研究高维向量自回归（VAR）中均值变点的定位问题，其中自回归动态在各机制间保持共同。我们提出一个三步程序：首先忽略依赖结构，从原始序列中获得初步变点估计；然后利用远离估计变点的观测估计一个共同的稀疏VAR；最后对序列进行滤波以精化变点位置。

对于单个变点，我们证明初步定位速率直接依赖于时间依赖的强度，而滤波则从oracle速率中消除了这一依赖性惩罚。在使用估计滤波器的情况下，精化速率还额外反映了估计VAR动态的误差，以及平稳均值的变化与VAR截距相应变化之间的关系。我们明确刻画了这些速率，并建立了滤波改善定位的条件。由于变点附近的滤波使用了相邻机制的滞后值，它还会在滤波均值中产生短暂的畸变；我们刻画了这种边界效应何时是无害的，并提出了一个转移校正的替代方案。

我们将该程序推广到变点数目未知的情形。使用重叠局部窗口生成候选位置，信息准则去除冗余和虚假候选并估计变点数目，随后利用估计的VAR滤波器对保留的位置进行精化。我们建立了变点数目估计的一致性以及其位置的同时定位速率。数值实验证实了定位增益，并展示了对共同VAR动态中等程度偏离的稳健性。将该方法应用于一大组美国宏观经济指标面板数据，识别出了具有经济可解释性的变点。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

We study the localization of mean changes in high-dimensional vector autoregressions (VARs) when the autoregressive dynamics remain common across regimes. We develop a three-step procedure that first obtains preliminary change-point estimates from the raw series ignoring the dependence structure, then estimates a common sparse VAR using observations away from the estimated changes, and finally filters the series to refine the change point locations.
  For a single change point, we show that the preliminary localization rate depends directly on the strength of temporal dependence, whereas filtering removes this dependence penalty from the oracle rate. With an estimated filter, the refined rate additionally reflects the error in estimating the VAR dynamics and the relation between the change in the stationary mean and the corresponding change in the VAR intercept. We characterize these rates explicitly and establish conditions under which filtering improves localization. Because filtering near a change point uses lags from adjacent regimes, it also creates a short-lived distortion in the filtered mean; we characterize when this boundary effect is innocuous and develop a transition-corrected alternative.
  We extend the procedure to an unknown number of change points. Overlapping local windows are used to generate candidate locations, an information criterion removes redundant and spurious candidates and estimates the number of changes, and the retained locations are subsequently refined using the estimated VAR filter. We establish consistency for the estimated number of changes and simultaneous localization rates for their locations. Numerical experiments corroborate the localization gains and show robustness to moderate departures from common VAR dynamics. An application to a large panel of U.S. macroeconomic indicators identifies economically interpretable changes.
