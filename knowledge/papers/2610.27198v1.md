# DeGroot Dynamics on Multiplex Networks with Directed and Acyclic Interlayer Connectivities

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27198v1)

## 一句话结论

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

在观点动力学模型中，研究者通常考察个体对某一话题的观点如何受到网络中邻居观点的影响。然而，人们对某一话题的观点也受到自身及邻居对相关话题观点的影响。例如，在选举中，选民对各种议题的观点会影响他们对候选人的看法。为了考虑人们对多个不同话题的观点之间的相互影响，我们研究了传统DeGroot共识模型的多层扩展。在我们的多层DeGroot模型中，不同的网络层代表不同的话题，有向的层间边（不允许形成环）编码不同话题之间的关系。在我们的模型中，个体对某一话题的观点既受到其邻居在该话题上观点的影响，也受到自身在相关话题上观点的影响。我们证明了在温和条件下，多层DeGroot模型收敛到一个极限状态。我们还证明了“源层”（代表基本信念）中的强连通性最终会在每一层中产生共识观点。特别地，我们表明源层上的共识可以导致其他层上的共识，即使这些层原本会极化。通过在双层网络上对多层DeGroot模型进行数值模拟，我们考察了（1）不同网络结构和（2）不同类型初始观点数据对观点动力学的影响。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

In models of opinion dynamics, researchers typically examine how an individual's opinion on one topic is influenced by the opinions of their neighbors in a network. However, people's opinions on a topic are also influenced by their opinions and their neighbors' opinions on related topics. In an election, for example, the opinions of a voter on a variety of issues inform their opinions of electoral candidates. To consider the influence between people's opinions on multiple distinct topics, we study a multiplex extension of the traditional DeGroot consensus model. In our multiplex DeGroot model, different network layers represent distinct topics and directed interlayer edges (which are not allowed to form cycles) encode relationships between distinct topics. In our model, an individual's opinion on one topic is influenced both by the opinions of their neighbors on that topic and by their own opinions on related topics. We prove under mild conditions that our multiplex DeGroot model converges to a limit state. We also prove that strong connectedness in ``source layers'' (which represent fundamental beliefs) eventually yields a consensus opinion in each layer. In particular, we show that consensus on source layers can lead to consensus on other layers even when those layers otherwise would be polarized. By numerically simulating our multiplex DeGroot model on a two-layer network, we examine the effects on opinion dynamics of (1) different network structures and (2) different types of initial opinion data.
