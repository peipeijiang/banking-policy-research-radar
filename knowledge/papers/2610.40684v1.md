<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

我们提出一个关于训练具有内生偏见的智能体的简单模型。委托人选择在哪些状态下训练智能体以及在这些状态下规定哪些行动。作为回应，智能体更新其偏见，然后在未训练状态下根据其新偏见采取最优行动，同时在已训练状态下遵循其训练。智能体选择其新偏见，以最小化其在初始偏见和最终偏见下损失的加权平均值。因此，训练可以通过智能体的偏见更新泛化到未训练状态。我们刻画了最优训练的特征，其涉及在已训练状态中的\emph{过度纠正}（over-correction）以及对训练状态的\emph{泛化感知}（generalization-aware，非贪婪）选择。如果只有一小部分状态可以被训练，贪婪状态选择近似最优，但过度纠正仍然至关重要。如果任何偏见函数都是可能的，或者如果委托人针对允许完全对齐可能性的偏见函数进行稳健评估，则训练泛化的价值消失，贪婪训练变为最优。在更悲观的目标下，\emph{纠正不足}（under-correction），甚至完全不训练，可能是最优的。当可能的偏见集合已知且包含完全对齐时，委托人从智能体偏好更加灵活（\emph{可纠正性}，corrigibility）中获益，但在其他情况下可纠正性可能是有害的。应用包括组织和家庭中的社会化，以及AI后训练和递归自我改进。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

We propose a simple model of training an agent with an endogenous bias. A principal chooses in which states to train the agent and what actions to prescribe in these states. In response, the agent updates their bias and then acts optimally given their new bias in untrained states, while following their training in trained states. The agent chooses their new bias to minimize a weighted average of their loss under their initial and final biases. Training can thus generalize to untrained states via the agent's bias updating. We characterize optimal training, which involves \emph{over-correction} in trained states and \emph{generalization-aware} (non-greedy) selection of training states. If only a small share of states can be trained, greedy state selection is approximately optimal, but over-correction remains essential. If any bias function is possible, or if the principal evaluates training robustly over bias functions that admit the possibility of full alignment, the value of training generalization vanishes, and greedy training becomes optimal. With a more pessimistic objective, \emph{under-correction}, or even no training at all, may be optimal. The principal benefits from the agent's preferences being more flexible (\emph{corrigibility}) when the set of possible biases is known and contains full alignment, but otherwise corrigibility can be harmful. Applications include socialization in organizations and families, and AI post-training and recursive self-improvement.
