# On-the-job search in Europe and the U.S.: precautionary vs. job ladder motives

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

尽管雇主间（E2E）的岗位转换已有充分记录，但仅凭这些数据无法揭示流动性的驱动因素：谁在搜寻、为何搜寻，以及搜寻如何转化为岗位转换。利用欧洲央行和纽约联储消费者预期调查的新颖面板数据，我们首次对欧元区十一个国家和美国的在职搜寻（OJS）及E2E转换进行了系统的跨国分析。我们的数据独特地包含了所有劳动者（而不仅仅是搜寻者）的OJS及其动机（预防性动机对应失业预期，职业阶梯动机对应薪酬满意度）的直接度量。我们发现，OJS十分普遍，使得在职劳动者成为搜寻者的主体，并且OJS能强有力地预测E2E转换。动机差异显著：预防性搜寻在欧洲占主导，而职业阶梯动机在美国占主导。OJS具有高度持续性，即使开始新工作后，仍有40%的劳动者继续搜寻。JEL分类号：J64, D84

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

While employer-to-employer (E2E) transitions are by now well-documented, these data alone cannot reveal what drives mobility: who searches, why, and how search translates into transitions. Using novel panel data from the ECB and NY Fed consumer expectations surveys, we provide the first systematic cross-country analysis of on-the job search (OJS) and E2E transitions across eleven euro area countries and the U.S. Our data uniquely include direct measures of OJS and its motives (job loss expectations for precautionary, pay satisfaction for job ladder) for all workers, not just searchers. We find that OJS is widespread, making employed workers the majority of searchers, and it strongly predicts E2E transitions. Motives differ dramatically: precautionary search dominates in Europe, while the job ladder motive dominates in the U.S. OJS is highly persistent, with 40% continuing to search even after starting a new job. JEL Classification: J64, D84
