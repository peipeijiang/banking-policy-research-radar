<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Using a unique firm-level survey from the Bureau of Economic Research in South Africa covering a panel of 1444 South African firms surveyed at quarterly frequency between 2000 and 2023, we examine how global oil news shocks shape firms’ inflation and wage expectations. We use exogenous oil supply news shocks following Känzig (2021) and embed them in a fixed-effects panel regression that controls for domestic macroeconomic and financial conditions through estimated factors. A one standard deviation increase in oil news shock predicts a rise in firm-level inflation expectations by 0.22 percentage points at the one-year horizon and 0.16 at two years, and wage expectations by 0.14 percentage points. The responses are largely robust to alternative shock identifications, to the number of estimated factors, and to the inclusion of a lagged dependent variable. Using the decomposition of oil shocks following Baumeister and Hamilton (2019), we show that the inflation response is driven by the supply disturbances. Inflation expectations at sectoral levels responds uniformly across the economy, while wage responses are stronger in sectors with active collective bargaining. The findings have direct implications for inflation-targeting policy in emerging economies, particularly in light of South Africa’s move to a lower target.
