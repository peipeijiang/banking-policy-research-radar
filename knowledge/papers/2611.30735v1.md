<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

保护性干预通常以其预防伤害的能力来评估。然而，保护还提出了一个不同的问题：谁承担适应暴力的负担？我利用美国的四项随机干预研究了这种保护性调整的分配。永久住房援助的提供使与基线伴侣分离并拥有稳定独立住房的比例增加了12.52个百分点。被分配至积极的警察响应方案减少了观察到的仅受害者离开的情况，并增加了仅嫌疑人离开的情况。被分配至紧急保护令增加了有记录的要求嫌疑人离开的指令。这些发现揭示了同一分配问题的两个边际：住房援助扩大了受害者建立独立住所的能力，而执法则将迁移义务转向嫌疑人。因此，保护性政策不仅塑造了获得安全的机会，还塑造了实现安全所需成本的分配。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Protective interventions are typically evaluated by their ability to prevent harm. Yet protection also raises a distinct question: who bears the burden of adjusting to violence? I examine this allocation of protective adjustment using four randomized interventions in the United States. An offer of permanent housing assistance increases separation from a baseline partner combined with stable independent housing by 12.52 percentage points. Assignment to active police-response protocols reduces observed victim-only departures and increases suspect-only departures. Assignment to an emergency protection order increases documented directions for suspects to leave. These findings reveal two margins of the same allocation problem: housing assistance expands victims' ability to establish an independent residence, while enforcement shifts displacement obligations toward suspects. Protective policies thus shape not only the opportunities for safety, but also the distribution of the costs required to achieve it.

</details>
