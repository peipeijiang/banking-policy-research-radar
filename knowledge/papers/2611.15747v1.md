# Huge If True: Learning from the Size of the Surprise

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

对盈利意外的反应取决于一个未知的乘数，该乘数将盈利映射为价值。然而，投资者从同一个数字中同时获知消息和乘数。较大的意外可能表明较小的乘数，而微小的意外则可能表明较大的乘数。我们建模了乘数不确定性的增加如何在平均意义上减弱反应，但使反应在零意外附近变得更加陡峭。在一项激励实验中，我们发现更大的处理约束降低了平均敏感性，但提高了零附近的敏感性。我们用现实世界的盈利公告证实了这一点：估值不确定性较高（作为乘数不确定性的代理变量）的股票平均反应较小，但在零附近反应更为陡峭。

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Responses to earnings surprises depend on an unknown multiplier mapping earnings to value. Investors, however, learn about the news and the multiplier from the same number. Large surprises may indicate small multipliers and tiny surprises large ones. We model how greater uncertainty about the multiplier attenuates responses on average but makes them steeper near zero surprises. In an incentivized experiment, we find that greater processing constraints lower average sensitivity but raise it near zero. We confirm this with real-world earnings announcements: stocks with higher valuation uncertainty, our proxy for multiplier uncertainty, respond less on average but more steeply near zero.

</details>
