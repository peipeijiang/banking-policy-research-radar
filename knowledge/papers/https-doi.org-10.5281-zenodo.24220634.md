# The Correction Freeze Problem in a New Domain: Blind and Exploratory Tests on Air Quality

[查看原文](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24220634)

## 一句话结论

> 论文将'Correction Freeze Problem'的信息极限路线（Route 4）原样应用于美国PM2.5空气质量与公众环境信念，发现公众感知精度未超过数据信息上限，属环境感知行为研究，与银行、货币、财政三领域无关。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

校正冻结问题（Correction Freeze Problem, CFP）的路径4，即信息极限，指出任何观察者检测缓慢变化的可靠性都无法超越数据中信息所允许的程度。此前两项严格的检验——分别针对通胀预期和气候信念——发现人们始终低于这一极限。本研究追问路径4能否在未经调整的情况下迁移至一个新领域。研究将相同的预测原封不动地应用于美国的细颗粒物（PM2.5），这是作者此前从未研究过的领域。路径4的检验在性质上属于预注册：预测在数据采集之前即已注册并发表，且应用时未作任何更改。EPA数据显示，2000年至2025年间年均中位水平下降了45%。以本文所用的噪声度量，该变化给出的d值为[数据缺失]，单次观测的最佳可能准确度介于0.67至0.81之间。盖洛普2026年环境民意调查发现，66%的美国成年人认为环境正在恶化，27%认为正在改善。现有准确度度量均未超过该极限。因此，路径4再次经受住了一项严峻挑战：一个作者此前不了解的领域，且没有任何可供调整的参数。至此，路径4已通过三项独立检验：两项关于通胀和气候的严格检验，以及这项关于空气质量的盲测跨领域检验。我们谨慎地报告这一结果。现有度量均为间接度量，且该检验本身无法将路径4与竞争性解释区分开来，因为调查询问的是笼统的“环境”状况，数据为全国层面，且信念随党派立场强烈波动。研究还以探索性方式考察了另外两条路径。对于路径2，一项针对2015年至2025年日度数据的探索性检验（规则在数据到达之前即已确定）发现，一旦空气质量变得令人警觉，人们查看空气质量的频率远高于此前，这与路径2的预测一致。然而，查看行为在阈值以下并未保持为零，因此严格形式未获支持，结论为不确定：路径2未被证伪。对于路径3，信念随总统党派更替而变化，而非随空气状况变化，这既符合路径3，也符合普通的党派性。研究最后提出了一项县级层面的设计，以区分上述各种解释。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Route 4 of the Correction Freeze Problem (CFP), the information limit, says that no observer can detect a slow change more reliably than the information in the data allows. Two earlier severe tests, on inflation expectations and climate belief, found that people stayed below this limit. This study asks whether Route 4 transfers to a new domain without adjustment. It applies the same predictions, unchanged, to fine particulate matter () in the United States, a domain the authors had never studied. The Route 4 test is preregistered by nature: the predictions were registered and published before the data were seen, and applied without change. EPA data show that the median annual level fell by 45% between 2000 and 2025. With the noise measure used here, this change gives , and the best possible accuracy for a single observation lies between 0.67 and 0.81. Gallup's 2026 Environment Poll finds that 66% of U.S. adults say the environment is getting worse and 27% say it is getting better. No available measure of accuracy exceeds the limit. Route 4 has therefore survived another difficult challenge: a domain the authors did not know, with no parameter that could be adjusted. It has now survived three independent tests: two severe tests on inflation and climate, and this blind out-of-domain test on air quality. We report this result with caution. The available measures are indirect, and the test does not, on its own, separate Route 4 from rival explanations, because the survey asks about the environment in general, the data are national, and beliefs move strongly with party. Two further routes were examined in an exploratory way. For Route 2, an exploratory test of daily data from 2015 to 2025, with rules fixed before the data arrived, finds that people check air quality far more once it becomes alarming, as Route 2 predicts. Checking does not, however, stay at zero below the threshold, so the strict form is not supported, and the verdict is inconclusive: Route 2 is not refuted. For Route 3, belief moved with changes of presidential party rather than with the air, which fits Route 3 but also ordinary partisanship. The study closes with a county-level design that can separate these accounts.
