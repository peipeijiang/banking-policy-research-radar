<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

传统风险度量通常依赖单一概率模型，可能忽略来自其他模型的信息，导致风险评估出现偏差。这一局限性无法仅通过风险管理中的资产分散化来解决。本文提出了一种多元化风险感知（Diversified-Risk-Perception，DRP）风险度量框架，将资产分散化与风险模型多元化相结合，以提供更全面的市场风险评估，同时降低对任何单一模型的依赖。首先，我们引入一种灵活的指数聚合函数来评估投资组合的风险，该函数考虑了所有损失分量的贡献，同时更加重视大额损失。其次，我们在风险度量中纳入多个风险模型，并基于它们对风险评估的感知来优化其权重。作为一个具体示例，我们引入了DRP期望损失（DRP expected shortfall，DRP-ES），建立了其理论性质，并推导了量化其对模型参数敏感性的界。在美国、中国A股和韩国股票市场的实证实验表明，基于DRP-ES的交易策略在不同时期均比市场基准实现了更高的夏普比率和更低的最大回撤。与基于单一概率模型的资产分散化策略相比，DRP策略在不同市场和时期表现出更稳定和稳健的绩效。这些发现表明，DRP框架能够适应不断变化的市场条件，并提高投资组合风险管理的稳定性和有效性。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Traditional risk measures generally rely on a single probability model, potentially overlooking information from alternative models and leading to biased risk assessments. This limitation cannot be addressed by asset diversification alone in risk management. In this paper, we propose a Diversified-Risk-Perception (DRP) risk measure framework that integrates asset diversification and risk model diversification to provide a more comprehensive assessment of market risk while reducing reliance on any single model. First, we introduce a flexible exponential aggregation function to evaluate the risk of a portfolio, which accounts for the contributions of all loss components while placing greater emphasis on large losses. Second, we incorporate multiple risk models in the risk measure and optimize their weights based on their perceptions on the risk assessments. As a specific example, we introduce DRP expected shortfall (DRP-ES), establish its theoretical properties, and derive bounds quantifying its sensitivity to model parameters. Empirical experiments in the U.S., Chinese A-share, and South Korean stock markets show that trading strategies based on the DRP-ES achieve higher Sharpe ratios and lower maximum drawdowns than market benchmarks across different periods. Compared with asset diversification strategies based on individual probability models, the DRP strategy exhibits more stable and robust performance across different markets and periods. These findings suggest that the DRP framework can adapt to changing market conditions and improve the stability and effectiveness of portfolio risk management.
