# Global Trends and Heterogeneity in Modified Adjusted Net Savings

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12987v1)

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

修正后的调整净储蓄（Modified adjusted net savings, MANS）是一种衍生出的弱可持续性指标，通过从调整净储蓄（adjusted net savings, ANS）中减去教育支出得到。由于ANS等于国民净储蓄加教育支出减去自然资源损耗和二氧化碳损害，MANS将储蓄和损耗成分分离出来，在教育支出否则将主导跨国比较时具有实用价值。本研究记录了1990—2021年间排除颗粒物排放损害的MANS的全球分布、长期趋势和跨国异质性。核算基础为世界银行世界发展指标序列NY.ADJ.SVNX.CD，MANS通过从该序列中减去教育支出构建。我们综合运用全球地图、总量时间序列、国家层面热力图、分布统计和收入组比较。结果表明，全球MANS总量已大幅增长，但其占名义GDP比重的演变更为波动且各国之间不均衡。人均MANS和全球MANS总量仍集中于高收入经济体和大型经济体，而低收入和中低收入组则表现出持续较低且更为波动的结果。国家排名具有高度持续性，一些人口大国表现出与自然资源依赖和结构转型相关的显著波动。本研究为监测可持续发展提供了可复现的描述性基准，并强调需要结合收入组和国别背景以当期美元解读MANS。研究结论对宏观经济政策、自然资本核算以及面向可持续性的投资策略设计具有参考意义。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Modified adjusted net savings is a derived weak sustainability indicator obtained by subtracting education expenditure from adjusted net savings (ANS). Because ANS equals net national savings plus education expenditure minus natural-resource depletion and carbon dioxide damage, MANS isolates the savings and depletion components and is useful when education expenditure would otherwise dominate cross-country comparisons. This study documents the global distribution, long-run trends, and cross-country heterogeneity of MANS excluding particulate emission damage over 1990-2021. The accounting base is the World Bank World Development Indicators series NY.ADJ.SVNX.CD, and MANS is constructed by subtracting education expenditure from this series. We combine global maps, aggregate time series, country-level heatmaps, distributional statistics, and income group comparisons. The results show that global MANS has increased substantially in aggregate, but its evolution as a share of nominal GDP has been more volatile and uneven across countries. Per capita MANS and global MANS totals remain concentrated in high-income and large economies, whereas low-income and lower-middle-income groups display persistently lower and more volatile outcomes. Country rankings are highly persistent, and some populous countries exhibit pronounced fluctuations associated with natural-resource dependence and structural change. The study provides a reproducible descriptive benchmark for monitoring sustainable development and highlights the need to interpret MANS in current US dollars alongside income-group and country-specific contexts. The findings are relevant for macroeconomic policy, natural-capital accounting, and the design of sustainability-oriented investment strategies.
