<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

数据中心快速扩张引发了人们对电力可负担性的担忧，然而为这些大型新增负荷提供服务的成本如何传导至居民用户，目前仍缺乏充分认识。本文通过将电力系统扩展、批发电力市场、输电网加固和零售成本分摊相关联，量化了得克萨斯州371个已公布的数据中心项目（到2030年总计38.9 GW）对居民用电成本的潜在影响。我们发现，在一个代表性情景中，满足这一需求会使模型中的居民用电成本增加1.64美分/千瓦时，其中68%来自更高的批发电价，32%来自受监管的输电费用。允许在数据中心附近新建燃气发电可将2023年居民成本增幅降至0.92美分/千瓦时，而将数据中心排除在输电成本分摊基数之外则会使其升至1.87美分/千瓦时。这些发现表明，数据中心增长带来的居民成本负担主要由批发电价上涨驱动，而非直接的输电投资成本，并且在很大程度上取决于发电选址、基础设施规划和成本分摊方式。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Rapid data-center expansion is raising concerns about electricity affordability, yet how the costs of serving these large new loads reach residential customers remains poorly understood. Here, we quantify the potential impact of 371 announced Texas data-center projects, totaling 38.9 GW by 2030, on residential electricity costs by linking power system expansion, wholesale electricity markets, transmission reinforcement, and retail cost allocation. We find that accommodating this demand increases modeled residential electricity costs in one representative case by 1.64 cents/kWh, with 68% arising from higher wholesale electricity prices and 32% from regulated transmission charges. Allowing new gas generation near data centers reduces the 2023 residential cost increase to 0.92 cents/kWh, whereas excluding data centers from the transmission cost-sharing base raises it to 1.87 cents/kWh. These findings reveal that the residential cost burden of data-center growth is driven primarily by wholesale electricity price increases, rather than direct transmission investment costs, and depends critically on generation siting, infrastructure planning, and cost allocation.
