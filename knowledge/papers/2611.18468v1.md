<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

投票权、群体成员资格以及可接受话语的边界等问题都涉及一个递归的自我稳定性问题，其中投票者、群体成员和可接受的评论者往往是自身包容与排斥规则的最终裁决者。为了理解意识形态或议题多样性如何影响这种包容与排斥的稳定性，我们引入了特许选举（franchise elections），即群体中的投票成员通过投票来改变选民范围。特别地，当行动者被置于意识形态或议题空间中时，一个聚合行动者观点的社会选择函数会诱导行动者对群体构成产生偏好，即行动者偏好那些能改善其社会选择函数结果的群体。我们证明，当意识形态空间是二维的时，这些特许选举能够产生复杂的行为，且这种复杂性对任何社会选择函数都存在。利用投票理论中的Yolk社会选择函数，我们说明特许选举可以预测：试图引入一条垂直于现有左右光谱的新意识形态轴线的行动者，除非与光谱的任一侧结盟，否则面临被剥夺权利的风险；且特许选举可以以任意周期循环。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

The questions of voting rights, group membership, and the boundaries of acceptable discourse all involve a recursive, self-stability issue, wherein the voters, group members, and acceptable commentators are often the ultimate arbiters of their own rules for inclusion and exclusion. In order to understand how ideological or issue diversity may impact the stability of such inclusion and exclusion, we introduce franchise elections, where voting members of a group vote to change the electorate. In particular, when agents are positioned inside an ideological or issue space, a social choice function that aggregates agent viewpoints induces a preference for agents over group composition, where agents prefer groups that improve the social choice function outcome for them. We demonstrate that when ideological space is two-dimensional, these franchise elections can generate complex behavior and that this complexity exists for any social choice function. Using the Yolk social choice function from voting theory, we illustrate that franchise elections can predict that agents attempting to introduce a new ideological axis perpendicular to an existing left-right spectrum risk disenfranchisement unless they ally with either side of the spectrum, and that franchise elections can cycle with any period.
