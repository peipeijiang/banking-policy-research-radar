# Import dependence and per capita production are main determinants of economies' food supply robustness under production shocks

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

主要生产经济体的粮食供应冲击可通过贸易网络传播，并在全球粮食系统中产生不均衡的影响。本研究考察了在全球主粮系统中，主要生产者面临生产冲击时各经济体的粮食供应稳健性。利用2023年小麦、水稻、玉米和大豆的生产、储备及双边贸易数据，我们构建了一个基于卡路里的全球粮食供应网络。我们扩展了一个动态冲击传播框架，并模拟了对主要生产经济体的生产冲击，追踪供应损失的传播路径。结果表明，不同作物和经济体之间的稳健性存在显著异质性。小麦的整体稳健性最高，而大豆的稳健性最低。稳健性较高的经济体要么相对游离于贸易网络之外，要么在积极参与贸易的同时保持强劲且稳定的国内生产，而稳健性较低的经济体则主要以高进口依赖为特征。进口依赖度和人均产量成为稳健性最重要的决定因素。基于这些发现，我们设计了两种针对高进口依赖经济体的反事实政策：增加储备可用性和调整贸易联系。反事实实验表明，这两种政策仅带来适度的整体改善，且效果在不同作物间存在显著差异。两种政策均能提高总体系统和小麦的稳健性，贸易调整对水稻更为有效，而对玉米和大豆的改善效果有限甚至为负。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Food supply shocks in major producing economies can propagate through trade networks and generate uneven impacts across the global food system. This study examines the robustness of economies' food supply under production shocks to major producers in the global staple food system. Using 2023 production, reserve, and bilateral trade data for wheat, rice, maize, and soybean, we construct a calorie-based global food supply network across economies. We extend a dynamic shock propagation framework and then simulate production shocks to major producing economies, tracing how supply losses propagate. The results show substantial heterogeneity in robustness across crops and economies. Wheat exhibits the highest overall robustness, whereas soybean shows the lowest. Economies with high robustness tend to be either relatively isolated from the trade network or actively engaged in trade while maintaining strong and stable domestic production, whereas low-robustness economies are predominantly those with high import dependence. Import dependence and per capita production emerge as the most important determinants of robustness. Based on these findings, we design two counterfactual policies targeting highly import-dependent economies: increasing reserve availability and adjusting trade linkages. Counterfactual experiments show that the two policies yield only modest overall improvements, with effects varying substantially across crops. Both policies improve robustness in the aggregated system and wheat, trade adjustment is more effective for rice, and it brings limited or even negative effects for maize and soybean.
