<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

自Waldman（1984）以来，人们已普遍认识到，劳动力市场中晋升的一个重要角色在于晋升充当了高能力劳动者的信号。本文探讨了一种常见且日益普遍的做法——职位头衔通胀（job title inflation），它发挥信号干扰作用，从而降低晋升的信号价值。我们首先构建一个两期模型，证明职位头衔通胀如何在均衡中出现并增加社会福利，原因有二：一是劳动者直接看重听起来令人印象深刻的职位头衔；二是该做法减少了因信号传递导致的岗位配置低效率。随后，我们在模型中引入年轻劳动者关于人力资本积累的投资选择，并证明在这种情况下，职位头衔通胀可能降低而非增加福利。也就是说，当晋升及相关的工资增长作为人力资本投资的重要激励时，职位头衔通胀的使用可能持续存在，即使该做法降低了整体效率和劳动者的预期效用。接着我们考虑三个扩展情形：（i）以劳动者流动为特征的环境；（ii）以工资透明度法律为特征的环境；（iii）以劳动者可见性异质性为特征的环境。此外，除了展示职位头衔通胀如何作为一种可能提高或降低福利的均衡现象出现之外，我们还讨论了我们的理论分析在多大程度上与现实劳动力市场中职位头衔通胀使用的证据相吻合。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Starting with Waldman (1984), it has become widely understood that an important role of promotions in labor markets is that promotions serve as signals of high worker ability. In this paper we explore the common and growing practice called job title inflation which serves a signal jamming role that reduces the signaling value of promotions. We first explore a two-period model and show how job title inflation can arise in equilibrium and increase social welfare both because workers directly value impressive sounding job titles and because the practice reduces job assignment inefficiencies due to signaling. We then introduce into the model an investment choice for young workers concerning human capital accumulation and show that job title inflation can decrease rather than increase welfare in that case. That is, when promotions and the associated wage increases serve as an important incentive for investing in human capital, the use of job title inflation can persist even though the practice lowers both overall efficiency and expected worker utility. We then consider three extensions: (i) a setting characterized by worker turnover; (ii) a setting characterized by a wage transparency law; and (iii) a setting characterized by heterogeneous worker visibility. Also, in addition to showing how job title inflation can arise as an equilibrium phenomenon that can either raise or lower welfare, we discuss the extent to which our theoretical analysis matches evidence concerning the use of job title inflation in real-world labor markets.
