# Estimating Rail Commuter Preferences under Endogenous Crowding: A Population Game Approach to Train Choice in Tokyo

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02592v1)

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

我们利用东京一条通勤铁路线上观测到的列车选择，估计通勤者对时刻表延误、旅行时间和拥挤的偏好。由于拥挤本身是这些选择的结果，我们将列车选择建模为一个博弈，其中列车选择与拥挤是联合决定的，并利用该博弈的闭式平稳分布通过最大似然法估计偏好。我们使用日本2015年大都市区公共交通调查的个体层面数据，并与列车时刻表相匹配，根据报告的工作开始时间构建期望到达时间，而非从观测到的到达时间推断。我们发现时刻表延误成本相对于拥挤成本较大。早到和晚到一分钟的时刻表延误分别相当于0.39分钟和0.49分钟的车内旅行时间，而在100%拥挤率下旅行一分钟所增加的等效成本为0.04分钟。然而，估计的拥挤成本对期望到达时间的设定较为敏感。这些结果表明，调度约束对于评估减少高峰时段拥挤的政策具有核心意义。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

We estimate commuters' preferences over schedule delay, travel time, and crowding from observed train choices on a commuter rail line in Tokyo. Because crowding is itself an outcome of these choices, we model train choice as a game in which train choices and crowding are jointly determined, and estimate the preferences by maximum likelihood using the closed-form stationary distribution of the game. Using individual-level data from Japan's 2015 Public Transport Survey for Metropolitan Areas matched with train timetables, we construct desired arrival times from reported work start times rather than inferring them from observed arrival times. We find that schedule delay costs are large relative to crowding costs. One minute of schedule delay early and late corresponds to 0.39 and 0.49 minutes of in-vehicle travel time, respectively, whereas one minute of travel at a crowding rate of 100% adds a cost equivalent to 0.04 minutes. The estimated crowding cost is, however, sensitive to the specification of desired arrival times. These results suggest that scheduling constraints are central to evaluating policies to reduce peak-hour crowding.
