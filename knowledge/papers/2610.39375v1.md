<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

本文考察工作在提供意义、目的、社群及其他非货币性收益方面的作用。基于一项原创的跨国调查——覆盖英国、美国、丹麦和印度共8，234名劳动年龄成年人——我研究了激励人们工作的因素，并着眼于未来人工智能经济转型的背景。我发现，81%的受访者表示对自己的工作感到满意，四个国家中多数受访者都受到就业中非货币性因素的强烈激励。运用原创的“工作价值观”（Work Values）指数，我表明三分之一的在职受访者受到工作的内在激励——即因其自身价值而重视自己的角色。近四分之一的人受到就业中社会和社群相关方面的驱动，17%受社会地位驱动，略超过四分之一的人受工作的交易性财务收益驱动。尽管多数人如果在获得当前薪资作为保障收入的条件下会离开自己的岗位，但五分之一的劳动者即使获得高达其薪资120%的保障收入仍会选择留下。内在激励型劳动者在任何替代水平下拒绝基本收入的可能性都显著更高。那些因非货币性原因而依附于自己工作的群体，如果获得保障收入替代但无法再从事原有角色，也更可能去从事志愿服务或类似工作的活动。至关重要的是，这些非货币性工作动机在收入分布上以及白领与蓝领劳动者之间均匀分布。我认为，这些发现对管理人工智能转型、理解人们对人工智能的态度以及解读政策偏好具有重要意义。保障基本收入或许确实是应对未来人工智能劳动力冲击的必要手段，但它最终可能无法补偿劳动者因失去从工作中获得的非货币性收益而遭受的损失。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

This paper investigates the role of work in providing meaning, purpose, community, and other non-monetary benefits. Using an original cross-national survey of 8,234 working-age adults across the UK, US, Denmark, and India, I study the factors that motivate people in their work, with an eye toward the future AI economic transformation. I find that 81% of respondents report being satisfied with their job, and a majority across all four countries are strongly motivated by the non-monetary components of their employment. Using an original "Work Values" Index, I show that one third of working respondents are motivated intrinsically by their job--valuing their role for its own sake. Nearly one quarter are driven by social and community-related aspects of their employment, 17% by social status, and just over one quarter by the transactional financial benefits of their job. Although a majority of people would leave their role if offered their current salary as a guaranteed income, one fifth of workers would stay even if offered up to 120% of their salary. Intrinsically motivated workers were disproportionately likely to refuse a basic income at any substitution level. Groups attached to their job for non-monetary reasons were also more likely to pursue volunteering or work-like activities if they received guaranteed income substitution but could no longer perform their role. Crucially, these non-monetary work motivations are evenly distributed across the income distribution and between white- and blue-collar workers. I argue that these findings have implications for managing the AI transition, understanding attitudes to AI, and making sense of policy preferences. Guaranteeing a basic income may indeed be necessary for managing any future AI labor shock, yet it could ultimately fail to compensate workers for any loss of the non-monetary benefits they derive from work.
