<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

双边市场平台常使用随机实验来评估新功能，这引出了一个问题：应当对顾客还是对房源进行随机化。对较小（\emph{短}）一侧进行随机化可以减少因竞争较弱而产生的干扰偏差，而对较大（\emph{长}）一侧进行随机化则提供更大的名义样本，可能降低方差。表面上，这些相互竞争的考量似乎表明在选择随机化哪一侧时存在偏差-方差权衡。我们在一个具有先到先得（FCFS）预订和异质性顾客偏好的双边市场中研究这一权衡，考察全局平均处理效应的差值均值估计量的偏差和方差。

我们的研究发现表明，并非存在偏差-方差权衡，事实上短侧随机化在{\em 两个}维度上通常都更可取。我们在市场失衡加剧的渐近情形下证明了这些结果。我们建立了以下条件：(1) 短侧随机化消除一阶干扰偏差，而长侧随机化则保留该偏差；(2) 短侧随机化所达到的方差率的渐近阶数不大于长侧随机化。关键洞察在于，尽管长侧包含更多参与者，但匹配数量受限于短侧的人口规模，这限制了随机化更大样本所显现的方差优势。我们的条件适用于异质性偏好和处理效应，并且为常见的顾客选择logit模型所满足。总之，这些发现支持在短侧进行随机化，尽管长侧可获得更大的样本量。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Two-sided marketplace platforms often use randomized experiments to evaluate new features, raising the question of whether to randomize customers or listings. Randomizing the smaller (\emph{short}) side can reduce interference bias due to weaker competition, while randomizing the larger (\emph{long}) side provides a larger nominal sample, potentially reducing variance. On the surface, these competing considerations suggest a bias-variance trade-off in choosing which side to randomize. We investigate this trade-off in a two-sided marketplace with first-come, first-served (FCFS) booking and heterogeneous customer preferences, studying the bias and variance of difference-in-means estimators of the global average treatment effect.
  Our findings suggest that rather than a bias-variance tradeoff, in fact short-side randomization is generally preferable on {\em both} dimensions. We prove these results in an asymptotic regime where market imbalance increases. We establish conditions under which (1) short-side randomization eliminates first-order interference bias, while long-side randomization retains it; and (2) short-side randomization also achieves a variance rate of no larger asymptotic order than long-side randomization. The key insight is that although the long side contains more participants, the number of matches is constrained by the population on the short side, limiting the apparent variance advantage of randomizing a larger sample. Our conditions accommodate heterogeneous preferences and treatment effects and are satisfied by familiar logit models of customer choice. Together, these findings support randomizing on the short side, despite the larger sample size available on the long side.
