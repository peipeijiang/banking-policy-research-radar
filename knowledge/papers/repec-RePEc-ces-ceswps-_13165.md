# Closing Windows: Fertility Decline and the Timing of Preventive War

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

一个相对军事力量正在增长的国家可能会推迟发动攻击，以提高其获胜的机会。生育率下降可以通过减少未来相对力量的增益来削弱这一动机。我研究了一个挑战者在谈判失败后选择是否以及何时发动攻击的问题。军事力量由技术和人力相结合，过去的出生人口在一个有限的时间范围内为未来征兵提供了一个相对可预测的基线。在初始相对力量固定的情况下，我证明，自身队列规模下降更快可能使立即攻击成为最优选择，即使获胜的机会本会继续改善。在向立即攻击过渡的临界点附近，自身下降更快会提前发动攻击，而防御方下降更快则会推迟攻击，前提是各自国家部分依赖人力。在相同条件下，减少挑战者对人力依赖的技术会推迟攻击。当两国技术密集程度相同时，队列下降率的共同上升不会改变攻击时机。这些结果关注的是战争选项的时机，而非人口老龄化对战争发起的净效应。其机制是绿色悖论（green paradox）的人口学类比。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

A state whose relative military power is growing may postpone an attack to improve its chances of victory. Fertility decline can weaken this incentive by reducing the future gains in relative power. I study a challenger choosing whether and when to attack after bargaining has failed. Military power combines technology and manpower, with past births providing a comparatively predictable baseline for future recruitment over a finite horizon. Holding initial relative power fixed, I show that faster own cohort decline can make immediate attack optimal even though the chances of victory would continue to improve. Near the transition to immediate attack, faster own decline advances attack and faster defender decline delays it, provided the respective country depends partly on manpower. Technology that reduces the challenger's reliance on manpower delays attack under the same condition. A common increase in cohort-decline rates leaves timing unchanged when both countries are equally technology-intensive. The results concern the timing of a war option rather than the net effect of ageing on war initiation. The mechanism is a demographic analogue of the green paradox.

</details>
