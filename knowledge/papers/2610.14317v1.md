<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

怀孕在婴儿照护开始之前就可能中断学业。我们使用《1997年全国青年纵向调查》的月度历史数据，研究怀孕期间和分娩后的学业中断情况，以及学生随后是否重返学校或获得文凭或GED。我们将受孕与持续入学中断进行联合建模，允许持续存在的未观测差异同时影响这两个过程。在事件时序识别假设下，怀孕使每月中断率提高到无怀孕情况下的近四倍。中断率在分娩前升高，并在分娩后持续保持较高水平。在中断之后，已经经历过怀孕的学生在两年内重返学校并获得文凭或GED的可能性更低。这些发现表明，学业中断在怀孕期间就已经存在，早于婴儿照护的额外需求开始之前。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Pregnancy can disrupt schooling before infant care begins. We use monthly histories from the National Longitudinal Survey of Youth 1997 to study school interruptions during pregnancy and after childbirth and whether students later return to school or earn a diploma or GED. We jointly model conception and sustained interruptions in enrollment, allowing persistent unobserved differences to affect both processes. Under the timing-of-events identifying assumptions, pregnancy raises the monthly interruption rate to nearly four times the rate without pregnancy. Interruption rates are elevated before delivery and remain elevated afterward. After an interruption, students already exposed to pregnancy return to school and earn a diploma or GED less often within two years. These findings show that schooling disruption is already present during pregnancy, before the additional demands of infant care begin.
