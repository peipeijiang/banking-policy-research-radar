# Going Beyond the In-/Out-Group Dichotomy: Investigating Altruism towards Middle-Groups

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09046v1)

## 一句话结论

> 通过实验研究中间群体是否受到不同于内群体和外群体的对待，发现只有在共享两个身份标记且重复暴露时才会出现层级化利他行为。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

群体内偏袒与群体外敌意已被充分记录，但现实世界中的群体情境很少符合简单的二元划分。通常，一个“中间群体”会与内群体共享某些身份标记，但并不完全属于该群体。人们会如何对待这类中间群体？我们通过一个正式的身份标记框架以及一项多实验室在线实验（N = 376）来回答这一问题，该实验采用一种不受声誉效应和需求特征影响的私人分配任务。我们发现，中间群体可能受到中立对待，即既不同于内群体偏袒，也不同于外群体敌意，但这仅在特定条件下成立。当中间群体与内群体共享两个身份标记（包括大学归属）时，会出现完整的层级结构：内群体受到偏袒，中间群体按分配规则被对待，而外群体则承担损失。然而，当中间群体仅共享一个标记时，无论共享的是哪个标记，该群体都会受到与外群体无差别的对待。此外，差异化行为仅在参与者在第二阶段重新接触实验指令和群体构成之后才会出现，而非在各轮次中逐渐形成。这些发现表明，参与者能够超越内群体/外群体的二元分类进行感知，但这种感知既需要足够的身份重叠，也需要对群体情境的重复接触。我们的身份标记框架为在更复杂情境中系统研究群体关系提供了一种工具。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

In-group favouritism and out-group hostility are well-documented, but real-world group settings rarely fit a simple dichotomy. Often, a "middle-group" shares some identity markers with the in-group without fully belonging to it. How do people treat such intermediate groups? We address this question using a formal identity marker framework and a multi-lab online experiment (N = 376) with a private allocation task immune to reputation effects and demand characteristics. We find that a middle-group can be treated neutrally, i.e., distinct from both in-group favouritism and out-group hostility, but only under specific conditions. When the middle-group shares two identity markers with the in-group, including university affiliation, a complete hierarchy emerges: the in-group is favoured, the middle-group is treated according to the allocation rule, and the out-group bears the loss. However, when the middle-group shares only one marker, it is treated indistinguishably from the out-group, regardless of which marker is shared. Furthermore, differentiated behaviour evolves only after participants have re-encountered the experimental instructions and group constellation in a second phase, not gradually across rounds. These findings demonstrate that participants can perceive beyond binary in-/out-group categorisation, but such perception requires both sufficient identity overlap and repeated exposure to the group setting. Our identity marker framework provides a tool for systematically studying group relations in more complex settings.
