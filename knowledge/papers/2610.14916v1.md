<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

外来入侵物种（Invasive Alien Species, IAS）造成大量生态和经济损害，这些损害往往跨越政治边界，从而为协调管理创造了强烈激励。我们构建了一个关于IAS控制的两区域生态-经济模型，该模型将预防和根除措施与以逻辑斯谛增长和Allee效应为特征的人口动态相结合。我们推导了关于生态阈值与策略行为相互作用的解析结果，并比较了合作（中央计划者）与非合作（开环纳什均衡和马尔可夫纳什均衡）下的结果。我们表明，在现实的控制成本下，无论是预防还是根除都无法在有限时间内消除入侵种群，而且完全阻止跨境扩散通常并非最优。在不存在Allee效应的情况下，零种群均衡是不稳定的，这意味着入侵物种将长期持续存在。相比之下，Allee效应的存在从根本上改变了系统动态，使灭绝在局部变得稳定，并形成一个吸引域，在该吸引域内根除变得可行。这一发现凸显了生态阈值的经济意义，并表明管理延迟如何可能使种群越过临界水平，此后无论后续控制努力如何，根除都不太可能实现。数值模拟进一步表明，相对于分散管理，合作管理扩大了导致根除的初始条件集合，并持续降低总社会成本。这些结果为IAS管理中的快速干预、预防性监管和更强有力的多边合作提供了正式依据，尤其是在欧盟等司法管辖区，因为在这些地区，列入清单和控制行动可能会因等待损害证据而延迟。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Invasive alien species (IAS) generate substantial ecological and economic damages that often transcend political boundaries, creating strong incentives for coordinated management. We develop a two-region ecological-economic model of IAS control that integrates prevention and eradication measures with population dynamics characterized by logistic growth and an Allee effect. We derive analytical results on the interaction between ecological thresholds and strategic behaviour and compare outcomes under cooperation (central planner) and non-cooperation (open loop Nash equilibrium and Markovian Nash equilibrium). We show that, under realistic control costs, neither prevention nor eradication can eliminate invasive populations in finite time, and complete prevention of cross-border spread is generally not optimal. In the absence of an Allee effect, the zero-population equilibrium is unstable, implying long-run persistence of the invasive species. By contrast, the presence of an Allee effect fundamentally alters system dynamics by making extinction locally stable and creating a basin of attraction within which eradication becomes feasible. This finding highlights the economic significance of ecological thresholds and demonstrates how delays in management can shift populations beyond critical levels, after which eradication is unlikely regardless of subsequent control efforts. Numerical simulations further show that cooperative management expands the set of initial conditions leading to eradication and consistently reduces aggregate social costs relative to decentralized management. The results provide a formal rationale for rapid intervention, precautionary regulation, and stronger multilateral cooperation in IAS management, particularly in jurisdictions such as the European Union where listing and control actions may be delayed pending evidence of harm.
