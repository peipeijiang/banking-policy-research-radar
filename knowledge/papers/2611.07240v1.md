<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Estimation and inference on a single large network are challenging due to dependence among observations. This paper studies nonparametric regression for outcomes defined on groups of $p$ agents, such as the indicator that three agents in a network form a triangle. Outcomes on groups that share an agent may be dependent, while outcomes on disjoint groups are independent. I call this problem subgraph regression; it nests dyadic regression as the special case $p=2$. Its leading application concerns subgraphs of a network, but the methods apply to any outcome defined on groups of agents. I establish a variance bound, uniform convergence rates in probability, and pointwise asymptotic normality for the subgraph analogue of the Nadaraya-Watson kernel regression estimator. The setting differs from regression with i.i.d. data in two ways: (i) the number of observations, of order $N^p$, overstates the effective sample size, which is $N$, and (ii) the rate of convergence depends on the dimension of the covariates of a single agent rather than of all $p$ agents, which mitigates the curse of dimensionality.
