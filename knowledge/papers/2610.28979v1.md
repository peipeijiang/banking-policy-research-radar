<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

选择合适的样本量是实验设计中的一个基本挑战。这一挑战在联合实验（conjoint experiments）中尤为突出，因为研究者同时针对多个估计目标，而这些目标的推断目标可能在不同样本量下才能实现。传统的样本量计算以事前方式应对这一挑战。在大型在线研究中，包括许多联合实验，数据往往持续到达，使研究者能够动态地停止数据收集。在此类情境下，相关的实际问题不是“我应该提前选择多大的样本量？”，而是随着数据不断积累，“我是否已经学到了足够的东西？”一种朴素的应对方式是反复检查传统置信区间和 \(p\) 值，但这样做会使其推断保证失效。我们通过开发一个适用于联合实验的通用随时有效（anytime-valid）框架来应对这一挑战，该框架允许持续监测和依赖于数据的停止。该框架为许多常见的联合实验估计目标提供了渐近 \(e\)-过程（\(e\)-processes）、置信序列（confidence sequences）和随时有效 \(p\) 值，并为任何可以表示为总体 OLS 系数线性对比的估计目标提供了通用方案。在常见的联合实验设定中，我们证明随时有效推断相对于固定样本设计可以带来显著的样本节省。提前停止的概率经常超过 50%，平均样本节省通常超过 25%。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Choosing an appropriate sample size is a fundamental challenge in experimental design. This challenge is particularly salient in conjoint experiments, where researchers simultaneously target many estimands whose inferential goals may be reached at different sample sizes. Conventional sample-size calculations address this challenge in an ex ante manner. In large online studies, including many conjoint experiments, data often arrive continuously, allowing researchers to dynamically stop data collection. In such settings, the relevant practical question is not "what sample size should I choose in advance?" but rather, as data accumulate, "have I learned enough yet?" A naive response is to repeatedly inspect conventional confidence intervals and $p$-values, but doing so invalidates their inferential guarantees. We address this challenge by developing a general anytime-valid framework for conjoint experiments that allows continuous monitoring and data-dependent stopping. This framework equips many common conjoint estimands with asymptotic $e$-processes, confidence sequences, and anytime-valid $p$-values, and provides a general recipe for any estimand that can be expressed as a linear contrast of population OLS coefficients. Across common conjoint settings, we demonstrate that anytime-valid inference can yield substantial sample savings relative to fixed-sample designs. The probability of early stopping frequently exceeds 50%, with mean sample savings often surpassing 25%.
