# The Double Diagonal Estimator: Reducing Bias in Two-Sided Marketplace Experiments

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

双边市场实验中参与者之间的干扰可能会严重偏倚传统的A/B测试。虽然双边随机化（two-sided randomization, TSR）——即同时对供给端和需求端进行随机化——能够提供缓解这种偏倚的估计量，但现有分析对于实践中常见的均衡市场条件下应优先选择哪种估计量所提供的指导有限，因为它们主要关注供给受限或需求受限的极端情形。我们提出双重对角（Double Diagonal, DD）估计量，这是一种用于干扰稳健处理效应估计的新方法。DD估计量对比处理-处理组与对照-对照组的配对结果，并利用非对角交互项（处理-对照与对照-处理）来校正干扰引起的偏倚。我们在一个简化的平均场市场模型下，首次对DD估计量与标准朴素TSR（TSRN）估计量进行理论比较，证明了两个关键结果：(i) 在外部选项参数的临界阈值以下，DD的偏倚严格小于标准估计量的偏倚；特别地，只要外部选项的吸引力不超过平台选项的两倍，DD的偏倚就更小；(ii) 两种估计量的偏倚方向相反，DD低估真实效应，而标准估计量高估真实效应。在现实市场设定下——涵盖异质性处理效应、供需失衡以及多种市场结构——的大量数值实验证实了我们的理论发现。在广泛的参数配置下，DD估计量表现出最低的偏倚，确立了其作为双边市场中可靠实验的稳健且通常更优的选择。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Interference between participants in two-sided marketplace experiments can severely bias conventional A/B tests. While two-sided randomization (TSR), which randomizes both supply and demand, enables estimators that mitigate this bias, existing analyses offer limited guidance on which estimator to prefer for the balanced market conditions common in practice, as they largely focus on extreme supply- or demand-constrained regimes. We introduce the Double Diagonal (DD) estimator, a novel method for interference-robust treatment effect estimation. The DD estimator contrasts the outcomes of treated-treated and control-control pairs and uses the off-diagonal interactions (treated-control and control-treated) to correct for interference-induced bias. We provide the first theoretical comparison between the DD and the standard naive TSR (TSRN) estimator under a simplifying mean-field market model, proving two key results: (i) DD's bias is strictly smaller than the standard estimator's below a critical threshold of the outside-option parameter; in particular, DD's bias is smaller whenever the outside option is at most twice as attractive as platform options; and (ii) the two estimators exhibit biases in opposite directions, with DD underestimating and the standard estimator overestimating the true effect, respectively. Extensive numerical experiments in realistic market settings--spanning heterogeneous treatment effects, supply-demand imbalances, and varied market structures--corroborate our theoretical findings. Across a wide range of configurations, the DD estimator demonstrates the lowest bias, establishing it as a robust and often superior choice for reliable experimentation in two-sided marketplaces.
