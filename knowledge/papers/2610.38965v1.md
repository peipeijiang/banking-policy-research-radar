# Orthogonal Inference for Conditional Z-Estimation without Cross-Fitting: A Distributional Nearest-Neighbor Approach

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

估计条件参数（如异质性处理效应）通常需要丰富的协变量调整，即使这些参数仅沿少数维度报告。在全样本上拟合 nuisance 函数使所有观测值均可用于调整，但会造成拟合得分与其被评估的观测值之间的依赖性，而交叉拟合（cross-fitting）通常可避免这种依赖性。我们针对恰好识别的条件矩方程，开发了基于全样本 nuisance 预测的逐点推断方法，并将条件平均处理效应作为特例加以恢复。我们利用分布最近邻（distributional nearest neighbors, DNN）——一种基于 U 统计量的平均化方法——将估计方程局部化到报告点附近。在局部设计、正交性、预测精度和定量删除稳定性的充分条件下，该估计量是渐近正态的，且冻结得分刀切法（frozen-score jackknife）在 nuisance 预测保持固定时一致地估计其极限协方差。在模拟中，全样本推断与交叉拟合推断表现相似，但由于 nuisance 近似误差、空间偏差和重学生化尾部而出现覆盖不足。利用收入和项目参与调查（Survey of Income and Program Participation）数据对 401(k) 资格的应用研究发现，不同 nuisance 预测方法得到的估计值相似，正态参考区间较宽。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Estimating conditional parameters, such as heterogeneous treatment effects, often requires rich covariate adjustment, even when the parameters are reported along only a few dimensions. Fitting nuisance functions on the full sample makes all observations available for adjustment, but creates dependence between fitted scores and the observations at which they are evaluated, which is typically avoided by cross-fitting. We develop pointwise inference with full-sample nuisance predictions for exactly identified conditional moment equations, recovering conditional average treatment effects as a special case. We localize the estimating equations around the reporting point using distributional nearest neighbors (DNN), a U-statistic-based averaging procedure. Under sufficient conditions on the local design, orthogonality, prediction accuracy and quantitative deletion stability, the estimator is asymptotically normal, and a frozen-score jackknife consistently estimates its limiting covariance with nuisance predictions held fixed. In simulations, full-sample and cross-fitted inference perform similarly, with coverage shortfalls from nuisance approximation error, spatial bias and heavy studentized tails. An application to 401(k) eligibility using Survey of Income and Program Participation data finds similar estimates across nuisance prediction methods, with wide normal-reference intervals.
