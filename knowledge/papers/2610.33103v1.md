<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

前景理论将风险选择分为三个部分：损失厌恶，以损失相对于收益的相对权重$λ$来衡量（例如，$λ= 2$意味着损失的权重大于收益的两倍）、对结果大小的敏感性递减（即价值函数的曲率和线性程度），以及概率的非线性加权。我通过元分析11项研究中涵盖32个语言模型的102个拟合估计值，检验了大型语言模型（Large Language Models, LLMs）是否表现出与人类相同的特性。三个组成部分呈现出不同的结果。损失厌恶程度适中：合并$λ$为1.26（95% CI 1.06至1.46），远低于最强的人类元分析估计值（$λ= 1.96$），而与更为保守的估计值（1.07和1.31）相容。在设计匹配的条件下，当收益和损失对称时，LLMs与人类相容（$λ= 1.15$对1.07），但当损失小于收益时则远低于人类（1.46对2.3），而后者正是人类损失厌恶最强的情形。在Tversky-Kahneman方法下，价值函数接近线性而非凹性（1.01，人类值为0.67），但在Tanaka方法下则比人类更具凹性（0.52，对0.67），因此敏感性递减取决于测量方法。概率加权呈现出类似人类的倒S形（Tversky-Kahneman方法下$γ= 0.81$，Tanaka方法下为0.84），但相关研究较少。总之，LLMs表现出较小的损失厌恶和较小的敏感性递减，但保留了类似人类的概率扭曲，然而这些概括性结论在考察特定模型家族和拟合方法时会发生变化。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Prospect theory splits risky choice into three parts: loss aversion, measured by the relative weight of losses to gains, $λ$ (e.g., $λ= 2$ implies that losses weigh twice as much as gains), diminishing sensitivity to the size of outcomes (i.e., the curvature and linearity of the value function), and the nonlinear weighting of probabilities. I examined whether Large Language Models (LLMs) display the same properties as people by meta-analyzing 102 fitted estimates from 11 studies covering 32 language models. The three components emerge differently. Loss aversion is modest: the pooled $λ$ is 1.26 (95% CI 1.06 to 1.46), far below the strongest human meta-analytic estimate ($λ= 1.96$) and compatible with more conservative ones (1.07 and 1.31). Matched by design, LLMs are compatible with people when gains and losses are symmetric ($λ= 1.15$ against 1.07), but well below them when losses are smaller than gains (1.46 against 2.3), where human loss aversion is strongest. Value functions are close to linear rather than concave under the Tversky-Kahneman method (1.01, against a human value of 0.67), but more concave than in people under the Tanaka method (0.52, against 0.67), so diminishing sensitivity depends on how it is measured. Probability weighting shows a human-like inverse S shape ($γ= 0.81$ under Tversky-Kahneman and 0.84 under the Tanaka method), though on few studies. In sum, LLMs show little loss aversion and little diminishing sensitivity, but retain human-like probability distortion, yet these generalizations shift when looking at specific model families and fitting methods.
