<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

我们研究一个国际抗菌素耐药性（AMR）俱乐部的形成，该俱乐部是由两个国家组成的联盟，成员国共同资助一种新型抗生素并获得早期使用权，而非成员国则需等待独占期结束。两国通过跨境流动性相联系，药物通过一种基金与退款机制融资，该机制在开发期间向开发商支付固定报酬，并在上市后根据新型抗生素的使用量按比例退款。我们的分析突显了一种独占性与流动性之间的权衡，这一权衡将抗生素创新与其他全球公共产品区分开来。较长的独占期通过提高背叛成本增强了联盟的内部稳定性，但会导致非成员国耐药性升高，并通过流动性反馈至俱乐部成员国。独占性与成员国出资在维持大联盟方面具有互补性，而独占性与流动性在边际上则表现为替代关系。将分析扩展至不同规模的国家会提高维持合作所需的最低独占期，但在部分合作情形下唯一地确定较大国家为出资方。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

We study the formation of an international antimicrobial resistance (AMR) club, a two-country coalition in which members jointly finance a new antibiotic and get early access, while non-members wait out an exclusivity period. The countries are linked by cross-border mobility, and the drug is financed through a fund-and-refund scheme that pays the developer a fixed reward during development plus a refund proportional to the use of the new antibiotic after launch. Our analysis highlights an exclusivity-mobility tradeoff that distinguishes antibiotic innovation from other global public goods. A longer exclusivity window strengthens internal stability of the coalition by increasing the cost of defection, but leads to higher resistance in non-member countries which feeds back to the club members through mobility. Exclusivity and member contributions are complementary in sustaining the grand coalition, while exclusivity and mobility act as substitutes at the margin. Extending the analysis to countries of different sizes raises the minimum exclusivity window needed to sustain cooperation, but uniquely identifies the larger country as the financier under partial cooperation.
