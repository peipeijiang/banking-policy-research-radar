# Pawnshops and Property-Related Crime: Lessons from Interwar Tokyo

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

本研究利用1927年至1935年的行政区面板数据和固定效应模型，考察了两次世界大战之间东京当铺与财产相关犯罪之间的关系。据此，将面向低收入借款人的公共当铺与以营利为导向的私人当铺进行了比较。结果显示，公共借贷与盗窃呈负相关，与诈骗和敲诈呈正相关；而私人借贷与诈骗、敲诈以及侵占均呈负相关。一项简单的货币化测算表明，犯罪数量和受害者损失可能得出不同的评估结果。总体而言，信贷与犯罪之间的关系取决于制度设计和犯罪类型。

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Using ward-level panel data for 1927 to 1935 and fixed-effects models, this study examines how pawnshops were related to property-related crime in interwar Tokyo. Accordingly, public pawnshops targeting low-income borrowers are compared with profit-oriented private pawnshops. The results reveal that public lending is negatively associated with theft and positively associated with fraud and extortion, whereas private lending is negatively associated with fraud and extortion as well as embezzlement. A simple monetization exercise shows that offense counts and victim losses can yield different assessments. Overall, the relationship between credit and crime depended on both institutional design and crime type.
