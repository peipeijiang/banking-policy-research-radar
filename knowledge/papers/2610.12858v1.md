“隐晦式安全”（Security by obscurity），即通过隐藏系统运作方式来保护其免受对手攻击的理念，长期以来在安全社区中评价不高。然而，其直觉上的吸引力始终存在，多年来在行业实践和公共辩论中反复出现。其逻辑一方面是，对系统进行公开审查是发现和回应问题的最佳方式；另一方面，过度开放可能为对手提供攻击路线图，从而增加风险。这并非纯粹的技术问题，而是关于人的问题：它关乎攻击者和善意的安全研究人员在现实世界中如何回应开放。因此，安全专家将他们的立场建立在数十年的社区经验之上；然而，该领域缺乏实证或理论基础来支撑这些长期存在的信念。我们首次对“隐晦式安全”进行了严格的建模，旨在刻画理性行为者在易出错的系统中将如何回应开放或隐晦。我们证明，在现实模型条件下的均衡中，“隐晦”系统比“开放”系统更可能遭受成功攻击——从而为这一长期公认的智慧提供了理论支持。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

"Security by obscurity," or the idea that hiding how a system works helps protect it from adversaries, has long been poorly regarded in the security community. Yet its intuitive appeal persists, and it has resurfaced repeatedly in industry practices and public debates over the years. The logic, on the one hand, is that open scrutiny of systems is the best way to find and respond to problems; and on the other hand, that too much openness can increase risk by giving adversaries a roadmap for attack. This isn't purely a technical question, but a human one: it's about how attackers and good-faith security researchers respond to openness in the real world. As such, security experts base their stance on decades of community experience; however, the field lacks empirical or theoretical foundations to back up these long-standing beliefs. We provide the first rigorous modeling of "security by obscurity," aiming to capture how rational actors will respond to openness or obscurity in fallible systems. We prove that in equilibrium under realistic model conditions, "obscured" systems are more likely to suffer successful attacks than "open" systems --- establishing theoretical support for the longstanding received wisdom.
