# Frontier Technology in Frontier Markets? Financing Global AI Entrepreneurship

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

人工智能（AI）的全球扩散是否延伸到了构建AI的企业之创立与融资环节？利用一个涵盖2015年至2022年间172个国家116,795家初创企业的新颖数据集，我们将公司描述和融资历史与创始人、早期员工及潜在投资者的就业和教育记录进行关联。我们记录了全球AI创业在实体层面与金融层面的显著分化。在实体层面，各地的AI初创企业均展现出通用目的技术的特征：在世界各个地区，它们所针对的客户行业数量都多于可比非AI初创企业，并且更倾向于销售适用于多个行业的横向产品。此外，在ChatGPT发布之后，基线任务更易于被AI替代的初创企业扩展了其工作活动的广度和商业人员配置。然而，此类可扩展企业所拥有的融资优势却高度局部化。北美AI初创企业的年融资额比非AI同行高出151%，中东高出181%，而东亚仅高出42%，欧洲高出16%，非洲、拉丁美洲或南亚则未检测到显著溢价。在控制了目标客户市场、创始人资质、早期团队人力资本和本地投资者特征后，上述因素能解释拉丁美洲的部分差距，但更广泛的全球分化依然存在。在非洲，全部AI融资的一半流向了基础算力和数据中心基础设施；排除这些提供商后可以发现，非洲AI产品初创企业的融资额比非AI同行低57%。这些模式提供了一个早期警示：AI或许能够触达世界各地的用户，却未必能在世界各地建立起企业。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Does the global diffusion of artificial intelligence (AI) extend to the creation and financing of the firms that build it? Using a novel dataset of 116,795 startups founded between 2015 and 2022 across 172 countries, we link company descriptions and financing histories to the employment and education records of founders, early employees, and potential investors. We document a stark divergence between the real and financial sides of global AI entrepreneurship. On the real side, AI startups everywhere display the hallmarks of a general-purpose technology: in every world region, they target more customer sectors than comparable non-AI startups and are more likely to sell horizontal products that apply across industries. Moreover, following the release of ChatGPT, startups whose baseline tasks were more amenable to AI expanded the breadth of their work activities and commercial staffing. Yet the financing advantage that such scalable businesses command is sharply localized. AI startups raise 151 percent more annual capital than non-AI peers in North America and 181 percent more in the Middle East, compared with 42 percent more in East Asia, 16 percent more in Europe, and no detectable premium in Africa, Latin America, or South Asia. Accounting for targeted customer markets, founder credentials, early-team human capital, and local investor characteristics explains part of the gap in Latin America but leaves the broader global divide intact. In Africa, half of all AI funding goes to basic compute and data-center infrastructure; excluding these providers reveals that African AI product startups raise 57 percent less than non-AI peers. These patterns provide an early warning that AI may reach users around the world without building firms around the world.
