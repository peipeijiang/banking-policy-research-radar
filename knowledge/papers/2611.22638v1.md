# Nested logit: model structure and normalisation

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22638v1)

## 一句话结论

> 论文辨析嵌套logit模型中RU1/RU2标签与非归一化形式（NNNL）的混淆，论证归一化只改变单位而除以结构参数才改变模型，并比较软件实现与算例，建议以模型实际做法而非标签来描述模型。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

嵌套logit（nested logit）仍然是一个广泛使用的模型，尤其是在应用研究中。然而，围绕该模型不同版本的存在、同一版本的不同归一化方式以及不同的软件实现，仍然存在大量混淆。本文认为，常用的标签RU1和RU2，以及“非归一化嵌套logit”（non-normalised nested logit, NNNL）这一名称本身，都助长了这种混淆。该模型有两种写法：一种是将效用除以包含它们的巢的结构参数，这种形式在构造上即与效用最大化一致；另一种不做这种除法，称为NNNL。这两种形式之间的区别并非归一化的问题。在顶层或底层进行归一化得到的是同一个模型，而NNNL本身也是经过归一化的。区分这两种形式的关键在于是否执行了除法运算。简言之，归一化改变的是单位，而除法改变的是模型。未做除法的形式仅在特定条件下才与效用一致的形式相吻合，否则只有在效用设定不同于原设定时才与效用最大化一致，此时通用系数变为巢特定系数。本文指出RU1已被用于三种不同的设定，比较了常用软件包如何归一化和报告其参数，并通过计算示例说明了相关后果。建议是：应当根据模型做了什么来描述模型，而不是使用这些标签。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Nested logit remains a widely used model, especially in applied work. However, there remains substantial confusion surrounding the existence of different versions of the model, different normalisations for the same version, and different software implementations. This paper argues that the labels in common use, RU1 and RU2, and the name non-normalised nested logit (NNNL) itself, have contributed to the confusion. The model is written in two forms: one in which utilities are divided by the structural parameter of the nest that contains them, which is consistent with utility maximisation by construction, and one without that division, known as NNNL. The difference between these forms is not one of normalisation. Normalising at the top or at the bottom gives the same model, and NNNL is itself normalised. What distinguishes the forms is whether the division is performed. In short, normalisation changes the units, whereas division changes the model. The undivided form coincides with the utility-consistent one only under specific conditions, and otherwise is consistent with utility maximisation only under utilities other than those specified, in which generic coefficients become nest-specific. The paper highlights that RU1 has been used for three different specifications, compares how widely used software packages normalise and report their parameters, and illustrates the consequences with calculated examples. The recommendation is that models be described by what they do, rather than by these labels.

</details>
