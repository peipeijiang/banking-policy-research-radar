<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

人工智能（AI）正日益深入地嵌入科研工作之中，但研究人员对不同科研任务中使用AI的态度可能并不一致。本研究基于一项国际性、自选自愿参与的样本，涵盖3,785名来自STEM及医学与健康科学领域的博士生，这些受访者参与了《自然》2025年研究生调查。我们分析了受访者在撰写研究论文、收集与分析数据、设计实验、追踪科学文献以及总结文献等方面使用AI的接受程度。潜在类别分析识别出四种截然不同的态度类型。占主导地位的类型体现了“分工”模式，即AI在文献相关任务中被广泛接受，但在与智力贡献密切相关的活动中（如写作、数据分析和实验设计）则遭到抵制。“维持现状”类型对各类任务普遍感到不适，“全能型”类型则普遍感到舒适，而“犹豫不决”类型则表现出较大的不确定性。这些模式表明，研究人员对AI的态度并非简单地围绕接受与拒绝的二元对立，而是围绕任务特定的边界来组织，这些边界很可能涉及任务委派、作者身份和责任归属。由于该调查衡量的是舒适度而非合法性，因此这些类型最宜被理解为带有规范性维度的态度配置。研究结果凸显了在AI治理、博士培养、披露机制和研究评价中采取任务特定方法的重要性。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly embedded in scientific work, but researchers may not evaluate its use uniformly across research tasks. This study examines task-specific attitudes towards AI among an international, self-selected sample of 3,785 PhD students in STEM and medical and health sciences who participated in Nature's Graduate Survey 2025. We analyse respondents' comfort with using AI for writing a research article, collecting and analysing data, designing experiments, tracking scientific literature, and summarising it. Latent class analysis identifies four distinct attitudinal profiles. The dominant profile reflects a "division of labour," in which AI is widely accepted for literature-related tasks but resisted in activities closely associated with intellectual contribution, such as writing, data analysis, and experimental design. A "status quo" profile is broadly uncomfortable across tasks, an "all-purpose" profile is broadly comfortable, and an "undecided" profile expresses substantial uncertainty. These patterns suggest that attitudes towards AI in research are organised less around a simple acceptance-rejection divide than around task-specific boundaries, likely concerning delegation, authorship, and responsibility. Because the survey measures comfort rather than legitimacy, the profiles are best interpreted as attitudinal configurations with a normative dimension. The findings highlight the importance of task-specific approaches to AI governance, doctoral training, disclosure, and research evaluation.
