# The profit-bias identity in sports betting: bookmaker profit as the public's prediction error

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

体育博彩将大量公众的资金持续转移至少数公司手中。关于这一资金流动最具影响力的解释出自Levitt（2004），该理论认为博彩公司通过将赔率设定偏离市场出清点，以利用公众可预测的偏见获利。该理论通过两个数字（某一方获胜的概率及其吸引的投注额占比）来计算利润，并假设某一方的投注占比与比赛结果相互独立。本文放宽了这一假设，推导出一个利润-偏见恒等式：利润是输方预期投注占比的仿射递增函数，而Levitt的表达式则是独立性假设下的特例。该恒等式将博彩公司的利润空间精确分解为三个渠道：其抽成（hold）、赔率偏移（price shading）与公众倾向的乘积，以及投注占比与结果之间的协方差。因此，若无赔率偏移，公众倾向本身毫无价值；反之，若无公众倾向，赔率偏移也毫无价值。在公众信念模型下，利润的驱动因素在于公众的贝叶斯误差：即代表性投注者选择输方的概率。我们给出了“金发姑娘区间”（Goldilocks Zone）的充分必要条件，即博彩公司与投注者双方均能获利的赔率区间。通过对1,139场美国职业棒球大联盟比赛进行检验，我们发现投注占比与结果之间表面上的相关性实则是一种辛普森悖论：当所有比赛合并分析时该相关性存在，但一旦按博彩公司偏向哪一方进行分组后，该相关性便消失。公众强烈偏向热门球队，但我们未检测到相应的赔率偏移，且实际利润与抽成之间无显著差异。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Sports betting moves money continuously from a large public to a small number of firms. The most influential account of that flow, due to Levitt (2004), holds that books price away from the market-clearing point to exploit predictable public biases. It computes profit from two numbers (the probability that a side wins the proposition and the fraction of handle it attracts), treating the share on a side as independent of the outcome. Here we relax that assumption and derive a profit-bias identity: profit is affine and increasing in the expected share of handle on the losing side, with Levitt's expression as the special case of independence. The identity resolves the book's margin into exactly three channels: its hold, the product of its price shading and the public's lean, and the covariance between bet share and outcome. A lean is thus worthless without shading, and shading is worthless without a lean. Under a public-belief model, the profit driver is the public's Bayes error: the probability of a representative bettor selecting the losing side. We provide necessary and sufficient conditions for a "Goldilocks Zone": prices at which book and bettor both profit. Testing these predictions on 1,139 Major League Baseball games, we find that the apparent dependence between bet share and outcome is a Simpson's paradox: present when games are pooled, but absent once they are separated by which side the book favored. The public leans heavily toward favorites, but we detect no matching shading, and the realized margin is indistinguishable from the hold.
