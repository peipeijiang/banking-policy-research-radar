<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

金融基础模型日益支持涵盖金融问答、信息披露分析、预测和决策的工作流程。在这些场景中，不确定性可能涉及证据在相关时间点是否有效且可获得、某种解释是否合理、哪些结果仍然可能，或在成本与风险约束下哪种行动更优。由于这些对象出现在工作流程的不同节点，不确定性不能简化为对最终输出的置信度。我们引入一个来源感知框架，区分证据与上下文、模型与推理、结果与行动三个层面的六种不确定性来源，同时将智能体工作流视为不确定性得以传播、改变形态或被降低的执行层。我们开发了一个基于机制的分类体系，其根基在于各方法如何构建或验证不确定性与可靠性表征，并利用该体系评估其在各金融任务领域中的适用性。我们进一步将金融领域的实证研究映射到该分类体系和任务领域中，以刻画当前研究图景。我们的分析表明，大多数被综述的研究处理的是金融工作流中个别环节的不确定性，而将证据与推理可靠性与下游预测及受约束行动相连接的工作仍然有限。通过连接不确定性产生的位置、其表征方式以及其如何影响下游决策，本综述为研究和评估金融基础模型中的不确定性提供了统一基础。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Financial foundation models increasingly support workflows spanning financial question answering, disclosure analysis, forecasting, and decision-making. In these settings, uncertainty may concern whether evidence is valid and available at the relevant time, whether an interpretation is justified, which outcomes remain plausible, or which action is preferred under costs and risk constraints. Because these objects arise at different points in a workflow, uncertainty cannot be reduced to confidence in a final output. We introduce a source-aware framework that distinguishes six sources of uncertainty across evidence and context, model and inference, and outcome and action, while treating agentic workflows as the execution layer through which uncertainty can propagate, change form, or be reduced. We develop a mechanism-based taxonomy grounded in how methods construct or verify uncertainty and reliability representations, and use it to assess their applicability across financial task domains. We further map finance-specific empirical studies across the taxonomy and task domains to characterize the current research landscape. Our analysis shows that most reviewed studies address uncertainty in individual parts of financial workflows, with limited work connecting evidence and reasoning reliability to downstream prediction and constrained action. By connecting where uncertainty arises, how it is represented, and how it bears on downstream decisions, this survey provides a unified foundation for studying and evaluating uncertainty in financial foundation models.
