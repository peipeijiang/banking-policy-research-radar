# Regional advantage in rugby sevens: Is there a home effect when nobody is home?

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16312v1)

## 一句话结论

> 该论文利用国际橄榄球七人制比赛数据，研究球队表现与比赛地点距离的关系，发现总体无区域优势，但部分球队存在距离相关的表现差异。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

我们研究了七人制橄榄球中是否存在“区域差异”（Regional Differential）现象：即在没有任何参赛队伍享有正式主场身份的赛事中，某些国家队是否会因赛事举办地点的不同而系统性地表现超常或失常。利用2016年至2025年间国际七人制橄榄球赛事（主要是世界橄榄球七人制系列赛，也包括七人制橄榄球世界杯和奥运会）中2672场男子和女子比赛的全部数据，我们将经典主场优势文献中的二元“本地性”指标替换为各队伍与主办国之间在地理、时间和文化 proximity（邻近性）上的连续度量。我们表明，邻近性与表现之间的总体关联（在朴素设定中看似显著且规模较大）几乎完全是“队伍构成”和“赛程安排”的产物：一旦通过对称的队伍-比赛面板数据中的固定效应吸收队伍和对手实力差异，普遍的区位优势便不复存在。然而，这一总体为零的结果掩盖了显著的异质性。对于一组明确界定的队伍（最显著的是法国，此外还有新西兰、美国、加拿大、西班牙、爱尔兰、肯尼亚、萨摩亚和巴西），其表现随主办城市与本国之间距离的增加而显著且单调地下降。一些传统的南半球强队（斐济、南非、澳大利亚、阿根廷）则基本不受距离影响。我们进一步表明，对某些队伍而言，这一梯度效应是通过东西方向的时差（jet lag）而非单纯的空间位移起作用的。因此，七人制橄榄球中的区域差异是真实存在的，但具有“队伍特异性”而非普遍性，这一区别在汇总估计中被掩盖了。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

We study the existence of a \emph{Regional Differential} in rugby sevens: whether, in tournaments where no competing team enjoys formal home status, some national sides systematically over- or under-perform depending on \emph{where} the event is staged. Using the universe of 2672 men's and women's matches from international rugby sevens tournaments played between 2016 and 2025, principally the World Rugby Sevens Series, but also the World Cup Sevens and the Olympic Games, we replace the binary ``locality'' indicator of the classic home advantage literature with continuous measures of geographic, temporal, and cultural proximity between each team and the host country. We show that aggregate associations between proximity and performance (which appear large and highly significant in naive specifications) are almost entirely an artifact of \emph{team composition} and \emph{scheduling}: once team and opponent quality are absorbed through fixed effects in a symmetric team-match panel, no general regional advantage survives. However, this null aggregate masks substantial heterogeneity. For a well-defined subset of teams (most notably France, but also New Zealand, the United States, Canada, Spain, Ireland, Kenya, Samoa, and Brazil), performance declines significantly and monotonically with the distance separating the host city from home. Some of the established southern-hemisphere powers (Fiji, South Africa, Australia, Argentina) are essentially distance-neutral. We further show that for some teams the gradient operates through east-west jet lag rather than pure displacement. The Regional Differential in sevens is therefore real but \emph{team-specific} rather than universal, a distinction obscured by pooled estimation.
