<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

CEO薪酬常常是自上而下地进行考察的，即将其视为CEO、董事会和股东之间谈判的结果。尽管这些方法为理解CEO薪酬的本质及其与企业绩效和公司内部不平等的关系提供了有价值的见解，但我们认为它们忽略了CEO与员工之间的内部决策动态。为填补这一研究空白，我们开发了一个基于NK框架的代理人模型（agent-based model），其中一位薪酬与公司长期绩效挂钩的CEO与若干薪酬与部门短期绩效挂钩的员工进行互动。他们各自薪酬函数中的这种时间性二分（temporal dichotomy）造成了激励的潜在时间错位（temporal misalignment）。通过改变CEO做出决策时所考虑的时间跨度（horizon），我们考察了这种激励的时间（错）对齐如何塑造企业绩效以及CEO与员工之间的薪酬不平等。我们的研究结果表明，将CEO的时间跨度与员工的短期激励对齐能够产生最高的绩效，在所考察的所有任务环境中均提高了达到的绩效水平以及改进速度。这一发现与CEO薪酬研究中通常给出的建议——即避免短期主义——形成对比。此外，该模型表明，所考察的最长时间跨度既产生了最低的绩效，也产生了最高的薪酬不平等。这种通常被归因于管理者权力（managerial power）的模式，在我们的模型中仅由激励错位所产生，而无需代理人对其自身薪酬拥有任何权力。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

CEO compensation has often been examined from the top down, i.e., as the outcome of a negotiation between the CEO, the board of directors, and the shareholders. While these approaches have provided valuable insights into the nature of CEO compensation and its relations with firm performance and intra-firm inequality, we believe they leave aside the internal decision-making dynamics of CEOs and employees. To fill this research gap, we develop an agent-based model built on the NK framework in which a CEO compensated for long-term firm performance interacts with several employees rewarded for short-term departmental performance. This temporal dichotomy in their respective compensation functions creates a potential temporal misalignment of incentives. By varying the horizon considered by the CEO to make their decisions, we examine how this temporal (mis)alignment of incentives shapes firm performance and pay inequality between the CEO and the employees. Our findings suggest that aligning the CEO's horizon with the employees' short-term incentives yields the highest performance, raising both the level attained and the speed of improvement across all task environments examined. This finding contrasts with the usual advice given in CEO compensation research, which is to avoid short-termism. Furthermore, the model shows that the longest horizon examined produces both the lowest performance and the highest pay inequality. This pattern, commonly attributed to managerial power, emerges in our model from incentive misalignment alone, without agents holding any power over their own compensation.
