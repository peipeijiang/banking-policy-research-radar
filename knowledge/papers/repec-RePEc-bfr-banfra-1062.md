# Is Monetary Policy Transmission Heterogeneous across Euro Area Countries and over Time? A Reassessment

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

我们评估了2000年至2025年期间欧元区各国货币政策传导（MPT）的异质性程度。利用月度局部投影法，我们在欧元区整体及国家层面估计了货币政策对一系列广泛传导变量的影响。首先，我们发现，尽管抵押贷款市场相关变量的反应存在不对称性，但产出和通胀对货币政策冲击的反应异质性有限。其次，根据我们的结果，关键的结构性差异，如家庭负债水平、债务期限、利率粘性以及部门构成，确实塑造了欧元区各国货币政策传导的异质性。然而，综合来看，这些差异在一定程度上相互抵消，导致货币政策传导相对同质。第三，月度FAVAR估计证实并拓展了我们的局部投影结果：产出和通胀的不对称性仍然有限，但主权利差、食品价格、信贷变量以及我们的消费代理变量的不对称性更高。最后，FAVAR模型的滚动窗口估计表明，货币政策传导中的时变异质性以暂时性且由危机驱动的分歧为特征，这些分歧持续回归至较低基线水平，反映了关键的货币政策干预措施，而非更深层次的结构性经济分歧。非常规货币政策在这一模式中发挥核心作用：在危机时期，它们倾向于缩小国家间分歧，对受影响最严重的国家产生更强影响，因此，根据我们的主要衡量指标，它们本质上作为一种异质性政策冲击发挥作用。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

We assess the degree of heterogeneity in monetary policy transmission (MPT) across euro area countries for the period 2000–2025. Using monthly local projections, we estimate the effects of monetary policy on a broad set of transmission variables at both euro area and national levels. First, we find limited heterogeneity of output and inflation responses to monetary policy shocks, despite asymmetric responses of mortgage market-related variables. Second, key structural differences, such as households’ indebtedness, debt maturity, interest rate rigidity and sectoral composition, do shape heterogeneities in MPT across euro area countries, according to our results. Considered jointly, though, these differences partly offset one another, leading to a relatively homogeneous transmission of monetary policy. Third, a monthly FAVAR estimation confirms and broadens our local projections results: asymmetry remains contained for output and inflation, but it is higher for sovereign spreads, food prices, credit variables and our consumption proxy. Finally, a rolling-window estimation of the FAVAR model shows that time-varying heterogeneity in MPT is characterized by temporary and crisis-driven divergences that consistently revert to a low baseline, reflecting key monetary policy interventions rather than deeper structural economic divergences. Unconventional monetary policies play a central role in this pattern: they tend to reduce country divergences during crisis periods, impacting more strongly the countries most affected, hence operating, by design, as a heterogeneous policy shock according to our main metric.
