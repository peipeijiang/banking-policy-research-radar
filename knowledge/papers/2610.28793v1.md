# A cross-cultural dataset on the structure of social networks: The ENDOW project

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28793v1)

## 一句话结论

> ENDOW项目在29国50个社区采集家庭间物质与非物质的多元社会支持网络数据，构建支持流动网络并比较网络统计量，发现结构异质性显著，挑战既有社会网络结构经验法则。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

我们呈现来自全球29个国家50个社区的多层社会网络数据，这些数据是“ENDOW”项目的一部分。这些社区展现出极大的多样性，从孟加拉国和瓦努阿图的渔民，到斐济和玻利维亚的园艺种植者，到纳米比亚和阿根廷的牧民，再到印度和哥伦比亚的农民和雇佣劳动者。数据收集在兼顾这种差异性的同时，仍然力求可比性。受访者代表其“共享单元”（家庭）回答了十个网络提示问题，涵盖不同类型的物质和非物质支持。据此，我们构建了表征每个社区中共享单元之间支持流动的网络。我们比较了ENDOW各社区之间的提名模式和网络汇总统计量，并与其他基准数据集进行了对比。汇总结果呈现出相当大的异质性，表明有必要重新审视关于社会网络结构的既有“经验法则”。这些度量指标为人类社交性与社会结构多样性的研究提供了新的参照点。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

We present multilayer social network data from 50 communities, located in 29 countries around the world, gathered as part of the "ENDOW" project. The communities show exceptional diversity, from fishers in Bangladesh and Vanuatu, to horticulturalists in Fiji and Bolivia, to pastoralists in Namibia and Argentina, to farmers and wage labourers in India and Colombia. Data collection embraced this variation, while still aiming for comparability. Respondents answered ten network prompts on behalf of their "sharing unit" (household), covering different types of material and immaterial support. From these, we construct networks representing flows of support between sharing units in each community. We compare nomination patterns and network summary statistics across the ENDOW communities and to other benchmark datasets. The summaries exhibit considerable heterogeneity, suggesting a need to revisit existing ``rules of thumb'' about social network structure. These measures contribute novel reference points for research on the diversity of human sociality and social structure.
