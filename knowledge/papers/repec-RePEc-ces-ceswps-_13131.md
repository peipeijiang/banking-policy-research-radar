# The Moral Cost of Carbon

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

我们定义了碳的道德成本（moral cost of carbon, MCC）这一概念：即个体在消费决策中隐含施加的内部碳价格。我们提出了一种实验程序来测量MCC的分布，并在两项实验中予以实施。研究得出五个事实：（i）MCC在零处有大量集中，但具有长右尾，条件中位数约为每吨500欧元。（ii）MCC在不同消费领域之间的平均值相近，但在个体内部并不相同。（iii）MCC与对碳抵消的支付意愿不同。（iv）MCC会随关于同伴估值的信息而变化。（v）当决策环境中存在以碳为框架的费用时，MCC可能受到动机挤出效应的影响。我们展示了MCC在地方气候政策设计中的用途。我们考虑一个规划者，其必须在碳费与强制披露旅游住宿碳足迹之间做出选择。披露是否应补充碳费，最终取决于福利核算中是否应计入道德成本。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

We define the concept of the moral cost of carbon (MCC): the internal carbon price individuals implicitly apply to their consumption decisions. We propose an experimental procedure to measure the distribution of the MCC and implement it in two experiments. Five facts emerge. (i) The MCC has a large mass at zero, but it has a long right tail, with a conditional median of about 500 euros per ton. (ii) It is similar on average across consumption domains, but not within individuals. (iii) It differs from the willingness to pay for carbon offsets. (iv) It is malleable with respect to information about peers' valuations. (v) It can be subject to motivation crowding-out when a carbon-framed fee is present in the decision environment. We illustrate the use of the MCC for the design of local climate policies. We consider a planner who must choose between a carbon fee and mandatory disclosure of the footprint of tourist stays. Whether disclosure should complement a carbon fee ultimately depends on whether moral costs should be accounted for in welfare.
