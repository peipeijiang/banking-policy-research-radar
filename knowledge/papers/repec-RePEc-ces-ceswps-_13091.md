<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

非互惠优惠贸易安排（PTAs），如普遍优惠制（GSP），是发展中国家的主要贸易政策工具。然而，现有数据仅粗略地追踪了资格变化，且关于其效果的证据不一。我们构建了一个涵盖38项方案（1950–2025年）的数据库，对毕业、中止和失效进行了编码。结构引力分析发现平均效应不显著，且对除最不发达国家（LDCs）以外的受惠国均不显著。对于最不发达国家，优惠使对授予国的出口增加了34%，而GSP优惠则增加了88%。这些收益集中在服装和鞋类领域，且未被关税所反映。

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Non-reciprocal preferential trade arrangements (PTAs), such as the Generalized System of Preferences (GSP), are the main trade policy instrument for developing countries. Yet, existing data track changes in eligibility only coarsely and evidence on their effects is mixed. We introduce a database of 38 schemes (1950–2025) coding graduations, suspensions, and lapses. Structural gravity analysis finds no significant average effect, and none for beneficiaries other than least developed countries (LDCs). For LDCs, preferences raise exports to grantors by 34 percent, and GSP preferences by 88 percent. These gains concentrate in apparel and footwear and are not captured by tariffs.

</details>
