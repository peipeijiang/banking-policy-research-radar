<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

当一位掌握更多信息的提议者提出政策建议时，回应者不应仅凭表面价值接受该建议，而应追问其为何被选中。我们建模分析了这种推断如何在利益大体一致的情况下仍可能削弱信任。提议者私下观察到一个可行政策的“篮子”并提出其中一项；回应者决定是否批准该提议，但可能缺乏评估其的专业知识。更大的篮子使帕累托改进更有可能存在，但也赋予提议者更大的空间来挑选一项有利于自己而牺牲回应者利益的政策。在唯一均衡中，帕累托改进常常被提出，却又被理性地拒绝。本应促成协议的灵活性反而滋生了不信任。

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

When a better-informed proposer puts forward a policy proposal, the responder should not take the proposal at face value but ask why it was chosen. We model how this inference can undermine trust even when interests are broadly aligned. A proposer privately observes a "basket" of viable policies and proposes one; a responder decides whether to approve the proposal but may lack the expertise to evaluate it. A larger basket makes a Pareto improvement more likely to be available but also gives the proposer greater scope to cherry-pick a policy that favors him at the responder's expense. In the unique equilibrium, Pareto improvements are often proposed and yet rationally rejected. Flexibility that should enable agreement instead breeds distrust.

</details>
