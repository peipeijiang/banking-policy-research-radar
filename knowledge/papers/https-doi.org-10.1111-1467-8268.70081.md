# Disinflation Costs in Africa: The Role of Policy Design and Monetary Institutions

摘要：本文研究了非洲牺牲率的决定因素，重点关注西非国家经济共同体（ECOWAS）地区，采用新构建的涵盖1960年至2024年间54个国家的通货紧缩事件面板数据。实证分析依托面板估计方法，包括聚类标准误OLS、FGLS和2SLS-IV方法，以处理异质性、面板特定异方差以及关键政策变量中潜在的内生性问题。结果提供了稳健的证据，表明货币制度框架和通货紧缩设计在塑造通货紧缩成本方面发挥核心作用。具体而言，货币目标制和汇率钉住制度始终与较高的牺牲率相关联，表明刚性名义锚在通货紧缩期间放大了产出损失。相比之下，货币联盟成员身份与较低的牺牲率相关联，这可能反映了信誉收益、政策协调以及一体化货币框架内更稳定的通胀预期。通货紧缩的政策相关特征，如速度、幅度和持续时间，也显著影响牺牲率，凸显了政策设计在塑造调整成本方面的重要性。总体而言，研究结果凸显了制度性货币框架和政策设计在塑造非洲通胀-产出权衡中的核心作用，并为非洲货币政策与区域一体化的讨论提供了新的实证证据。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

ABSTRACT This paper investigates the determinants of the sacrifice ratio in Africa, with a focus on the ECOWAS region, using a newly constructed panel of disinflation episodes covering 54 countries over the period 1960–2024. The empirical analysis relies on panel estimations, including OLS with clustered standard errors, FGLS, and 2SLS‐IV approaches, to account for heterogeneity, panel‐specific heteroskedasticity, and potential endogeneity in key policy variables. The results provide robust evidence that monetary institutional frameworks and disinflation design play a central role in shaping disinflation costs. In particular, monetary targeting and exchange‐rate pegs are consistently associated with higher sacrifice ratios, suggesting that rigid nominal anchors amplify output losses during disinflation. By contrast, monetary union membership is associated with lower sacrifice ratios, which may reflect credibility gains, policy coordination, and more stable inflation expectations within integrated monetary frameworks. Policy‐related characteristics of disinflation, such as speed, magnitude, and duration, also significantly influence the sacrifice ratio, highlighting the importance of policy design in shaping adjustment costs. Overall, the findings highlight the central role of institutional monetary frameworks and policy design in shaping the inflation‐output trade‐off in Africa and provide new empirical evidence to inform debates on monetary policy and regional integration in Africa.
