<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

保险费率必须协调基于风险的定价、组合覆盖率和反歧视要求。我们引入一个框架，其中费率通过分类、类别内基准风险价格的聚合以及平衡转移来构建，并通过若干不同标准进行评估。这产生了一个多目标费率设计问题，其可达目标集和Pareto前沿描述了由此产生的权衡。
风险充分性通过收取保费与个体基准风险价格之间的差异来衡量。组合覆盖率通过使总索赔扣除保费收入后可接受所需的资本来评估。反歧视通过各费率类别中受保护群体构成的差异以及受保护群体所经历的保费效应来考察。我们分析这些标准之间的权衡，并证明刻画其关系的数学结果：(i) 在有限多个基准价格水平下实现完美风险充分性的推断阈值；(ii) 在更严格的推断约束下风险不充分性的下界；(iii) 类别合并时风险不充分性和推断差异的精确变化，以及用于连续基准价格类别上标量化优化的动态规划；(iv) 在平衡转移下可达的受保护群体总保费效应及其最小风险不充分性成本；(v) 将推断差异与在受保护群体保费效应离散度约束下所需的风险不充分性联系起来的尖锐下界；以及(vi) 在固定受保护群体总保费效应下组合覆盖率改善的条件及其额外风险不充分性成本。一项数值汽车保险研究说明了这些结果。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Insurance tariffs must reconcile risk-based pricing, portfolio coverage, and anti-discrimination requirements. We introduce a framework in which tariffs are constructed through classification, aggregation of benchmark risk prices within classes, and balanced transfers, and are evaluated by several distinct criteria. This yields a multi-objective tariff-design problem whose attainable objective set and Pareto frontier describe the resulting trade-offs.
  Risk adequacy is measured by the discrepancy between charged premiums and individual benchmark risk prices. Portfolio coverage is assessed through the capital required to make aggregate claims net of premium income acceptable. Anti-discrimination is examined through differences in protected-group composition across tariff classes and through premium effects experienced by protected groups. We analyze the trade-offs among these criteria and prove mathematical results characterizing their relationships: (i) the inferential threshold for perfect risk adequacy with finitely many benchmark-price levels; (ii) lower bounds on risk inadequacy under stricter inferential constraints; (iii) exact changes in risk inadequacy and inferential disparity when classes are merged, and dynamic programming for scalarized optimization over consecutive benchmark-price classes; (iv) attainable protected-group total premium effects and their minimum risk inadequacy costs under balanced transfers; (v) a sharp lower bound linking inferential disparity to the risk inadequacy required under a bound on protected-group premium-effect dispersion; and (vi) conditions for portfolio-coverage improvement and its additional risk inadequacy cost at fixed protected-group total premium effects. A numerical motor-insurance study illustrates the results.
