<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

构建可信的对照组是观察性因果推断的核心挑战。传统方法通过已观测特征或处理前结果轨迹来评估可比性，尽管因果识别依赖于处理后未观测的未处理潜在结果。我们提出一个前瞻性框架，使用预后得分（prognostic scores）来选择或加权对照单元：这些得分基于处理前信息，预测单元在未接受处理情况下将经历的结果。根据因果设计所依据的可比性条件，这些得分针对未处理结果水平或变化量。它们可以利用未处理单元观测到的处理后结果进行估计，或完全基于历史数据进行估计。为构建这些得分，我们利用表格基础模型（tabular foundation models），其广泛的预训练使其能够捕捉预测变量之间的复杂关系并有效提取其预测信息，即使在应用特定样本有限的情况下也是如此。关键在于，预测指导对照组构建，而观测结果决定处理效应估计。我们推导了估计误差的精确分解，将其分解为预后得分不平衡和预测误差差异，阐明了为何仅凭预测准确性或得分平衡均无法保证因果识别。国家支持工作（National Supported Work）应用表明，更强的处理前协变量平衡不一定产生更接近实验基准的估计，凸显了预后得分比较的实际相关性。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Constructing credible comparison groups is a central challenge in observational causal inference. Conventional approaches assess comparability through observed characteristics or pre-treatment outcome trajectories, although causal identification depends on unobserved untreated potential outcomes after treatment. We propose a forward-looking framework that selects or weights controls using prognostic scores: predictions, based on pre-treatment information, of the outcomes units would experience without treatment. These scores target either untreated outcome levels or changes, depending on the comparability condition underlying the causal design. They can be estimated using post-treatment outcomes observed for untreated units or exclusively from historical data. To construct these scores, we leverage tabular foundation models, whose extensive pretraining enables them to capture complex relationships among predictors and effectively extract their predictive information, even when application-specific samples are limited. Crucially, predictions guide comparison-group construction, whereas observed outcomes determine treatment effect estimates. We derive an exact decomposition of estimation error into prognostic-score imbalance and differences in forecast errors, clarifying why neither predictive accuracy nor score balance alone guarantees causal identification. The National Supported Work application reveals that stronger pre-treatment covariate balance does not necessarily yield estimates closer to experimental benchmarks, highlighting the practical relevance of prognostic-score comparisons.

</details>
