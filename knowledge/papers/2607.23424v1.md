# Wrong and More Confident: A Field Experiment on Language Models Taking a Graduate Economics Exam

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23424v1)

## 一句话结论

> 通过现场实验，发现添加无关信息（红鲱鱼）显著降低语言模型在研究生微观经济学考试中的正确率，且模型自信度未降低，推理能力无保护作用。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

红鲱鱼（red herring）——即添加到问题中的无关段落——会显著导致语言模型推理错误并给出错误答案。然而，模型仍会写出完整的解释，且其给出的答案与所展示的步骤保持一致。红鲱鱼破坏了推理过程，却使解释保持完整且连贯。我在研究生经济推理基准（Graduate Economic Reasoning Benchmark, GERB）上证明了这一点。该基准包含六十道研究生级别的微观经济学问题，每道题都有详细的设定、经过验证的答案以及逐步参考解答，并在有无红鲱鱼的条件下呈现，由三十八个语言模型在任务内$2\times2$设计中作答。红鲱鱼使正确答案的概率降低了12.3个百分点，约占这些模型正确回答量的四分之一，且三十八个模型中有三十七个在红鲱鱼条件下准确率更低。推理能力并不能提供保护。在默认进行推理的模型与无推理模式的模型之间，以及开放权重模型与封闭权重模型之间，该效应均未检测到显著差异，尽管开放权重模型在达到相近准确率时，每个正确答案的成本显著更低。红鲱鱼对模型自认为容易的问题造成的损害最大。更糟糕的是，红鲱鱼反而会使模型认为问题比其纯净版本更容易，而非更难，即便它回答该问题的错误率更高。纯净版本的问题本身已经很难，平均而言，模型每五个问题中正确回答的不到三个。错误的答案仍然附有完整的解释，与其自身推理保持一致，且模型保持自信，因此若不与经过验证的答案进行核对，就无法发现这一错误。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

A red herring, an irrelevant passage added to a problem, makes a language model reason incorrectly and answer incorrectly far more often. Yet the model still writes out a full explanation, and the answer it gives remains consistent with the steps it shows. The red herring corrupts the reasoning, while leaving the explanation intact and coherent. I show this on the Graduate Economic Reasoning Benchmark (GERB), sixty graduate-level microeconomics problems, each a detailed setup with a verified answer and a step-by-step reference solution, presented with and without the red herring and answered by thirty-eight language models in a within-task $2\times2$ design. The red herring lowers the probability of a correct answer by 12.3 percentage points, about a quarter of what these models answer correctly, and thirty-seven of the thirty-eight are less accurate under it. Reasoning capability confers no protection. The effect does not differ detectably between models that reason by default and models with no reasoning mode, nor between open-weight and closed-weight models, though open-weight models reach comparable accuracy at a substantially lower cost per correct answer. The damage is largest on the problems the model rates as easy. Worse, if anything the red herring leads a model to rate a problem as easier, not harder, than its clean version, even as it answers that problem wrong more often. The clean problems are already hard, and on average the models answer fewer than three of every five correctly. A wrong answer still comes with a full explanation, is coherent with its own reasoning, and the model stays confident, so the error cannot be caught without checking it against the verified answer.
