<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

我们考察企业杠杆率的持续性是否仅反映了企业自身的调整摩擦，还是也反映了经济关联企业过去的融资选择。我们将年度企业基本面数据与时变的基于文本的产品市场网络相结合，并使用去因子工具变量估计一个具有交互效应的异质性动态部分调整模型。该估计量能够处理时变网络权重以及企业特定系数中的空间依赖。网络加权的滞后杠杆率为正且具有统计显著性，其平均系数约占账面杠杆率自身滞后系数与网络滞后系数之和的45%。考虑网络依赖后，归因于企业自身融资历史的持续性降低，而隐含的企业自身调整速度提高。企业特定估计结果显示，在自身杠杆率持续性以及对若干杠杆率决定因素的反应中，存在显著的产品市场聚类现象，表明融资行为的异质性与产品市场邻近性存在系统性关联。产品市场联系的演变对网络依赖具有重要的定量影响：与使用年度网络权重相比，保持网络固定会产生大得多的网络系数和显著更大的间接效应。因此，网络依赖是杠杆率动态的一个经济上重要的组成部分，而其度量取决于经济联系的设定方式和企业行为的异质性。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

We examine whether corporate leverage persistence reflects only firms' own adjustment frictions or also the past financing choices of economically connected firms. We combine annual firm fundamentals with time-varying text-based product-market networks and estimate a heterogeneous dynamic partial-adjustment model with interactive effects using defactored instrumental variables. The estimator accommodates time- varying network weights and spatial dependence in firm-specific coefficients. Network-weighted lagged leverage is positive and statistically significant, with a mean coefficient representing approximately 45% of the sum of the own and network lag coefficients for book leverage. Accounting for network dependence reduces the persistence attributed to firms' own financing histories and increases the implied own-firm speed of adjustment. Firm-specific estimates reveal significant product-market clustering in own-leverage persistence and in responses to several leverage determinants, showing that heterogeneity in financing behaviour is systematically related to product-market proximity. The evolution of product-market connections matters quantitatively for network dependence: holding the network fixed produces a substantially larger network coefficient and considerably larger indirect effects than using annual network weights. Network dependence is therefore an economically important component of leverage dynamics, while its measurement depends on both the specification of economic connections and heterogeneity in firm behaviour.
