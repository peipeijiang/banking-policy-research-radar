# When is the deepest drawdown also the longest?

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41990v1)

## 一句话结论

> 论文研究正漂移随机游走中回撤深度与持续时间的排序关系，证明在轻尾损失下最深回撤与最长回撤渐近分离，而在重尾损失下二者以概率趋于1重合。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

最深的回撤是否也需要最长的时间才能恢复？对于具有独立同分布、均值为正且有限增量的随机游走，我们发现，在轻尾损失下，最深和最长的回撤过程最终几乎必然是不同的，而在重尾损失下，它们以趋于1的概率重合。更精确地说，当损失具有有限指数矩时，最深回撤过程的持续时间相对于\(n\)次回撤过程中最长者的比例依概率收敛到一个显式的\(ρ\in(0,1)\)，而其下降持续时间的秩为\(n^{1-ρ+o_{\mathbb{P}}(1)}\)。这些极限对任意固定数量的领先深度均成立；在高斯模型中，\(ρ=1/2\)，且与漂移和方差无关。这种分离反映了达到较大深度与维持较长回撤过程所需的不同指数代价。对于正则变化损失，一个共同的大损失同时支配深度和持续时间：持续时间比几乎必然趋于1，并且对于每个固定的\(k\)，最深的\(k\)次和最长的\(k\)次回撤过程以趋于1的概率具有相同顺序的相同索引。对于任意满足\(k_n=o(n)\)且\(k_n\to\infty\)的序列，在最深的\(k_n\)次和最长的\(k_n\)次回撤过程中，共同回撤过程的比例在轻尾情形下依概率收敛到零，在重尾情形下依概率收敛到一。这些排序规律对于确定性观测时间范围内的已完成回撤也同样成立。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Does the deepest drawdown take the longest to recover? For random walks with iid increments of positive finite mean, we find that the deepest and longest episodes are eventually distinct almost surely under light-tailed losses, but coincide with probability tending to one under heavy-tailed losses. More precisely, when losses have a finite exponential moment, the deepest episode's duration, relative to the longest among $n$ episodes, converges in probability to an explicit $ρ\in(0,1)$, while its descending duration rank is $n^{1-ρ+o_{\mathbb{P}}(1)}$. These limits hold for every fixed number of leading depths; in the Gaussian model, $ρ=1/2$, independently of drift and variance. The separation reflects the different exponential costs of reaching a large depth and sustaining a long episode. For regularly varying losses, a common large loss governs both depth and duration: the duration ratio tends to one almost surely, and for every fixed $k$, the $k$ deepest and $k$ longest episodes have the same indices in the same order with probability tending to one. For any $k_n\to\infty$ with $k_n=o(n)$, the proportion of common episodes among the $k_n$ deepest and $k_n$ longest converges in probability to zero in the light-tailed case and to one in the heavy-tailed case. These ranking laws also hold for completed drawdowns within a deterministic observation horizon.

</details>
