# Sectoral inter-dependencies drive the loss of structural balance in signed financial networks

符号图提供了一种有效的架构，用以刻画合作与冲突共存的系统。其概念源于心理学中的平衡理论，并已在多个领域得到应用。金融市场便是可以使用符号网络建模的一个典型例子，其中资产在价格变动中表现出相关性。在系统性风险期间，这种符号金融网络会表现出平衡的丧失，这一点已被持续证实。在此，我们探讨这种结构失衡如何在金融网络中的不同尺度上分布，揭示其介观层面的起源。采用结构平衡理论的框架，我们使用基于三元组模体的极化度量，研究结构失衡在不同行业尺度上的分布情况。我们利用源自标准普尔500指数的纵向数据，分析了全局极化及其行业构成的时间演化。通过将全局极化分解为行业内和行业间两个组成部分，我们表明，在系统性风险凸显的时期，结构失衡主要源于行业之间的相互作用，而非行业内部的相互作用。我们采用随机化方案，确认所观察到的失衡配置具有统计显著性，并非低阶相互作用的伪影。我们推导出一个回归方程，证明全局极化的方差可由宏观经济变量很好地解释，这表明经济危机期间全局极化水平较低，是由供应链中断和通胀不确定性的复合压力所驱动。总体而言，这些发现为理解局部行业冲突如何在金融网络中传播，并在经济危机期间促成大规模结构不稳定，提供了一个定量分析框架。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Signed graphs provide an effective architecture for portraying a system in which cooperation and conflict coexist. Emerging from the concept of balance in psychological sciences, they have found applications across several domains. Financial markets are one such example that can be modeled using signed networks, where assets exhibit correlations in price movements. During periods of systemic risk, such a signed financial network shows a loss of balance, which has been consistently demonstrated. Here, we explore how this structural imbalance is distributed across scales within the financial network, revealing its mesoscopic origin. Adopting the framework of structural balance theory, we use a measure of polarization based on triadic motifs to investigate the distribution of structural imbalance across varying sectoral scales. We analyze the temporal evolution of global polarization and its sectoral constituents using longitudinal data derived from the S&P 500 index. By decomposing global polarization into intra-sectoral and inter-sectoral constituents, we show that structural imbalance arises predominantly from interactions between sectors rather than within them during periods marked by systemic risk. We employ randomization protocols to confirm that observed imbalance configurations are statistically significant and not artifacts of lower-order interactions. We derive a regression equation demonstrating that the variance in global polarization is well explained by macroeconomic variables, indicating that low levels of global polarization during economic crises are driven by compounding pressures from supply chain disruptions and inflation uncertainty. Collectively, these findings provide a quantitative framework for understanding how localized sectoral conflicts propagate across the financial network and contribute to large-scale structural instability during periods of economic crisis.
