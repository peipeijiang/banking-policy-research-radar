<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

本文研究了当具有统计显著结果的论文被选择性发表时，p-hacking对已发表估计偏倚的影响。我们表明，快速p-hacking——即导致p值大幅变化的操作——总是加剧选择性发表带来的偏倚。另一方面，慢速p-hacking——即导致p值小幅变化的操作——在选择较弱时加剧偏倚，但在选择较强时则缓解偏倚。在一个同时包含两类p-hacking的模型中，我们证明正态性假设能够识别效应的真实分布，以及在无p-hacking的选择性发表下将获得的反事实均值。将该模型应用于行为助推与发展援助效应的元分析中，我们发现初步证据表明，缓解与加剧两种情形在实践中均可能出现。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

This paper studies the effects of p-hacking on the bias of published estimates when papers with statistically significant results are selectively published. We show that fast p-hacking---actions that lead to large changes in p-values---always exacerbates the bias from selective publication. On the other hand, slow p-hacking---actions that lead to small changes in p-values---exacerbates bias when selection is weak, but mitigates it when selection is strong. In a model featuring both types of p-hacking, we show that a normality assumption identifies the true distribution of effects as well as the counterfactual mean that would obtain under selective publication without p-hacking. Applying the model to meta-analyses on the effects of behavioral nudges and development aid, we find suggestive evidence that both mitigation and exacerbation can arise in practice.
