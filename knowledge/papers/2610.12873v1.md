# An efficient stochastic generalized method of moments estimator for nonlinear moment models

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

我们研究Chen、Lee、Liao、Seo、Shin和Song提出的非线性随机GMM算法的渐近性质，该算法用于递归估计由可能非线性和过度识别的矩条件$\E[g(X,β^*)]=0$定义的参数。每次观测对矩Jacobian、逆协方差矩阵和Gauss--Newton预条件子的估计进行更新，其计算成本与样本量无关。对于线性和非线性工具变量矩，我们推导出精确的秩二Sherman--Morrison--Woodbury更新。该算法将预条件随机逼近与迭代平均相结合。在预条件单调性条件下——该条件在线性模型中全局满足，在一般情况下在一致试点估计的局部邻域内满足——我们建立了几乎必然收敛性和收敛速率，以及具有有效GMM方差的函数型中心极限定理。当步长指数超过$2/3$时，这些结果要求矩函数及其Jacobian具有四阶矩。逆Gauss--Newton矩阵一致地估计该方差，而一个投影线性化统计量使得能够对过度识别约束进行在线检验。我们将结果推广到具有长期方差权重的$L$-相依数据，但需要更强的矩假设。模拟表明，在$10^6$个观测值下，平均估计器在精度和覆盖率上接近离线两步GMM，同时所需的计算时间仅为其一小部分，且在多达$10^7$个观测值下具有固定内存。在线检验实现了正确的检验水平 and 可比的功效。在一个使用1980年美国人口普查中$254{,}654$名女性估计第三个孩子对工作周数影响的应用中，单遍估计在不同数据排序下平均与离线GMM的差异在一个标准误的几分之一以内，且两种方法在大多数排序下得出相同的过度识别检验结论。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

We study the asymptotic properties of the nonlinear stochastic GMM algorithm proposed by Chen, Lee, Liao, Seo, Shin and Song for recursively estimating parameters defined by possibly nonlinear and overidentified moment restrictions $\E[g(X,β^*)]=0$. Each observation updates estimates of the moment Jacobian, inverse covariance matrix and Gauss--Newton preconditioner at a cost independent of sample size. For linear and nonlinear instrumental-variable moments, we derive an exact rank-two Sherman--Morrison--Woodbury update. The algorithm combines preconditioned stochastic approximation with iterate averaging. Under a preconditioned monotonicity condition, satisfied globally in linear models and locally around a consistent pilot estimate in general, we establish almost sure convergence and rates, as well as a functional central limit theorem with the efficient GMM variance. These results require four moments of the moment function and its Jacobian when the step exponent exceeds $2/3$. The inverse Gauss--Newton matrix consistently estimates this variance, and a projected linearised statistic enables online testing of overidentifying restrictions. We extend the results to $L$-dependent data with long-run variance weights under stronger moment assumptions. Simulations show that the averaged estimator approaches offline two-step GMM in accuracy and coverage at $10^6$ observations, while requiring a fraction of its computing time and fixed memory at up to $10^7$ observations. The online test achieves correct size and comparable power. In an application estimating the effect of a third child on weeks worked using $254{,}654$ women from the 1980 US Census, single-pass estimates agree with offline GMM within a fraction of a standard error on average across data orderings, and both methods yield the same overidentification-test conclusion in most orderings.
