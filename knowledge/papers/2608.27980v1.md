# The Race for Elite Destinations: Education Competition and Low Fertility in Korea

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27980v1)

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

韩国拥有全球最低的生育率，同时存在激烈的教育竞争。家庭将终身收入的9%投入教育，其中大部分用于私人补习，而这些投入的回报率几乎为零。我证明，对热门职业的竞争会产生一种配置外部性：当所有家庭增加教育支出时，入学门槛随之提高，养育子女的成本变得高昂。在一个针对该机制进行校准的量化模型中，将基于分数的选拔替换为保持容量不变的抽签制度，可使每对夫妇的完成生育率提高0.24个孩子。生育补贴政策带来的生育率提升仅为该增幅的八分之一。教育税收政策几乎没有任何效果。代际间生育率的下降主要源于偏好转变，而非竞争加剧。

</details>

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

South Korea has the world's lowest fertility and an intense education race. Families devote nine percent of lifetime income to education, mostly to private tutoring with near-zero measured returns. I show that competition for coveted careers generates an assignment externality: when all families spend more, the admission bar rises and children become costly. In a quantitative model calibrated to this mechanism, replacing score-based assignment with a capacity-preserving lottery raises completed fertility by 0.24 children per couple. Pronatal transfers deliver one eighth of that increase. Education taxes deliver almost none. Preference shifts, not a fiercer race, explain the decline across cohorts.
