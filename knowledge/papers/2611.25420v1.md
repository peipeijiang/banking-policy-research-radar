<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

当只有少量目标观测值时，应当如何将在历史数据上训练的预测器适配到新的经济环境或任务中？我们提出结构微调（structural fine-tuning），这是一个引入经济约束来指导预训练预测器适配的框架。该框架不需要完整的结构模型；相反，它利用基于经济学知识的约束，从简单且普遍存在的假设到特定的主力模型不等。在理论上，我们刻画了经济约束在何种条件下能够改进相对于原始模型和普通微调的预测表现。随后，我们通过一个信用发行人在医疗支付领域类似医疗补助（Medicaid）式扩张之后的信用额度决策模拟实验来展示该框架。基于资格、未偿医疗账单、现金流和群体层面风险边界施加约束，我们的框架相对于普通神经元门控、LoRA和全量微调，将预测风险的均方根误差降低了28%至39%，并将发行人的策略遗憾降低了3%至10%。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

How should a predictor trained on historical data be adapted to a new economic environment or task when few target observations are available? We propose structural fine-tuning, a framework that introduces economic restrictions to guide the adaptation of pretrained predictors. The framework does not require a complete structural model; instead, it utilizes constraints based on economic knowledge, ranging from simple, ubiquitous assumptions to particular workhorse models. Theoretically, we characterize conditions when economic restrictions improve prediction over the original model and ordinary fine-tuning. Then, we illustrate the framework through a simulation exercise of a credit issuer's credit-limit decision following a Medicaid-style expansion in medical payments. Imposing restrictions based on eligibility, outstanding medical bills, cash flows, and group-level risk bounds, our framework lowers the root mean squared error of predicted risk by 28 to 39 percent and the issuer's policy regret by 3 to 10 percent relative to ordinary neuron gating, LoRA, and full fine-tuning.
