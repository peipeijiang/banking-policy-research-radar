<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

我们针对面板反事实估计量开发了保形推断（conformal inference），利用预测误差在单元间的可交换性而非时间上的可交换性。现有的针对处理单元较少的面板的保形方法依赖于处理单元残差的时间性质，这些性质容易受到强时间异质性（如非平稳性）的影响。我们转而留出每个对照单元，隐藏其相关结果，并使用与处理单元相同的规则进行预测。目标日期估计量（包括双重差分和合成控制）利用对照单元的处理前和目标日期结果来构建反事实，允许全保形置信集和Jackknife+区间。全路径估计量（包括因子模型插补和矩阵补全）还利用对照单元在其他处理后日期的结果；对于这些估计量，我们使用Jackknife+。在单元间可交换性下，这两种程序均产生有限样本的、逐周期的置信集，且不需要平稳性或随机分配假设。我们刻画了覆盖性成立的条件，并说明了为什么仅靠共同的预测规则不能确保覆盖性。对于多个处理单元，分块Jackknife+覆盖其平均效应，而重标记检验对所有处理单元上的尖锐原假设是精确的。我们证明合成控制安慰剂检验是一种无效应的全保形检验，可推广至全路径估计量。我们还将我们的程序与参数自助法联系起来，并根据所允许的推断对常见估计量进行了分类。我们在三个实证应用中展示了我们的程序。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

We develop conformal inference for panel counterfactual estimators using the exchangeability of prediction errors across units rather than over time. Existing conformal methods for panels with few treated units rely on temporal properties of treated-unit residuals, which are vulnerable to strong temporal heterogeneity such as nonstationarity. We instead hold out each control unit, hide its relevant outcomes, and predict them using the same rule applied to the treated unit. Target-date estimators, including difference-in-differences and synthetic control, use controls' pre-treatment and target-date outcomes to construct the counterfactual, allowing both full conformal confidence sets and Jackknife+ intervals. Full-path estimators, including factor-model imputation and matrix completion, also use controls' outcomes at other post-treatment dates; for these, we use the Jackknife+. Both procedures yield finite-sample, period-wise confidence sets under this across-unit exchangeability, without requiring stationarity or random assignment. We characterize when the coverage holds and show why a common prediction rule alone does not ensure it. For multiple treated units, a block Jackknife+ covers their average effect, while a relabeling test is exact for sharp nulls over all treated cells. We show that the synthetic control placebo test is a full conformal test of no effect that extends to full-path estimators. We also relate our procedures to a parametric bootstrap and classify common estimators by the inference they allow. We illustrate our procedures in three empirical applications.

</details>
