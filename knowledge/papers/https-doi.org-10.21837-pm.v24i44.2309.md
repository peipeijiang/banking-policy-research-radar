# INTEGRATING FLEXIBLE WORK ARRANGEMENTS FEATURES INTO BANKING OFFICE ENVIRONMENTS: EVIDENCE FROM KUALA LUMPUR CITY CENTRE

## 一句话结论

> 该研究通过问卷调查和回归分析，考察吉隆坡商业银行办公场所中灵活工作安排特征的供给与员工期望差距及其对员工满意度的影响，发现智能工具、灵活工位、隐私区域和远程支持是满意度的显著预测因素。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

灵活工作安排（Flexible Work Arrangements, FWAs）的迅速普及已改变了工作场所规划和办公空间设计，要求组织开发能够更好地支持员工不断变化需求的工作环境。尽管银行业已日益采用混合和灵活工作模式，但探讨城市银行办公空间中FWAs特征整合的研究仍然有限。本研究调查了马来西亚吉隆坡商业银行办公空间中FWAs特征的实施情况，以识别与员工满意度相关的工作场所特征，并为未来工作场所规划策略提供参考。本研究采用定量方法，通过结构化问卷调查对在吉隆坡市中心商业银行总部工作的250名员工进行了调查。研究使用描述性统计评估员工的感知满意度和期望，随后通过差距分析识别当前工作场所供给与员工期望之间的差异。之后进行皮尔逊相关分析，以检验FWAs特征与员工满意度之间的关系，并进一步采用多元线性回归分析识别各个FWAs特征的独特预测贡献。研究结果表明，尽管FWAs特征的可用性处于中等水平，但员工报告的期望始终高于其当前的工作场所体验。在所有六项FWAs特征中均发现了显著的期望—体验差距，尤其是在智能工作场所技术、健康空间、协作区域以及隐私与专注区域方面。此外，所有FWAs特征均与员工满意度呈显著正相关，而多元回归分析显示，智能工具与技术支助、灵活工位、隐私与专注区域以及远程工作支持是员工满意度的显著正向预测因素。本研究通过提供关于马来西亚银行业中与员工满意度相关并显著预测员工满意度的FWAs特征的实证证据，为工作场所规划和办公空间设计文献作出了贡献。研究结果为办公空间设计师、设施管理人员、人力资源部门以及银行组织提供了实践指导，有助于通过更紧密地协调物理工作场所设计与FWA政策，开发更加灵活、以员工为中心并支持混合工作的办公环境。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

The rapid adoption of Flexible Work Arrangements (FWAs) has transformed workplace planning and office design, requiring organisations to develop work environments that better support evolving employee needs. Although the banking sector has increasingly embraced hybrid and flexible working models, limited research has examined the integration of FWAs features within urban banking offices. This study investigates the implementation of FWAs features in commercial banking offices in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia, to identify workplace characteristics associated with employee satisfaction and inform future workplace planning strategies. A quantitative approach was adopted using a structured questionnaire survey administered to 250 employees working in commercial bank headquarters located in Kuala Lumpur City Centre. Descriptive statistics were used to evaluate employees' perceived satisfaction and expectations, followed by gap analysis to identify discrepancies between current workplace provision and employee expectations. Pearson correlation analysis was subsequently conducted to examine the relationships between FWAs features and employee satisfaction, followed by multiple linear regression analysis to identify the unique predictive contribution of individual FWAs features. The findings indicate that although FWAs features are moderately available, employees consistently reported higher expectations than their current workplace experiences. Substantial expectation–experience gaps were identified across all six FWAs features, particularly in smart workplace technologies, wellness spaces, collaboration zones, and privacy and focus areas. Furthermore, all FWAs features demonstrated significant positive relationships with employee satisfaction, while multiple regression analysis showed that Smart Tools and Technology Support, Flexible Workstations, Privacy and Focus Areas, and Remote Work Support were significant positive predictors of employee satisfaction. This study contributes to workplace planning and office design literature by providing empirical evidence on the FWAs features associated with and significantly predicting employee satisfaction in the Malaysian banking sector. The findings offer practical guidance for office designers, facilities managers, HR departments and banking organisations in developing more flexible, employee-centred, and hybrid work supportive office environments through closer alignment between physical workplace design and FWA policies.
