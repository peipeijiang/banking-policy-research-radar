# Workplace dependence in urban economies

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07588v1)

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

远程工作已从根本上重塑了城市经济生活以及跨城市的活动空间组织。然而，灵活工作的可及性在不同行业、收入群体和性别之间分布不均，从而在健康风险、社会交融和经济机会方面造成差异。因此，了解工作场所依赖度（WPD）的集中区域至关重要，但其在城市区域中的分布情况至今仍知之甚少。本研究将某大型欧洲城市的高分辨率逐时人口数据与详细的公司记录相结合，考察地理位置、行业构成和社会经济特征如何影响实际的工作场所出勤。通过比较新冠疫情限制措施低强度期与高强度期的工作场所活动，我们识别出工作场所依赖度的决定因素。研究发现，尽管行业和企业生产率是关键驱动因素，但工作场所依赖度与收入及性别之间的关系高度依赖于距城市中心的距离。在市中心附近，女性占多数的地点和收入多元化的地点表现出最高的工作场所依赖度，这与一支残余的、受地点束缚的服务业劳动力特征相符。这些发现揭示了城市核心区存在一种空间依赖性的“服务陷阱”，将远程工作不平等从个体层面扩展至整个城市生态系统。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Remote work has fundamentally reshaped urban economic life, and the spatial organisation of activity across cities. However, access to flexible work is distributed unevenly across industries, income groups, and genders, creating disparities in health risks, social mixing, and economic opportunity. Understanding where workplace dependence (WPD) is concentrated is therefore important, yet its distribution across urban areas remains poorly understood. Here we pair fine-grained hourly population data with detailed company records in a large European city to examine how location, industry composition, and socio-economic characteristics shape physical workplace attendance. By comparing workplace activity during periods of low versus high COVID-19 restrictions, we identify the determinants of WPD. We find that while industry and firm productivity are key drivers, the relationship between WPD, income, and gender is highly contingent on distance from the city center. Near the centre, female-majority and income-diverse locations show the highest WPD, consistent with a residual, place-bound service workforce. These findings reveal a spatially contingent 'service trap' at the urban core, extending remote-work inequalities beyond individuals to the urban ecosystem as a whole.

</details>
