<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

宪法作为一国法律与政治体系的正式基础，具有重要的经济和政治效应。然而，关于为何在某些社会中宪法能对政治家形成有效约束，而在其他社会中却基本被忽视，我们仍知之甚少。在此，我们探讨国家文化是否对宪法遵从产生影响。我们利用新的宪法遵从指标，对115个国家的横截面数据进行了研究。结果发现，个人主义倾向更强的社会表现出更高的宪法遵从水平。这些结果具有稳健性，并扩展至工具变量估计。它们揭示了从文化特质到长期经济发展的一条新的传导渠道：个人主义国家文化增强了宪法自我约束的可信度。我们的分析还支持了一个更为普遍的观点，即正式制度的效果取决于其所嵌入的非正式制度环境。关于宗教方面，我们的结果与既往研究一致，这些研究将现代穆斯林世界的发展不足归因于制度质量的欠缺。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Constitutions as the formal foundation of a country's legal and political system have important economic and political effects. Yet, we still know little about why constitutions set effective constraints on politicians in some societies, while being largely disregarded in others. Here, we ask if national culture matters for constitutional compliance. We study a cross-section of 115 countries, making use of novel indicators of constitutional compliance. We find that societies with a more individualistic population exhibit higher levels of compliance. These results are robust and extend to instrumental variables estimations. They imply a novel transmission channel from cultural traits to long-term economic development: individualistic national culture increases the credibility of constitutional self-commitments. Our analysis also supports the more general idea that the effects of formal institutions depend on the informal institutional environment in which they are embedded. Regarding religion, our results are consistent with past research that attributes the lack of development in the modern Muslim world to deficient institutional quality.

</details>
