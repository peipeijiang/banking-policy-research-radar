<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

自主研究系统能够产出看似可信的稿件，但其科学价值难以验证；而自动化审稿系统则可能奖励表面上的完整性。我们考察这一差距如何演变为对研究共同体的一种自我强化威胁。在一个双种群演化博弈中，作者在面向接收的研究与探索性研究之间进行选择，审稿人则在代理式评估与审慎评估之间进行选择。更廉价的面向接收的产出会增加审稿压力，使代理式评估更具吸引力；代理式评估反过来又会强化产出审稿人兼容性工作的激励。我们推导出该反馈维持一个稳定体制的条件——该体制奖励发表成功，同时降低被接收工作的科学价值。一个解析性的吸引域边界解释了为何相同的制度规则既可以支撑这一体制，也可以支撑伴随审慎审稿的探索性研究。数值示例展示了发表数量如何掩盖科学损失、更快的调整如何强化既有实践，以及为何一旦该循环建立，部分改革可能失败。审慎审计与审稿人奖励可以逆转该反馈，而临时性支持若能使共同体跨越吸引域边界，则可产生持久恢复。该模型识别出一种警示机制，而非预测某一特定会议：审稿系统可以在保持运转且对个体具有私人激励的同时，逐渐降低对科学发现的支撑效力。其制度启示在于奖励经独立验证的贡献与实质性核查，而非仅仅加速论文产出与评分。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Autonomous research systems can produce convincing manuscripts whose scientific value is difficult to verify, while automated reviewers can reward the appearance of completeness. We examine how this gap can become a self-reinforcing threat to the research community. In a two-population evolutionary game, authors choose between acceptance-oriented and exploratory research, and reviewers choose between proxy evaluation and careful assessment. Cheaper acceptance-oriented production can increase review pressure, making proxy assessment more attractive; proxy assessment can in turn strengthen the incentive to produce reviewer-compatible work. We derive conditions under which this feedback sustains a stable regime that rewards publication success while reducing the scientific value of accepted work. An analytical basin boundary explains why the same institutional rules can support either this regime or exploratory research with careful review. Numerical illustrations show how publication counts can conceal scientific loss, how faster adjustment can reinforce existing practices, and why partial reform may fail once the cycle is established. Careful audits and reviewer rewards can reverse the feedback, while temporary support can produce lasting recovery if it moves the community across the basin boundary. The model identifies a warning mechanism rather than forecasting a particular conference: a review system can remain operational and privately rewarding while becoming less effective at supporting discovery. Its institutional implication is to reward independently validated contributions and consequential checking, rather than accelerate paper production and scoring alone.
