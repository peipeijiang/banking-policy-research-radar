<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

为解决对竞争力和碳泄漏的担忧，实施雄心勃勃气候政策的国家日益倾向于将单边碳定价与边境碳调整相结合。这些措施旨在通过对进口商品中所含（未定价的）碳排放征收费用，并（可能）对出口商品的碳成本予以返还，从而在国内受监管的能源密集型和贸易暴露型行业与其国外竞争者之间创造公平的竞争环境。与此同时，这些措施也被视为可能具有保护主义动机，并将气候政策的负担转嫁给较贫穷的发展中经济体——这些经济体对气候变化的历史责任较小，脱碳的财政和技术能力有限，且可能依赖碳密集型出口。为了开展知情的政策辩论，理解边境碳调整如何改变单边排放定价对所有国家的经济负担至关重要。然而，由于现有经济文献中对关键驱动因素的假设存在分歧，且这些因素的相对重要性从一开始就难以区分，从事前模拟分析得出的定量影响估计差异相当大。本文基于一个大规模全球贸易与碳使用可计算一般均衡模型的受控模拟，对决定边境碳调整影响的三个基本维度进行了系统性敏感性分析：(i) 政策设计选择；(ii) 供需的价格响应性；(iii) 刻画各国生产、消费和贸易模式初始异质性的投入产出数据。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

To address concerns about competitiveness and carbon leakage, countries with ambitious climate policies are increasingly looking to combine unilateral carbon pricing with border carbon adjustments. These measures aim to create a level playing field between domestically regulated energy-intensive and trade-exposed industries and their competitors abroad by imposing charges on the (unpriced) carbon embodied in imports and (potentially) rebating carbon costs on exports. At the same time, they are seen as measures that may have protectionist motivation and shift the burden of climate policy onto poorer developing economies that bear less historical responsibility for climate change, have limited financial and technological capacity for decarbonization, and may depend on carbon-intensive exports. For an informed policy debate, understanding how border carbon adjustments alter the economic burden of unilateral emissions pricing across all countries is essential. However, quantitative impact estimates derived from ex-ante simulation analyses vary considerably in the applied economic literature due to divergent assumptions on key drivers whose relative importance is difficult to distinguish from the outset.  Based on controlled simulations with a large-scale computable general equilibrium model of global trade and carbon use, this paper provides a systematic sensitivity analysis of three fundamental dimensions that determine the impacts of border carbon adjustments: (i) policy design choices; (ii) the price responsiveness of supply and demand; and (iii) the input-output data characterizing initial heterogeneities of production, consumption, and trade patterns across countries.
