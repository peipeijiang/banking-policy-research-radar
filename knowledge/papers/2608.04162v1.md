# Correlation Matrices in High Dimensions: The Elliptope as a Sample-Correlation Ensemble

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

$n\times n$相关矩阵的集合，称为椭圆体（elliptope），其体积以超指数速率$\exp\{-\tfrac14 n^2\log n\}$衰减。我们刻画了该消失体积的集中位置。均匀随机抽取的矩阵在逐项意义上接近单位矩阵，但在全局上却远离它且近乎奇异：其最大绝对相关系数的阶为$\sqrt{\log n/n}$，其Frobenius距离渐近于$\sqrt n$，其经验谱分布收敛于比率为一的Marchenko-Pastur律，其最小特征值服从精确的$\operatorname{Beta}(1,d)$分布，其中$d=n(n-1)/2$，因此其阶为$n^{-2}$。更一般地，在每一个$\operatorname{LKJ}(η)$律下，不同的非对角元素恰好是两两独立的。对于均匀律，这给出了极端相关点过程极限的Chen-Stein证明，以及有限维超阈值计数相对于具有精确有限$n$均值的Poisson律的$O(n^{-1})$全变差界。我们还识别出两个不同的尺度：$η_n\asymp n$改变极限谱，而$η_n\asymp n^2$则是保持Frobenius距离有界所必需的。最后，对于有界、中心化的独立同分布非对角设定，投影到最近的相关矩阵所产生的平方修复成本渐近地至少为其非对角部分Frobenius范数平方的一半。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

The set of $n\times n$ correlation matrices, known as the elliptope, has volume decaying at the super-exponential rate $\exp\{-\tfrac14 n^2\log n\}$. We characterize where this vanishing volume concentrates. A uniform draw is entrywise close to the identity yet globally far from it and nearly singular: its maximum absolute correlation is of order $\sqrt{\log n/n}$, its Frobenius distance is asymptotic to $\sqrt n$, its empirical spectral distribution converges to the Marchenko-Pastur law with ratio one, and its smallest eigenvalue has the exact $\operatorname{Beta}(1,d)$ distribution, where $d=n(n-1)/2$, and is therefore of order $n^{-2}$. More generally, distinct off-diagonal entries are exactly pairwise independent under every $\operatorname{LKJ}(η)$ law. For the uniform law, this yields a Chen-Stein proof of the extreme-correlation point-process limit and an $O(n^{-1})$ total-variation bound for finite-dimensional exceedance counts relative to Poisson laws with their exact finite-$n$ means. We also identify two distinct scales: $η_n\asymp n$ alters the limiting spectrum, whereas $η_n\asymp n^2$ is needed to keep the Frobenius distance bounded. Finally, for a bounded, centered i.i.d. off-diagonal specification, projection to the nearest correlation matrix incurs a squared repair cost asymptotically at least one-half of the squared Frobenius norm of its off-diagonal part.
