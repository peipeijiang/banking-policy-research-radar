# Did the energy crisis turn borrowers green? Zonal power prices and green bond choice on Oslo Børs

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39843v1)

## 一句话结论

> 利用2021-2023年北欧分区电价差异，结合ESMA与Euronext数据构建奥斯陆交易所债券样本，用发行人-季度固定效应检验本地电价暴露是否提高绿色债券选择概率，结果未发现显著效应，绿色债券增长更多与市场整体趋势相关。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

2021至2023年的能源危机导致北欧电价急剧上涨，且各竞价区（bidding zones）之间存在显著差异。我们利用这一差异来检验，暴露于较高本地电价下的发行人是否更倾向于选择绿色债券而非传统债券。我们通过将ESMA FIRDS参考数据（包括已到期债券）与Euronext绿色债券分类及发行人注册信息相结合，构建了2019年至2025年间在奥斯陆证券交易所（Oslo Børs）上市的债券数据集。随后，我们将发行人与各自的电力竞价区进行匹配，并利用发行人和季度固定效应检验本地电价与绿色债券选择之间的关系。

绿色债券在新发行中的占比从2019年的15%上升至2024年的48%，但我们未发现统计学上显著的证据表明较高的本地电价增加了绿色债券发行的可能性。这一发现在三至十二个月的价格暴露窗口下以及对于公用事业公司和其他发行人均保持一致。然而，估计结果依赖于21个识别发行人，在80%统计功效下的最小可检测效应为电价每上涨10 EUR/MWh对应3.1个百分点，这意味着不能排除更小的效应。在已有绿色债券（即已具备现有框架）的发行人中，估计效应更大但不精确。我们同样未发现碳价格与绿色债券选择之间的明确关联，尽管该分析仅涵盖八家公用事业公司。总体而言，研究结果表明，挪威绿色债券发行的增长与更广泛的市场趋势关联更为密切，而非与本地电价的差异相关。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

The energy crisis of 2021 to 2023 caused a sharp increase in Nordic electricity prices, with substantial differences across bidding zones. We use this variation to examine whether issuers exposed to higher local electricity prices were more likely to choose green bonds over conventional bonds. We construct a dataset of bonds listed on Oslo Børs between 2019 and 2025 by combining ESMA FIRDS reference data, including matured bonds, with Euronext green bond classifications and issuer registry information. We then match issuers to their electricity bidding zones and examine the relationship between local power prices and green bond choice using issuer and quarter fixed effects.
  The share of green bonds among new listings increased from 15% in 2019 to 48% in 2024, but we find no statistically significant evidence that higher local electricity prices increased the likelihood of green bond issuance. This finding is consistent across price exposure windows of three to twelve months and for both utilities and other issuers. However, the estimates rely on 21 identifying issuers, and the minimum detectable effect at 80% statistical power is 3.1 percentage points for a 10 EUR/MWh increase in electricity prices, meaning that smaller effects cannot be ruled out. The estimated effect is larger but imprecise among issuers with an earlier green bond, which implies an existing framework. We also find no clear association between carbon prices and green bond choice, although this analysis covers only eight utilities. Overall, the findings suggest that the growth of green bond issuance in Norway was more closely associated with broader market trends than with differences in local electricity prices.
