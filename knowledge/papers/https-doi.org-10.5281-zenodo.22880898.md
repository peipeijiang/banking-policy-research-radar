<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

2026年第一季度涉及美国、伊朗和以色列的局势升级，将一场地区安全危机与全球能源成本、通胀预期和金融投资组合联系在了一起。本文考察了其初始宏观经济传导效应，尤其关注土耳其和黄金市场。本研究结合了结构化叙事综述与公共机构数据的描述性分析。其主要观察窗口为2026年1月至3月；此后的预测和统计修订另行标识。布伦特原油月度现货均价在2月至3月间上涨了45.48%。国际预测随后预期增长放缓，而土耳其价格数据显示，尽管3月年度总体通胀有所下降，能源价格压力依然显著。黄金的反应则不那么直接：季度正收益与3月的显著下跌并存。货币换算为土耳其里拉投资者缓冲了损失，但季度名义收益仍未能完全匹配土耳其消费者通胀的复利效应。这些结果支持这样一种解释：实物供应中断、美元融资条件和国内政策相互交织，而非各自独立运作。本文认为，中断持续时间和金融韧性比单一的商品价格峰值更具参考价值。文章还区分了黄金可能的分散化作用与对地缘政治事件或本地购买力损失的 guaranteed 保护。本文的贡献在于对早期冲击提供了综合且可复现的描述，而非对其总经济成本的因果估计。政策含义涉及有针对性的家庭支持、可信的货币沟通、供应多元化以及企业流动性管理。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

The escalation involving the United States, Iran, and Israel in the first quarter of 2026 connected a regional security crisis to global energy costs, inflation expectations, and financial portfolios. This article examines its initial macroeconomic transmission, with particular attention to Türkiye and the gold market. The study combines a structured narrative review with descriptive analysis of public institutional data. Its principal observation window is January–March 2026; subsequent forecasts and statistical revisions are identified separately. Monthly Brent spot averages increased by 45.48 percent between February and March. International forecasts subsequently anticipated slower growth, while Turkish price data revealed substantial energy pressure despite a decline in annual headline inflation in March. Gold exhibited a less straightforward response: positive quarterly returns coexisted with a pronounced March decline. Currency conversion cushioned the loss for a Turkish lira investor, but the quarterly nominal gain did not quite match compounded Turkish consumer inflation. These results support an interpretation in which physical supply disruption, dollar funding conditions, and domestic policy interact rather than operate independently. The article argues that disruption duration and financial resilience are more informative than a single peak commodity quotation. It also distinguishes gold’s possible diversification role from guaranteed protection against either geopolitical events or local purchasing power losses. The contribution is an integrated and reproducible account of the early shock, not a causal estimate of its total economic cost. Policy implications concern targeted household support, credible monetary communication, supply diversification, and the management of corporate liquidity.
